The Chart tool turns a small table of numbers into a column, line, pie, area or scatter
chart drawn with ordinary {product} objects.  The chart remembers its data: reopen the Chart
sheet at any time to change the numbers or the chart type and the drawing updates.  You can
recolor and restyle the bars, lines and wedges, replace columns with pictures
(*pictographs*), and, when you want to take the drawing apart by hand, ungroup the chart into
plain paths and text.  Charts are drawn in grays; color them afterwards with the usual tools.

== Creating a chart

To create a chart:

. Choose the Chart tool from its slot in the Tools panel.
. Drag on the canvas to set the chart's initial size.  The Chart sheet opens on its *Data* tab
  with the top-left cell active.
. Enter data.  Click a cell or move with the arrow keys, then type in the entry field above
  the grid; press kbd:[Return] to commit.  btn:[Undo] in the sheet reverts your last entry.
  btn:[Import…] reads a tab-delimited text file, such as one exported from Numbers or Excel,
  into the grid starting at the active cell.
. To label the chart, leave the top-left cell empty, put the legend names across the first
  row and the category labels down the first column.  Labels run along the bottom of the
  chart and the legend sits at its right.  To use a number as a label, type it in quotation
  marks.
. btn:[Transpose] swaps rows and columns.  Drag the small triangles above the column dividers
  to widen or narrow columns in the grid (this only affects the sheet).
. Set *Decimal precision* (how many decimals values are rounded to) and *Thousands separator*.
. Click btn:[Apply] to draw the chart and keep the sheet open, or btn:[OK] to draw it and
  close.  Empty cells plot as 0.
. Click the *Type* tab to choose the chart type and its options (below).

To edit a chart's data:

. Select the chart.
. Double-click the Chart tool.  The Chart sheet opens with the chart's data.
. Click a cell, or drag across several.  btn:[Cut], btn:[Copy] and btn:[Paste] work on the
  selected cells; paste fills from the active cell.
. btn:[Transpose] swaps rows and columns; for a scatter chart, btn:[Switch XY] swaps the axes.
. Click btn:[Apply] or btn:[OK].

== Chart types and options

To choose a chart type:

. In the Chart sheet, click the *Type* tab.
. Click one of the six types and set its options.
. Click btn:[Apply] to preview or btn:[OK] to finish.

To set axis values and tick marks:

. On the *Type* tab, click btn:[X Axis…] or btn:[Y Axis…].  Buttons are dimmed for an axis
  without numeric values, and for pies.
. Choose how values are chosen:
+
Calculate from data:: {product} picks a range and steps that fit the data.
Manual:: You set *Minimum*, *Maximum* and *Between*, the step between labeled values.  A
negative *Between* runs the axis from high to low.
. Set tick marks: *Major* marks at each labeled value, drawn *none*, *across*, *inside* or
  *outside* the axis; *Minor* marks between them, with *Count* per interval and the same
  placement choices.
. Add a *Prefix* or *Suffix* to every value label, such as `$` or `%`.
. Click btn:[OK], then btn:[Apply] on the sheet.

== Styling chart elements

To restyle part of a chart:

. Choose the Subselect tool and click a bar, line, wedge, label or legend swatch.
. To take the whole series with it, choose menu:Edit[Select > Superselect] or press kbd:[~].
. Apply strokes, fills, colors and transformations as to any object.

Your styling is remembered per series and per element, so re-applying the data or changing
the chart type keeps it wherever the element still exists.  Perspective can be applied to a
whole chart but not to single elements (xref:perspective.adoc[Perspective]).
menu:Edit[Find and Replace > Graphics] can recolor a chart in one pass.

== Pictographs

To replace columns with a picture:

. Draw or select the artwork and choose menu:Edit[Copy].
. With the Subselect tool, select a column of the series to change.
. Choose menu:Extensions[Chart > Pictograph].
. Click btn:[Paste In] to load the copied artwork into the preview; btn:[Copy Out] copies the
  preview's artwork back to the clipboard.
. Choose how columns are filled:
+
Repeating:: Stacks copies of the artwork to the column's height, using a partial copy for the
remainder.
Off:: Stretches one copy to the column's height.
. Click btn:[OK].

To remove a pictograph, select a column of the series and choose menu:Extensions[Chart >
Remove Pictograph].

== Ungrouping a chart

Choose menu:Modify[Ungroup] on a chart to turn it into a plain group of paths and text.  You
can then edit everything freely, but the data is gone: the Chart sheet no longer opens for it,
even if you regroup it.

== Working with others

A chart is shared as its data plus your styling choices, and every collaborator's copy draws
the chart from those.  When someone changes a value, adds a row or switches the chart type,
you see the chart redraw; when you recolor a series at the same time, both changes hold.  Two
people editing the *same cell* end up with the value entered last; the other value is offered
in the review sheet after an offline session.  Importing a file replaces the whole table, so
cells someone else edited during your offline period are listed for review.  Ungrouping a
chart while a collaborator edits its data leaves them with a plain drawing and a review
entry.

== Specification

=== Data model

The choice: store the generated bars, labels and axes as real child nodes, or regenerate them
on read from the data?

*Stored children* make every element an ordinary object (styling and transforms need no
special model) but make the chart itself a merge hazard: every btn:[Apply] deletes and
re-creates dozens of nodes, so a concurrent recolor of a bar is always *edit vs delete*, two
concurrent Applies produce two sets of children, and a stale replica that re-applies with old
data resurrects a whole chart.

*Regenerate on read* keeps the document small and makes data edits and style edits
orthogonal: a cell write and a series recolor are different registers.  Its cost is that
styling must be modeled explicitly, keyed by something stable across regenerations.

We regenerate on read.  A chart is a `chart` node whose only real children are pictograph
sources; everything visible is derived by `WTModel` from `ChartProps`.  Styling is a sequence
of overrides keyed by *(series, index)*, which is stable as long as the row or column that an
element belongs to still exists.

[source,protobuf]
----
// doc/v1/chart.proto
message ChartProps {
  CommonProps common = 1;                     // transform places the chart; name, notes
  Size size = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];   // plot area, local units
  // The data table.  Columns give identity and order to cells across rows.
  repeated ChartColumn columns = 3 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_SEQUENCE];
  repeated ChartRow rows = 4 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_SEQUENCE];
  bool transposed = 5;                        // Transpose: a view flag, never rewrites cells
  bool switch_xy = 6;                         // scatter only
  uint32 decimal_precision = 7 [(buf.validate.field).uint32.lte = 10];
  bool thousands_separator = 8;
  ChartType type = 9;
  ChartOptions options = 10;                  // STRUCT
  AxisOptions x_axis = 11;                    // STRUCT
  AxisOptions y_axis = 12;                    // STRUCT
  // Styling and pictographs per series or element.  SEQUENCE so concurrent overrides of
  // different elements never collide.
  repeated ChartOverride overrides = 13 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_SEQUENCE];
}

message ChartColumn { ElementId id = 1; }     // identity + order only
message ChartRow {
  ElementId id = 1;
  // One cell per column, keyed by column id.  SEQUENCE for identity; order is irrelevant
  // (the column sequence orders them on read).  Missing cell = empty = 0.
  repeated ChartCell cells = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_SEQUENCE];
}
message ChartCell {
  ElementId id = 1;
  ElementId column = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];   // which column
  string text = 3;                            // as typed: "12.5", "Q1", "\"2024\""
}

enum ChartType {

  CHART_TYPE_GROUPED_COLUMN = 1; CHART_TYPE_STACKED_COLUMN = 2; CHART_TYPE_LINE = 3;
  CHART_TYPE_PIE = 4; CHART_TYPE_AREA = 5; CHART_TYPE_SCATTER = 6;

enum ChartMarker { CHART_MARKER_UNSPECIFIED = 0; CHART_MARKER_NONE = 1; CHART_MARKER_SQUARE = 2;

  double cluster_width = 2;                   // percent; 0 reads as 80
  double pie_separation = 3 [(buf.validate.field).double = {gte: 0, lte: 50}];
  ChartMarker markers = 4;                    // NONE, SQUARE, DIAMOND, TRIANGLE, CIRCLE
  bool data_numbers = 5;
  bool drop_shadow = 6;
  bool legends_across_top = 7;
  ChartAxisDisplay axis_display = 8;          // LEFT (default), RIGHT, BOTH
  bool gridlines_x = 9;
  bool gridlines_y = 10;

  uint32 minor_count = 7;
  string prefix = 8 [(buf.validate.field).string.max_len = 16];
  string suffix = 9 [(buf.validate.field).string.max_len = 16];
}

message ChartOverride {
  ElementId id = 1;
  ElementId series = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];   // a column id, or a row id for pies/stacked
  ElementId index = 3 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];    // a row id (or column id); unset = whole series
  AppearanceProps appearance = 4;                  // strokes/fills/effects; unset = chart default
  Transform transform = 5 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];  // extra transform
  // Pictograph: a child node of the chart (a group) used in place of the column.

  bool repeating = 7;

Series identity uses element ids of columns or rows, so a series keeps its style through
transposition (which only flips which sequence is "series") and through row/column inserts
elsewhere.

=== Merge semantics

Cell edit vs. cell edit (same cell):: `text` LWW; review lists it.  Live, the sheet shows the
remote value arrive in the grid without disturbing the cell being typed in (the entry field
holds the draft until kbd:[Return]).

Two people create the same cell:: A cell is created when a column-row slot is typed into for
the first time; two replicas may each insert a `ChartCell` for the same `(row, column)`.
*Read normalization:* when a row holds several live cells for one column, the one with the
greatest cell element id wins; the others are ignored, not deleted.

Add a column vs. add a row:: Independent inserts; the new row has no cell in the new column,
which reads as empty.  Column identity means concurrent column inserts by two people never
scramble across rows (the reason columns are first-class).

Delete a column vs. type in it:: The cell write applies; the column is deleted, so the cell is
not read.  Review: *edit vs delete* on the chart node.

Transpose vs. anything:: A single flag; never conflicts with cell edits.  Two concurrent
transposes both write `true` (or both `false`) and collapse to one, listed as same register.

Import vs. edit:: Import deletes every row and column and inserts new ones in one change.
Concurrent cell edits become edit-vs-delete entries; *Restore* re-inserts the row with the
edited cell.

Type/options vs. data:: Independent registers.

Style override vs. data change:: An override keyed by a series that was deleted dangles and
is ignored on read (kept in the log).  Two overrides for the same key from two people: both
are live elements; read rule: greatest element id wins, so the later creator's style shows
and the review sheet lists the chart as *both edited*.  An override edited by two people
(same element, same field) is ordinary register LWW.

Pictograph vs. Remove Pictograph:: `pictograph` ref set vs. cleared: LWW.  The source node,
a child of the chart, is deleted by Remove; a concurrent set that points to it dangles and
reads as no pictograph.

Ungroup vs. edit:: Ungroup creates a `group` node with materialized children (paths and text
from the derived drawing, including the overrides) and deletes the chart node.  Concurrent
data or style edits land on the deleted chart: *edit vs delete*, *Restore* brings the chart
back beside the group.

Read-time normalizations (complete list)::
* Missing cell → empty → 0 (labels: empty string).
* Duplicate cells per (row, column) → greatest id.
* Duplicate overrides per (series, index) → greatest id.
* Override with dangling series/index → ignored.
* `column_width`/`cluster_width` of 0 → 80.
* Scatter with fewer than two columns → no points drawn, axes still drawn.
* Non-numeric cell in a numeric position → 0 (quoted text is a label wherever it sits).
* `size` non-positive → chart renders nothing; selectable from Layers.

=== Offline behavior

Works offline.  Each cell commit is one change; btn:[Apply] writes only registers that
changed (type, options), so a large data session is many small changes that coalesce per
register.  Importing writes one change that can be large (up to the 10,000-op limit; larger
tables are refused with a message naming the limit).

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.  The server validates the per-field bounds and
the op limit.

=== Client

Layout engine:: `WTRender.ChartLayout` (pure function `ChartProps → [ChartElement]`) computes
axes, ticks, labels, legend, series geometry and drop shadow in local units, applying
`decimal_precision`, prefix/suffix and separators to labels, and produces `Path` and `Text`
values with stable synthetic ids `(chartNodeId, series, index, role)`.  The display list
treats these like child nodes (rendering, hit-testing, selection glyphs) but they are never in
the tree.  Grayscale ramp per series: 90% black for the first, lightening in equal steps.

Selection:: APP-006's selection can hold synthetic chart element ids; presence broadcasts them
and other clients resolve them through their own layout, which is deterministic.  Superselect
(kbd:[~]) expands a synthetic id to every element of its series.  Applying an appearance or a
transform to a synthetic selection writes a `ChartOverride` (creating one for the key if
none exists) instead of touching the element.

Chart sheet:: SwiftUI sheet with a grid (`NSTableView`-backed for keyboard navigation), entry
field with draft state, Undo (local to the sheet's draft), Import via `NSOpenPanel` parsing
tab-delimited UTF-8, Transpose, column-width triangles (view state), *Type* tab with the type
buttons and options, and the two Axis sheets.  btn:[Apply] commits pending registers; btn:[OK]
commits and closes; btn:[Cancel] discards uncommitted drafts (already-committed cells stay;
Apply is not undone by Cancel).

Pictograph:: The pasted artwork becomes a `group` child of the chart node (real nodes, hidden
from the Layers panel except under the chart's disclosure); the layout engine instantiates it
per column with repeat or stretch.  btn:[Copy Out] serializes that subtree to the pasteboard.

Ungroup:: Materializes the layout's elements as nodes under a new `group` at the chart's
transform; text elements become text blocks; pictograph instances become groups.

Undo:: One group per cell commit, per Apply, per override write, per pictograph change, per
ungroup.

NOTE: As built by DRAW-032 (render half; `WTRender/Charts`).  `ChartLayout(spec:typesetter:)` lays out a
`ChartSpec` -- the chart node, type, plot size, the normalized `ChartTable` (series and categories
by element id, `values[category][series]`; WTModel applies transposition, switch XY, dedup and the
missing-cell rule), `ChartOptions`, the two `ChartAxis` (manual `ChartAxisRange`), precision,
separator, and series and element `ChartStyle`s (appearance, extra transform about the element's
centre, pictograph, repeating) -- into `ChartElement`s with `ChartElementID(chart, series, index,
role, ordinal)`; `displayItem(transform:)` draws them as one group.  WTText depends on WTRender, so
labels are shaped by a `LabelTypesetter` (`CoreTextLabels`: one Core Text line per label, Helvetica
9 pt by default).  Choices the spec leaves open: automatic axes include 0 and step in 1, 2 or 5 ×
10ⁿ for about five intervals; scatter plots each pair of columns as a series (x from the first) and
reads markers *None* as squares; area bands stack in series order; pies are laid out side by side,
one per category, clockwise from twelve o'clock, separation moving each wedge out along its bisector;
the drop shadow is the element filled 50% gray, 3 units behind and to the right, cut at the
baseline for columns; the legend sits 12 units right of the plot (beyond right-axis labels) or
across the top; data numbers use the chart's precision and separator, axis labels the step's
decimals with the axis prefix and suffix.  WTModel's `Chart`, its commands, merge tests and ungroup
are the model half of DRAW-032 and are not delivered here.  Measured: a 100 × 20 table lays out in
0.7 ms in a release build (the 5 ms budget is enforced in release builds only).

NOTE: As built by DRAW-032 (model half; `WTModel/Charts.swift`, `ChartCommands.swift`).  `Chart`
reads the live columns and rows and, per row, the winning cell of each live column (greatest cell
id; a deleted column's cells are not read).  `Chart.table` applies transposition, then takes the
first row as the legend when it holds labels and no values (the top-left cell aside) and the first
column as the categories likewise; a label is quoted or not a number, commas are accepted in
numbers, other text and missing cells read 0; *Switch XY* swaps each pair of columns of a scatter
chart only.  `liveOverrides(for:)` keeps the greatest element id per `(series, index)` and drops a
series or index that is not in the table.  Unset option enums read as the defaults (square markers,
axis at the left, major ticks outside, minor none); a manual axis range with a zero step or
minimum not below maximum reads as automatic.  The builder draws `ChartLayout.displayItem` with the
chart's transform; pictograph sources (children of the chart) are drawn in their own space for
`ChartStyle.pictograph` and are not scene objects, and the chart repaints when they change.
Commands: `CreateChart` ("Chart"; two decimals), `SetChartCell` ("Edit Chart Data": the winning
cell's `text`, or a new cell), `InsertChartLines` ("Insert Row", "Insert 3 Columns"),
`DeleteChartLines`, `ImportChartData` ("Import Chart Data"; tab-delimited text; refused past 10,000
ops), `SetChartFields` ("Transpose", "Switch XY", "Change Chart Type", "Change Chart": one register
per changed field of the options and axis STRUCTs) and `SetChartOverride` ("Style chart element":
creates the override, replaces its stack, sets transform, pictograph and repeating).  Not yet
built: *Ungroup* for charts, the pictograph paste, and the full eight-case merge suite (duplicate
