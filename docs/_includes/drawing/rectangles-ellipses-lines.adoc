Rectangles, ellipses and lines are the shapes you draw most, so {product} gives each its own
tool and makes the first two *live* shapes: a rectangle remembers that it is a rectangle, so
you can round its corners or retype its size in the Object panel long after drawing it.  A
line is an ordinary two-point path.  The three tools share one slot in the Tools panel; press
and hold the slot to pick one.

== Drawing a rectangle, ellipse or line

To draw a rectangle, ellipse or line:

. In the Tools panel, choose the Rectangle, Ellipse or Line tool from the shape tool group
  (the single-key shortcuts are listed on xref:toolbars.adoc[Toolbars]).
. Drag on the canvas from one corner (or end) to the other and release.

To draw from the center instead of a corner:

. Choose the tool.
. Hold kbd:[Option] and drag.  The point where you pressed stays the center of the shape.

Modifier keys while dragging::
kbd:[Shift]:: Constrains the shape: a rectangle becomes a square, an ellipse a circle, and a
line snaps to the constrain angle and every 45° from it.
kbd:[Option]:: Draws from the center.
kbd:[Option+Shift]:: Draws a constrained shape from the center.
kbd:[Space]:: Repositions the shape while you are still dragging; release kbd:[Space] to go
back to sizing it.  Works for polygons and stars too.

The *constrain angle* is a setting, normally 0°, that tilts every constrained operation: at
15°, kbd:[Shift] snaps lines to 15°, 60°, 105° and so on, and rectangles and ellipses are drawn
rotated by 15°.  Set it as *Constrain angle* in menu:{product}[Settings… > Object]
(xref:preferences.adoc[Preferences]).

The new shape takes the current default stroke and fill
(xref:default-attributes.adoc[Default attributes]) and is selected when you release.

== Rectangles with rounded corners

A rectangle's corners can be rounded uniformly or one at a time.

To set the corner radius before drawing:

. Double-click the Rectangle tool in the Tools panel.
. In the Rectangle Tool sheet, type a radius or drag the slider.  0 is a square corner; the
  slider runs to 100 points, and you can type larger values.
. Click btn:[OK], then draw.  Every rectangle you draw from now on starts with this radius.

To round the corners of an existing rectangle by dragging:

. Choose the Pointer tool and click the rectangle.  A small round radius handle appears just
  inside each corner.
. Drag any handle inward along the corner's diagonal.  With *Uniform* on (the default) all four
  corners follow.  The handle sits at the centre of the rounding, so it moves in as the corners
  grow.

To round corners independently:

. Select the rectangle with the Pointer tool.
. In the Object panel, turn off *Uniform*.  Four radius fields appear.
. Type a radius for each corner, or drag a corner's handle: with *Uniform* off, a handle rounds
  its own corner only.  Corners you re-lock (by turning *Uniform* back on) share the largest value.

A radius larger than half the shorter side is shown clamped, so a very rounded rectangle
becomes a stadium or a circle rather than folding over itself.

TIP: Corner rounding is not only for rectangles.  The *Corners* live effect
(xref:live-effects.adoc[Live vector effects]) rounds, scoops or chamfers the corners of any
shape -- stars, polygons, paths you drew -- and can be added to a rectangle too, for an
inverted or chamfered corner.  The rectangle's own radius stays the quickest way to round a
rectangle; while a rectangle carries a Corners effect, the effect takes over, the *Radius* field
is dimmed with a note, the Pointer's radius handles go away, and the Subselect tool's corner
widgets adjust the effect instead.

== Editing a shape by numbers

Select a rectangle or ellipse and the Object panel shows its position and size:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Field |Meaning

|X, Y
|Position of the shape's top-left corner (before any rotation), in the document's unit,
measured from the page's zero point.

|W, H
|Width and height.

|Radius (rectangles)
|One field with *Uniform* on, four (top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left) with it
off.

|Start angle, End angle, Closed (ellipses)
|An arc of the ellipse; see <<arcs,Arcs>>.
|===

Type a value and press kbd:[Return] to apply it.  This is also how to draw to exact
dimensions: drag a rough shape, then type its size.

[[arcs]]
== Arcs

An ellipse can show just an arc of itself.  In the Object panel, *Start angle* and *End angle*
are in degrees, counterclockwise from the ellipse's 3 o'clock position (90 is the top), and the
arc runs counterclockwise from the start to the end.  With *Closed* on (the default) the arc is
closed through the centre, a wedge like a pie slice; off, only the curve is drawn, and it cannot
be filled or used as a clipping path.  When the two angles are equal the whole ellipse is drawn.
The ellipse stays a live shape: resize it and the arc follows.  To draw an arc as a free path
instead, use the Arc tool (xref:spirals-arcs.adoc[Spirals and arcs]).

[[editing-points]]
== Editing a shape's points

A rectangle or ellipse stays a live shape until you edit its points; then it becomes an ordinary
path, and you can reshape it like any path you drew.

To edit a shape's points:

. Choose the Subselect tool, or hold kbd:[Option] with the Pointer tool.  The shape's points show
  as a path's do: four corners for a rectangle, two points at each rounded corner, four curve
  points round an ellipse, an arc's points and its centre.
. Click a point (kbd:[Shift]-click or drag a marquee for more), then drag it, drag its handles,
  nudge it with the arrow keys, kbd:[Option]-click it to change its type, or press kbd:[Delete].  A click with the Pen tool on the shape's outline adds a
  point.

The first such edit converts the shape to a path and makes the edit, as one step: the Edit menu
says *Undo Edit Points*, and one undo brings the live shape back.  The status bar notes the
change ("Rectangle converted to a path").  Nothing else about the shape changes -- its fill,
stroke, effects, position, rotation and rounded corners look exactly as before -- but the Object
panel then shows a path's fields instead of the radius, size and arc fields.

The path commands work the same way on a selected shape: menu:Extensions[Distort > Add Points],
menu:Modify[Alter Path > Simplify], *Reverse Direction*, *Remove Overlap* and *Correct Direction*,
menu:Modify[Split] at selected points, and the Knife, Eraser and Freeform tools convert the shape
and apply in one step.  A command that would leave the shape unchanged -- *Correct Direction* on
a rectangle, say -- leaves it live.

To convert a shape without editing it, choose menu:Modify[Ungroup] (kbd:[Cmd+Shift+G]).  Either
way, the conversion can only be reversed with menu:Edit[Undo].

== Working with others

A collaborator can round the corners of a rectangle you are resizing; you will see the corners
change while your drag continues, and both edits stay.  If you both change the same thing --
say, the width -- the value set last wins and the other person sees theirs replaced.  If
someone converts a rectangle to a path -- by editing its points or with Ungroup -- while you are
editing its radius, your radius change has nowhere to land: the shape becomes a path with the
corners it had when they converted it, and the review sheet offers to restore the rectangle
beside the path.  If you both convert it at once, you each get a path.
After working offline, any shape you and someone else both changed appears in the review
sheet with both sets of values.

== Specification

=== Data model

`doc/v1/shape.proto`.  A shape's geometry is *derived on read*: `WTModel` exposes every
`rect` and `ellipse` node as a `Path` (four corner points with arc corners, or four curve
points) so that every consumer -- rendering, hit-testing, blends, text on a path, export --
sees a path.  Only the shape editors care about the kind.

[source,protobuf]
----
// A live rectangle.  Its local frame has its top-left corner at the origin;
// CommonProps.transform places, rotates and skews it on the pasteboard.
message RectProps {
  CommonProps common = 1;
  // Size is one register: a Shift-constrained resize sets both together and a concurrent
  // resize by someone else should replace both, not produce a shape nobody dragged.
  Size size = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  CornerRadii corners = 3;                    // STRUCT: each corner is its own decision
  AppearanceProps appearance = 4;
}

message CornerRadii {
  bool uniform = 1;                           // Object panel "Uniform"; default true
  double top_left = 2     [(buf.validate.field).double.gte = 0];
  double top_right = 3    [(buf.validate.field).double.gte = 0];
  double bottom_right = 4 [(buf.validate.field).double.gte = 0];
  double bottom_left = 5  [(buf.validate.field).double.gte = 0];
}

// A live ellipse inscribed in its local frame, or an arc of it (DRAW-061).
message EllipseProps {
  CommonProps common = 1;
  Size size = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  AppearanceProps appearance = 3;
  double start_angle = 4;                     // degrees, counterclockwise on screen from 3 o'clock
  double end_angle = 5;                       // equal to start_angle: the whole ellipse
  bool open = 6;                              // the curve only; closed is a wedge through the centre
}

// Size (width, height; ATOMIC where used) is in common.proto.
----

A line is a `PathProps` with one open contour of two corner points, so it needs nothing here.
The Rectangle Tool sheet's radius is a *tool setting*, kept in preferences, not in the document.

=== Merge semantics

Per field::
`size`: ATOMIC.  `corners.*`: each radius and `uniform` a register.  `appearance`: see the
attribute stack.  Position and rotation: `common.transform` (ATOMIC, owned by
xref:moving.adoc[Moving] and xref:transforming.adoc[Transforming]).

Resize vs. resize:: LWW on `size`; the other size is retained for the review sheet.

Arc edits:: `start_angle`, `end_angle` and `open` are three registers: concurrent edits of
different ones all apply, the same one is LWW (vector
`crdt-conformance/vectors/registers/ellipse-arc-concurrent.textproto`).

Resize vs. round corners:: Independent registers; both survive.  If the resulting radii exceed
half the shorter side, the read clamps them (below); nothing is rewritten.

Uniform toggled vs. one corner edited:: `uniform = true` reads all four corners as the value of
`top_left`.  The concurrent per-corner edit is preserved in its register and reappears if
*Uniform* is turned off again.  The Object panel writes all four radii when *Uniform* is on
(so turning it off later shows four equal values), which means a uniform edit and a per-corner
edit of `top_left` collide as *same register*.

Corners effect vs. own radius:: A live, unhidden `CornersEffect` in the rectangle's
`appearance` supersedes `corners` on read: the derived path is built with zero radii and the
effect rounds it (xref:live-effects.adoc[Live vector effects]).  The `corners` registers are
not rewritten, so concurrent edits to them still merge and read again once the effect is
removed or hidden.  Nothing is reviewed: the two are different registers.

Convert to path vs. any shape edit:: Conversion (menu:Modify[Ungroup], or a point edit or path
command on the shape, xref:decisions.adoc#D-078[D-078]) creates a `path` node from the geometry
the converter saw and deletes the shape node.  Concurrent edits to the shape node apply to the
deleted node: *edit vs delete* in the review sheet, with *Restore* bringing the shape back
alongside the path.  The point edit that converted it writes only to points its own change
created, so it cannot collide with anyone.  Two concurrent conversions leave two paths.

Read-time normalizations::
* Each radius is clamped to half of `min(width, height)`.
* Ellipse angles read modulo 360, a non-finite angle as 0; equal angles read as the whole
  ellipse, where `open` does nothing.
* `uniform = true`: all corners read as `top_left`.
* A live Corners effect present: all radii read as 0 and the effect applies (above).
* A `size` with a zero or negative dimension renders nothing and is selectable only from the
  Layers panel; the client validators prevent writing one.

=== Offline behavior

Works offline; nothing needs the server.  A resize drag coalesces to one `size` write.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

Tools:: `RectangleTool`, `EllipseTool`, `LineTool` in `WTApp`, on the APP-003 `Tool` protocol.
The drag state machine handles modifier changes mid-drag (kbd:[Shift] and kbd:[Option] can
be pressed or released at any time before mouse-up) and kbd:[Space] as a "move the anchor
point" sub-state.  The node is created on mouse-down with a zero size and its `size` written
on each drag event; a drag that ends with a size under 1 pt is undone locally (the create and
its edits coalesce to nothing in the outbox).

Geometry:: `WTGeometry.rectPath(size:radii:)` produces four corner points with quarter-circle
corners as cubic segments (kappa 0.5523), clockwise from top-left; `ellipsePath(size:)`
produces four curve points, clockwise from the top.  The constrain angle is applied by
composing a rotation into `common.transform` at creation.

Object panel:: `rect` and `ellipse` sections registered with APP-007: X/Y/W/H (X/Y edit
`transform`, W/H edit `size`), Radius with the Uniform lock, and per-corner fields.

Radius handles:: With the Pointer tool (xref:decisions.adoc#D-078[D-078]), radius handles are drawn at
each corner along the diagonal, in the shape's local space, and drag in local units; hit-tested
before the tool, except with kbd:[Option], which goes to the point under the handle.  When the rectangle carries a
live Corners effect they are not shown; the Subselect tool's corner widgets drive that effect's
`radius` instead (FX-047), and the Object panel's *Radius* field is dimmed with "Overridden by
Corners effect".

Point editing:: A shape's derived path carries synthetic point ids that selections name, so the
point gestures, the point section and the path commands reach it as a path's (D-078).  WTModel's
`ShapeConversion.asPaths` wraps any path command aimed at a live shape in `ConvertingShapes`: the
conversion and then the command against the converted state, as one change.

Undo:: One group per drag; one per Object panel field commit; Ungroup is one group labelled
"Convert to path"; a point edit that converts is one group labelled "Edit Points", a path command
one under its own label.
