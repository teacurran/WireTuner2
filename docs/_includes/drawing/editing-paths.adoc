Once a path exists you can move its points, change what kind of points they are, pull its
handles, add and remove points, cut it apart, rub parts of it out, push its segments around
with the Freeform tool, and thin it out with Simplify.  The Pointer and Subselect tools do
the fine work; the Freeform, Knife and Eraser tools do the broad strokes; the Object panel
gives every point a numeric address.

== Selecting and moving points

To select and move points:

. Choose the Pointer tool for a path that stands on its own, or the Subselect tool for a
  path inside a group.
. Click the path, then click a point.  kbd:[Shift]-click to add more points, or drag a
  marquee around several.
. Drag any selected point.  All selected points move together.

The arrow keys nudge selected points by the *Cursor distance* preference; kbd:[Shift]+arrow
nudges ten times as far (xref:moving.adoc[Moving]).  kbd:[Option]-clicking a point with the
Pointer toggles it between corner and curve; kbd:[Option]-dragging a retracted handle out of
a point extends it.

== Adjusting handles

To adjust a point's handles:

. With the Pointer or Subselect tool, select the path, then click a point.  Its handles
  appear, along with the near handles of the two neighboring points.
. Drag a handle.  Dragging away from the point deepens the curve; dragging onto the point
  straightens the segment.

How the handles respond depends on the point type (xref:vector-basics.adoc[Vector basics]):
a curve point's handles pivot together, a corner point's move independently, and a connector
point's handle only lengthens along its fixed line.  Dragging a corner point's handle across
its point curves a straight segment; dragging a curve point's handle across its point turns
the curve inside out.

== Changing points in the Object panel

Select one or more points and the Object panel adds a point section.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Control |Meaning

|Point type
|Three buttons: corner, curve, connector.  Changing the type keeps the handles where they are
if the new type allows, otherwise pulls them into line.

|Handles
|Two buttons that retract the point's incoming or outgoing handle.  To extend a retracted
handle, kbd:[Option]-drag it out of the point on the canvas.

|Automatic
|Lets {product} choose the handles so the path flows as smoothly as possible through this
point, and keeps choosing as the neighbors move.  Curve points get recomputed handles; corner
and connector points are not given handles they did not have.  Dragging a handle by hand turns
Automatic off for that point.

|X, Y
|The point's location in the document's unit from the page's zero point.  Hidden when more
than one point is selected.
|===

== Adding and deleting points and segments

To delete points or segments:

. With the Subselect tool, click the path, then click a point or segment.  kbd:[Shift]-click
  to select more.
. Press kbd:[Delete].

Deleting a point joins its neighbors with a new segment.  Deleting a segment opens the path
there; deleting a segment from a path that is already open splits it into two paths.

To add a point by hand:

. Select the path.
. Choose the Pen tool and click on a segment.  A corner point is placed at the click without
  changing the curve's shape.

To add points everywhere:

* Choose menu:Extensions[Distort > Add Points], or click *Add Points* on the Extension
  Operations toolbar.  One point is added halfway along every segment of the selected paths.

== Reshaping with the Freeform tool

The Freeform tool reshapes a path by pushing or pulling its segments directly, adding,
removing and retyping points as it goes.  It has two modes, chosen in its sheet.

To set the Push/Pull options:

. Double-click the Freeform tool.
. Choose *Push/Pull* and set:
+
Push size:: Diameter of the push pointer, 1 to 1000 pixels.
Push precision:: 1 to 10; higher values follow the pointer more closely and add more points.
Pull bend:: *By length* affects a stretch of path *Length* (1 to 1000 pixels) long around the
grab point; *Between points* affects the whole segment between its two points, and marks the
pointer with a small *s* while active.
Pressure:: With a tablet, *Size* lets pen pressure vary the push size and *Length* lets it
vary the pull length.
. Click btn:[OK].

To push or pull a selected path:

* Press on the path and drag to *pull* it: the segment follows the pointer.  A small *s*
  marks pull mode.
* Press beside the path and drag into it to *push*: the circle pointer shoves the path ahead
  of it, reshaping only what it touches.

Keys in Push/Pull mode::
kbd:[Shift]:: Constrains the pointer's movement.
kbd:[Option] (before pressing):: Temporarily switches between *By length* and *Between
points* for this pull.
kbd:[Option] (after starting the drag):: Clones the path and reshapes the copy.
kbd:[{startsb}] / kbd:[{endsb}]:: Shrinks or grows the push pointer.

To set the Reshape options:

. Double-click the Freeform tool.
. Choose *Reshape area* and set:
+
Size:: Pointer diameter, 1 to 1000 pixels.
Strength:: 1 to 100%, how hard the pointer distorts what it touches.  The inner circle of the
pointer shows it.
Precision:: 1 to 10.
Pressure:: *Size* and *Length* as above.
. Click btn:[OK].

To reshape a selected path:

. With *Reshape area* chosen, press near the path.  The pointer becomes two concentric
  circles.
. Drag across the path.  The path bends with the pointer, strongly at the center and less
  toward the outer circle, and the effect fades the further you drag -- good for tentacles,
  branches and flames.

Keys in Reshape mode::
kbd:[Shift]:: Constrains the pointer's movement.
kbd:[{startsb}] / kbd:[{endsb}]:: Shrinks or grows the pointer.
kbd:[Up] / kbd:[Down]:: Lowers or raises the strength.

== Splitting paths

To set the Knife tool options:

. Double-click the Knife tool.
. Set:
+
Cut:: *Free* follows your drag; *Straight* cuts along a line from press to release.  In
*Free* mode, hold kbd:[Option] to cut straight for part of the drag and
kbd:[Shift] to constrain it.
Width:: 0 to 72 points.  0 makes one hairline cut; larger values make two cuts that far apart,
removing the strip between them.
Close cut paths:: Closes each piece so it keeps the original's fill.  Off, the pieces are open
and show only the stroke -- unless *Show fill for new open paths* is on, in which case they
keep the fill anyway.
Tight fit:: Makes a freehand cut follow your hand exactly instead of smoothing it.
. Click btn:[OK].

To cut a selected path with the Knife:

* Drag the Knife across the path.  Every crossing becomes a cut.

To split paths at points:

. Select one or more paths and, on each, the points to split at.
. Choose menu:Modify[Split].  Each selected point becomes the end of one piece and the start
  of another.

With the *Smoother editing* general preference off, the new points that a cut creates may
not show until you reselect the path.

== Erasing

To set the Eraser options:

. Double-click the Eraser tool.
. Set *Min* and *Max* width, 0 to 72 points.
. Click btn:[OK].

To erase part of a selected path:

. Choose the Eraser tool.
. Drag across the path.  What the eraser covers is removed; what remains becomes one or more
  paths.

Keys while erasing::
kbd:[{startsb}] / kbd:[{endsb}]:: Narrows or widens the eraser.
Pen pressure:: With a tablet, sets the width between *Min* and *Max*.

== Simplifying and cleaning up

To simplify a selected path:

. Choose menu:Modify[Alter Path > Simplify].
. Set *Amount*: higher removes more points.
. Click btn:[Apply] to preview; adjust and apply as often as you like.
. Click btn:[OK] to keep the result or btn:[Cancel] to restore the original.

Simplify is most useful on traced and Pencil-drawn paths.  To find heavy paths across a
document, menu:Edit[Find and Replace > Graphics] can select paths with more than a given
number of points (xref:find-replace.adoc[Find and replace]).  Flatness, in the Object panel,
is the other lever for slow printing (xref:vector-basics.adoc[Vector basics]).

== Reversing direction

To reverse the direction of a selected path:

* Choose menu:Modify[Alter Path > Reverse Direction].

The start point becomes the end point, arrowheads swap ends, text on the path runs from the
other end, and the path's role in a blend changes.  For a composite path whose overlapping
contours fill wrongly, menu:Modify[Alter Path > Correct Direction] (also under
menu:Extensions[Cleanup]) sets every contour's direction so that overlaps of opposite-running
contours are hollow, the usual intent (xref:combining-paths.adoc[Combining paths]).

== Working with others

Point edits are the finest-grained thing {product} shares: two people can reshape different
parts of one path at once without getting in each other's way, and you see their points and
handles move live.  The tools on this page behave predictably when a collaborator is working
on the same path:

* Moving or retyping a point that someone else deletes at the same moment: the point is gone
  and the path heals; your change is noted for the review sheet if you were offline.
* A Knife cut or Split while someone else edits points: the piece that keeps the original
  path's identity keeps their edits; edits to points that moved into a new piece are listed for
  review after they reconnect.
* Freeform pushes, Eraser strokes and Simplify rewrite stretches of a path wholesale, so a
  collaborator's edit to a point inside that stretch will be listed for review rather than
  merged.
* Reversing direction never conflicts with anyone's point edits.

== Specification

=== Data model

None beyond `PathProps`.  Tool settings are preferences.

=== Merge semantics

All operations here are commands from DRAW-002 and merge as specified on
xref:vector-basics.adoc[Vector basics].  How each tool maps to registers decides what
survives a concurrent edit:

[cols="1,2,2"]
|===
|Operation |Writes |Concurrent edit to the same path

|Move points / nudge
|`anchor` per selected point
|Other points untouched; same point: LWW, review lists it.

|Drag a handle
|`in_handle` or `out_handle` (both for curve points)
|See the curve-tandem case in Vector basics.

|Change type / retract / Automatic
|`kind`, handles, `automatic`
|Independent registers; a `kind` change vs. a handle drag can leave a curve point with
unlinked handles, rendered as stored.

|Delete point
|element `deleted`
|Move of that point lost (edit vs delete); neighbors untouched.

|Delete segment (open at k)
|`closed = false`, `start`
|Point edits untouched.  On an open path: split, below.

|Add point on segment / Add Points
|element insert + two neighbor handles
|Concurrent handle drags LWW; shape approximately preserved.

|Freeform push / pull / reshape
|`anchor`, handles of the touched points; inserts and deletes of points inside the stretch
|Edits to points outside the stretch survive; inside, register-by-register LWW and
edit-vs-delete for removed points.  The tool writes once per drag event, coalesced offline.

|Knife / Split
|Retained piece: `closed`, `start`, deletes of the removed strip's points, inserts of the
cut points.  New pieces: new `path` nodes with copied points.
|Retained piece keeps concurrent edits; new pieces' points are new ids, so edits to the
originals are edit vs delete.  The piece that keeps the original node is the one containing
the original `start` (or the first stored point).

|Eraser
|As Knife, with width.
|As Knife.

|Simplify
|Replaces the contour's points (delete all, insert new)
|Every concurrent point edit is edit vs delete; the review sheet lists the path once with
*Restore* re-inserting the edited point at its old position.  Simplify's btn:[Apply] preview
is local until btn:[OK]; only the final result is written.

|Reverse Direction
|`reversed`
|Never conflicts with point edits; two reversals collapse to one.

|Correct Direction
|`reversed` per contour
|Same.
|===

Read-time normalizations: none beyond Vector basics.

=== Offline behavior

Works offline.  Freeform drags coalesce per register; Simplify and Knife produce one change
each.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

Pointer and Subselect:: Point and handle dragging lives in `WTApp.PointEditing`, used by both
tools (the difference is only what they select: the Pointer selects top-level objects, the
Subselect reaches into groups, per xref:selecting.adoc[Selecting]).  A drag writes registers on
every drag event, throttled to one change per 16 ms; the release writes the final value.

Freeform:: `FreeformTool` samples the pointer, computes the affected parameter range on the
contour (by length: arc-length window around the nearest point via GEO-001; between points: the
whole segment), and refits that stretch with GEO-004 at the chosen precision after each event.
Reshape uses a Gaussian falloff of `strength` over the pointer radius and decays with distance
dragged.  Pressure options map `NSEvent.pressure` to size or length.  The push pointer is
drawn in the overlay at the configured pixel size regardless of zoom.

Knife and Eraser:: Both build a cutting shape (a hairline or a stroked strip of the given
width, GEO-003) and intersect it with the selected paths (GEO-002), then apply the split rules
above.  *Tight fit* skips the smoothing pass on the freehand cut.

Simplify:: `SimplifySheet` with a slider and btn:[Apply]; the preview is rendered from a
temporary path in the overlay, the document is written on btn:[OK].

Object panel:: Point section registered with APP-007 (delivered by DRAW-004); this page
wires the commands.

Undo:: One group per drag, per key nudge burst (nudges within 500 ms group), per Delete, per
Split, per Simplify OK, per Reverse.
