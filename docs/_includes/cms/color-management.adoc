Color management is how {product} keeps the colors you see on screen honest about the colors
that will come out of a press, a proof printer, or someone else's display.  A monitor makes
color by adding red, green and blue light; a press makes it by laying cyan, magenta, yellow and
black ink on paper, and the press can reach far fewer colors than the screen.  Every device in
between -- a scanner, a camera, a colleague's laptop, a proofer -- has its own idea of what a
given color number means.  A color management system reads a *profile* for each device (a
mathematical description of the colors that device can produce) and translates between them, so
that the swatch you mixed looks as close as the hardware allows to what will finally be printed
or shown.

In {product} the color decisions that affect how the artwork is *interpreted* live in the
document and travel with it: the working color spaces, the rendering intent, the proof setup,
and how spot colors are previewed.  Everyone who opens the document sees the artwork through
the same settings.  Every color in the artwork also carries its own space -- sRGB, Display P3,
Lab, OKLab or CMYK (xref:spot-process.adoc[Spot and process colors]) -- so a Display P3 red
mixed on a MacBook is a Display P3 red on every other Mac, shown in full on a P3 screen and as
the nearest color a narrower screen can make, never silently rewritten.  The one thing that
belongs to your Mac, not to the document, is your display's own profile, which macOS manages
(xref:monitor-calibration.adoc[Calibrating your display]).

NOTE: Apple ColorSync is the color engine.  Color settings live in the
menu:File[Color Settings…] sheet, stored in the document so that collaborators share it.
Display calibration and color conversion are covered by
xref:monitor-calibration.adoc[Calibrating your display] and
xref:color-tables.adoc[Color conversion and spot color preview].

== A color-managed workflow

Most documents need only the first two steps.  The rest matter when you are preparing
artwork for a specific press or proofer.

. Let macOS profile your display, or calibrate it with a measuring device
  (xref:monitor-calibration.adoc[Calibrating your display]).
. Choose the document's working spaces -- the CMYK profile that defines what its CMYK values
  mean and the RGB profile it outputs in (xref:color-profiles.adoc[Choosing color profiles]).
. Choose a rendering intent that suits the artwork (<<intent,About rendering intent>>).
. Set up proofing so the screen simulates the output device, and turn on
  menu:View[Proof Colors] while you judge color (<<proof,Proofing colors on screen>>).
. For imported photographs and other bitmaps, check that each image is interpreted through the
  right source profile (xref:image-color.adoc[Managing image color]).

If your artwork is destined for the web or another screen, the working RGB space (sRGB by
default) is the output space for anything you ask to be converted, wide-gamut colors are
written as such where the format allows (xref:export-bitmap.adoc[Exporting bitmaps],
xref:publish-html.adoc[Publishing as HTML]), and proofing is normally off.  If it is destined
for print, the CMYK working profile should describe the press or the paper standard your
printer works to.

[[intent]]
== About rendering intent

When a color from one space cannot be reproduced in another -- a vivid screen blue that no
ink can match -- something has to give.  The rendering intent is the rule for what gives.  The
result depends on the artwork and on the two profiles, so try another intent if a proof looks
wrong.

Perceptual::
Squeezes the whole source gamut into the destination so that the *relationships* between
colors are kept, even though most values shift a little.  Best for photographs.

Saturation::
Keeps colors as vivid as possible at the cost of accuracy.  Best for charts, diagrams and
presentation graphics where punch matters more than fidelity.

Relative Colorimetric::
Reproduces every in-gamut color exactly after aligning the white of the source to the white
of the destination; out-of-gamut colors are clipped to the nearest reproducible color.  Best
for illustration, and the default.

Absolute Colorimetric::
Like Relative Colorimetric, but without aligning the white points, so the paper's own tint is
simulated as well.  Best for matching brand and logo colors on a proof.  Two distinct source
colors may collapse onto one destination color.

The intent in Color Settings is the document's default.  An individual image can override it
(xref:image-color.adoc[Managing image color]).

[[proof]]
== Proofing colors on screen

Proofing makes the display pretend to be another device.  With proofing set to the separations
press and menu:View[Proof Colors] turned on, an RGB photograph is shown the way it will print
after separation, and a CMYK swatch is shown through the press profile and then your display
profile, rather than as the nearest bright screen color.

NOTE: The proof *setup* is part of the document's Color Settings, so that everyone proofing
the document simulates the same device; the on/off switch is per window, so turning it on does
not change what your collaborators see.

To set up proofing:

. Choose menu:File[Color Settings…].
. Under Proof setup, choose what the screen should simulate:
+
[horizontal]
None:: No simulation.  RGB colors are shown through the working RGB profile and CMYK colors
through the working CMYK profile, both converted straight to your display.  Choose this for
artwork that will be viewed on screen.
Separations Printer:: Simulate the press described by the Working CMYK profile: each color as
it will print on its own plate.
Composite Printer:: Simulate a composite proofer (a device that prints all colors in one
pass).  Choose its profile from the Composite Printer menu.  Tick *Composite simulates
separations* if the proofer should itself imitate the separations press -- that is, show
what the press will do, printed on the proofer -- which is what most contract proofing wants.
Leave it unticked when the composite printer *is* the final output.
. Optionally tick *Simulate paper white* (show the paper's tint instead of pure screen white;
  this implies Absolute Colorimetric for the last step to the display) and *Simulate black
  ink* (show the press's deepest black rather than the display's).
. Click btn:[OK].

To turn proofing on or off, choose menu:View[Proof Colors] (kbd:[Cmd+Option+Y]).  The window's
title bar shows *Proof: <profile name>* while it is on.  Proofing is a view setting: it never
changes a color value, and it does not affect export or printing, which apply the document's
output settings directly (xref:printing.adoc[Printing], xref:export-formats.adoc[Export formats]).

== Working with others

Color settings belong to the whole document, so when a collaborator changes them you see it
at once: the canvas re-renders and a brief message names the change, for example "Priya changed
Working CMYK to ISO Coated v2".  No color value in the artwork changes; only its interpretation.

If two people change the same setting at the same moment, the later change stands and the
earlier one is offered back in the review sheet (menu:File[Review Merge…]), so nothing is
silently lost.  Because a working-space change affects how every object looks, any change to
color settings made while you were offline -- yours or theirs -- is listed in the review
sheet when you reconnect, even when nothing else overlapped.

Proofing on or off is yours alone: turning on menu:View[Proof Colors] changes your window and
nobody else's.  Custom profiles loaded from `.icc` files are part of the document and reach
everyone; a collaborator who has not received the profile data yet sees the artwork through
the nearest bundled profile with a *profile pending* badge in the status bar until it arrives.

== Specification

=== Data model

`ColorSettings` is a field of the `settings` node's props (`SettingsProps.color`, well-known
node `0:1`).  `ProfileRef` and the enums are shared with
xref:image-color.adoc[Managing image color] and xref:color-profiles.adoc[Choosing color
profiles]; they are defined here.

[source,protobuf]
----
// proto/wiretuner/doc/v1/color_management.proto (sketch)

// Identifies one ICC profile.  Always used as an ATOMIC register: name, hash, bundled id and
// space describe the same bytes and are meaningless apart.
message ProfileRef {
  // The profile's description tag, shown in menus and the review sheet.  Informational.
  string name = 1 [(buf.validate.field).string.max_len = 256];
  // SHA-256 of the ICC data.  Set for every profile, bundled or not, so two clients can tell
  // whether they hold the same bytes and the review sheet can tell "same name, different
  // profile".  For a custom profile this is also its blob hash (BlobService).
  bytes sha256 = 2 [(buf.validate.field).bytes.len = 32];
  // Non-empty for a profile shipped inside the app ("srgb", "display-p3", "default-cmyk", ...).
  // Readers use the bundled data and never fetch a blob.  Empty means: fetch the blob.
  string bundled_id = 3 [(buf.validate.field).string.max_len = 64];
  // Color space class of the profile, so a reader can choose a fallback of the right class
  // without having the ICC data.
  ProfileSpace space = 4;
}

enum ProfileSpace {
  PROFILE_SPACE_UNSPECIFIED = 0;
  PROFILE_SPACE_RGB = 1;
  PROFILE_SPACE_CMYK = 2;
  PROFILE_SPACE_GRAY = 3;
  PROFILE_SPACE_LAB = 4;
}

enum RenderingIntent {
  RENDERING_INTENT_UNSPECIFIED = 0;   // "use the document default" where a default exists
  RENDERING_INTENT_PERCEPTUAL = 1;
  RENDERING_INTENT_RELATIVE_COLORIMETRIC = 2;
  RENDERING_INTENT_SATURATION = 3;
  RENDERING_INTENT_ABSOLUTE_COLORIMETRIC = 4;
}

enum ProofTarget {
  PROOF_TARGET_UNSPECIFIED = 0;       // reads as NONE
  PROOF_TARGET_NONE = 1;
  PROOF_TARGET_SEPARATIONS = 2;       // simulate the Working CMYK press
  PROOF_TARGET_COMPOSITE = 3;         // simulate ProofSettings.composite_profile
}

// STRUCT (default for a message field): each setting is an independent choice and two people
// changing different ones concurrently should both win.
message ColorSettings {
  // Working RGB: the RGB *output* space (Convert to RGB, RGB exports, untagged RGB images).
  // Artwork colors carry their own space in `Color.space` and are never reinterpreted by
  // this profile.  Unset reads as bundled "srgb".
  ProfileRef rgb_profile = 1 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  // Working CMYK; the separations press.  Unset reads as bundled "default-cmyk".
  ProfileRef cmyk_profile = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  // Document default intent.  UNSPECIFIED reads as RELATIVE_COLORIMETRIC.
  RenderingIntent intent = 3;
  // Written as its negation so that the unset default is "on".
  bool no_black_point_compensation = 4;
  // Written as its negation so that the unset default is "on" (the *Color manage spot colors* setting).
  bool no_spot_color_management = 5;
  // Profile assumed for RGB images with neither embedded nor assigned profile.
  // Unset reads as rgb_profile.
  ProfileRef default_image_rgb_profile = 6 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  // Proof setup (View > Proof Colors simulates this).  STRUCT.
  ProofSettings proof = 7;
}

message ProofSettings {
  ProofTarget target = 1;
  // Used when target == COMPOSITE.  Unset reads as cmyk_profile.
  ProfileRef composite_profile = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  // The composite proofer imitates the separations press (Working CMYK) rather than being
  // the final device.  Also read by the PRINT epic when printing to a composite device.
  bool composite_simulates_separations = 3;
  bool simulate_paper_white = 4;      // last step to display uses absolute colorimetric
  bool simulate_black_ink = 5;
}
----

The *Proof Colors* on/off state is per window and lives in the local `view` table
(xref:offline.adoc[Offline]) as a `local_only` field on the window's view state, owned by the
document-view page.  It is never synced.

=== Merge semantics

Per field::
* Every `ProfileRef` field is ATOMIC: two concurrent choices of Working CMYK resolve by OpId
  order; the loser is retained in the log and offered back.
* `intent`, the two negated booleans and `default_image_rgb_profile` are independent
  registers: one person changing the intent while another changes the CMYK profile keeps
  both.
* `ProofSettings` is STRUCT for the same reason; `target` and `composite_profile` are
  separate registers, so "switch to composite" and "pick the composite profile" from two
  people compose into a sensible state.

Nasty cases::
* *Two working-space changes at once* (A picks SWOP, B picks FOGRA): LWW on the register.
  Both replicas converge; the loser's canvas visibly changes after the merge.  This is the case
  the always-list rule below exists for.
* *Profile chosen, blob not yet uploaded*: A loads a custom `.icc` and switches to it while
  offline.  The `SetFields` carries the hash; the blob follows through the blob queue.  Until
  it arrives, other replicas read the field normally but *render* through the fallback
  (below) and show the *profile pending* badge.  No replica ever writes a "fix-up" change.
* *Undo across a remote change*: undoing a color-settings change restores only the registers
  that still hold this user's value (xref:crdt-model.adoc[CRDT model]); a collaborator's later
  change to the CMYK profile survives an undo of the local RGB profile change.

Review sheet::
A change to any register under `SettingsProps.color` is *always listed* in the review sheet
after reconnecting, whether it was made locally or remotely and whether or not it overlapped
with anything, under the heading "Color settings changed by <user>" with the old and new
profile names (and a "same name, different data" note when only the hash differs).  This is
proposed as an addition to the decision rules in xref:reconcile.adoc[Reconciling offline
edits]: a working-space change alters the appearance of every object, which is exactly the
kind of surprise the review exists to prevent, yet it touches only one node and would
otherwise merge silently.  Live (online) changes are not reviewed; they are announced with a
toast naming the user and the setting.

Read-time normalizations::
* An unset `ProfileRef` reads as the default named in the sketch.
* A `ProfileRef` with an empty `bundled_id` whose blob is not available locally renders through
  the bundled default of the same `space` (`srgb` for RGB, `default-cmyk` for CMYK, `generic-gray`
  for GRAY); the model exposes a `pending` flag that the status bar and the Color Settings sheet
  display.  When the blob arrives the renderer is invalidated; nothing is written.
* A `ProfileRef` whose `space` does not match the field (a CMYK profile in `rgb_profile`, which
  a buggy client could write) reads as unset.
* `composite_profile` unset with `target == COMPOSITE` reads as the Working CMYK profile.

=== Offline behavior

Everything on this page works offline.  A custom profile chosen offline is queued in
`blobs_pending` like any other blob and uploaded after the change log; other clients tolerate
its absence as above.  Profile blobs are small (4 KiB to 3 MiB) and are uploaded before images
in the queue.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes and storing profile blobs through `BlobService`.
The server never converts a color.

=== Client

Package:: The color-management kernel lives in `WTRender` as `WTColor` (a module directory,
not a separate package): profile registry, conversion cache, proof transforms.  `WTModel`
exposes `ColorSettings` with defaults resolved; `WTApp` owns the sheet and the View menu item.

Profiles as Core Graphics objects:: Every `ProfileRef` resolves to a `CGColorSpace` built with
`CGColorSpace(iccData:)` from bundled data or the blob cache, cached by `sha256`.  Bundled
profiles are loaded once per process.  The display's profile comes from
`NSScreen.colorSpace` for the window's screen and is refreshed on
`NSWindow.didChangeScreenProfileNotification` and when the window moves between displays.

Rendering in tagged spaces:: The display list carries every vector color as a `CGColor` in
the space it is stored in (COLOR-024 builds them): sRGB colors in `CGColorSpace.sRGB`,
Display P3 colors in `.displayP3`, CIELAB colors in `.genericLab` (D50), OKLab colors
converted by matrix to linear sRGB and carried in `.extendedLinearSRGB` (whose range admits
components outside 0..1, so a P3-only OKLab color is not clipped before the display sees it),
CMYK colors in Working CMYK, and spot colors as their preview color (see
xref:color-tables.adoc[Color conversion and spot color preview]).  Working RGB appears in
rendering only for untagged RGB images.  Tile contexts and window backing stores are tagged
Display P3 at 8 bits per channel (16 for exports above 8-bit), so wide-gamut colors survive
to the window server, which matches each tile to the display's profile: a P3 display shows
them, an sRGB display clips them per channel (ColorSync relative colorimetric), and the
document is untouched either way -- this is exactly the "None" proof state.  Because the same
display list prints and exports, screen and output agree (REND-001's pixel-identity
requirement).

Soft proof:: When *Proof Colors* is on, the tile renderer builds a proof transform from
`ColorSettings`: a `ColorSyncTransform` whose chain is source space (the color's own
`CGColorSpace` above, or the image's source profile) → proof profile (to-device with the
document intent, black point compensation as set) → proof profile (device-to-PCS) → display
profile (relative colorimetric, or absolute when `simulate_paper_white`).  A P3 color proofed
to the separations press therefore shows what the press makes of it, which is the same P3 →
Working CMYK conversion *Convert to CMYK* and separations use (CMS-003).  Vector and
gradient-stop colors are converted once per distinct color at display-list build and cached
by `(sha256 of color bytes, proof key)` -- the space tag is part of the bytes, so an sRGB and
a P3 color with equal components never share a cache entry; images and rasterized effects are
converted per tile through a `CGColorConversionInfo` built from the same chain.  Gradients are proofed by converting stops, not interpolated pixels -- an accepted
approximation.  `simulate_black_ink` off means the display's black is substituted for the
proof profile's darkest value in the last step (black point scaling).

Invalidation:: Any change to `SettingsProps.color` or to the display profile invalidates every
tile and the cached converted colors; the invalidation from a remote change goes through
REND-004 as a whole-document dirty rect.

NOTE: As built by CMS-002, CMS-003, CMS-006 and CMS-007 (`WTRender/WTColor/Profiles.swift`,
`Converter.swift`, `ColorManagement.swift`; xref:decisions.adoc#D-068[D-068]).  *Registry*: `WTColor.ProfileRegistry`
takes the bundled profiles (`srgb`, `display-p3`, `generic-gray`, `default-cmyk` = Generic CMYK)
from macOS's own ColorSync profiles at run time rather than shipping ICC files, so their hashes are
those of the installed macOS; no press characterization is bundled yet (Q-008 stays open for the
licence check).  An embedded profile is recognized as bundled by the SHA-256 of its bytes with the
ICC header's flags, rendering intent and profile id zeroed, so a copy saved with another header
intent still reads as `srgb`.  Custom profiles come from `register(iccData:)` or from
`blobLoader` (CMS-008 supplies it); `resolve` gives the bundled fallback with `pending`.  Installed
profiles are enumerated with `ColorSyncIterateInstalledProfiles`, keeping the `mntr`, `prtr`, `scnr`
and `spac` classes (device links, abstract and named-color profiles are dropped); 13 profiles take
about 2 ms here, the 500-profile budget is not measured.  *Converter*: `WTColor.Converter` builds
one `ColorSyncTransform` per chain of `(profile hash, intent, black point compensation)` steps and
caches it; colors enter a profile with their step's intent and leave it with the next step's.
CIELAB and OKLab enter through the Generic Lab profile (OKLab converted to Lab D50 by formula),
CMYK through the Working CMYK passed in.  Converted colors are cached per `(Color, chain)`, the
space tag included.  `canonical(_:step:)` rounds half away from zero (1/255, 1/10000).  The
100,000-color conversion takes 0.21 s in a debug build; the 50 ms budget is enforced in release
builds only.  The cross-version corpus (two macOS versions within 1/255) is not committed: this
Mac has one macOS.  *Working space*: `ColorManagement` is renderer state on both renderers, the
Metal canvas (`setColorManagement`) and the tile cache's renderer.  Tiles, bitmaps and the Metal
layer are tagged with its `workingSpace`, `.sRGB` by default (so every existing golden is
unchanged) and `.displayP3` when the app's canvas sets it, as this page asks.  Both renderers ask
`workingComponents` for every solid color -- a color already in the working space as stored;
sRGB, P3, Lab and OKLab by the exact formulas, clipped per channel; CMYK through Working CMYK with
the document intent and black point compensation -- so Metal and Core Graphics put identical
numbers into the tile, and the window server matches the tagged tile to the display (changing the
display profile needs no re-render; `displayColorSpaceChanged` refreshes the display query).  PDF
output carries each color tagged in its own space (`taggedCGColor`: CMYK in the Working CMYK
space, OKLab in Extended Linear sRGB) and never proofs.  Gradients, patterns, Custom, Textured and
Lens fills and raster effects still evaluate in sRGB: their colors are mapped through
`sampledColor` (CMYK through Working CMYK, proofed under a proof) and wide-gamut stops are clipped
to sRGB -- a recorded limitation.  *Soft proof*: `ColorManagement.proof` (`WTColor.ProofSetup`)
converts every solid color and every sampled paint's colors through the chain once per distinct
color, and each decoded image level through the same chain (`ImageProofCache`); the chain ends in
the working space, not the display profile, and the window server makes the last match to the
display.  *Simulate paper white* leaves the proof device with absolute colorimetric; *Simulate
black ink* off turns on black point compensation for the last step.  A mask's gradient is
transparency and is never converted.  The per-window flag, the menu item and the title-bar
indicator are WTApp's.  The frame-time budget under proof is not measured.

AppKit:: The Color Settings sheet is an `NSPanel` sheet with `NSPopUpButton`s for profiles
(grouped: Bundled, Installed, Document, Other…), an `NSPopUpButton` for intent, checkboxes for
the rest, and a Proof setup group.  The View menu item is validated from the window's view
state.  The toast for remote changes uses the shared notification strip from the COLLAB epic.

Undo:: One change per btn:[OK] labelled "Change Color Settings"; the sheet edits a draft and
writes only the registers that differ from the current values.

NOTE: Deviation recorded with PRINT-007.  Black point compensation is WTColor's own, not
ColorSync's: ColorSync's compensation moved the white point of some pairs (sRGB white into Generic
CMYK came out as about C 11% M 8% under relative colorimetric, as `CGColor.converted` with its black
point option also gives), which put ink under white paper on every separation.  A chain is split
through Generic Lab at each relative colorimetric step that asks for compensation; the two profiles'
black points (Lab black taken into the device and back) are matched linearly in XYZ with the D50
white fixed, and runs between splits stay single ColorSync transforms (no split where the black
points are equal, as when a proof device is Working CMYK).  Other intents ignore the flag.  White
now separates to no ink, sRGB black to the profile's rich black, and without compensation colours
agree with Core Graphics' conversion within 1/255 (`BlackPointCompensationTests`).

NOTE: Fixed in use (2026-10-01): a CMYK colour stored inline -- an unnamed fill, stroke, gradient
stop or text colour, and a swatch reference read from its cache outside a scene build -- reached
the renderers as the naive complement `R = (1 − C)(1 − K)` (`Appearances.color`, written before this
epic), while the same colour as a swatch and in every export went through Working CMYK: C 80 M 5
