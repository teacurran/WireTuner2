A comment is a note pinned to the drawing: "make this red", "is this the approved logo?",
"@Priya can you check the kerning here?".  Comments live in the document, next to the artwork
they are about, so a conversation about the star badge happens *on* the star badge rather than
in an email that has to describe where the star badge is.  Anyone who can open the document
can read them; commenters, editors and owners can write them; and everyone who is mentioned
finds out.  Comments are part of the document and sync like everything else, but they are
never part of the artwork: they do not print, and they are left out of exports unless you ask
for them.

== Pins and threads

Every comment thread has a *pin* on the canvas: a small numbered marker in its author's color.
A pin is attached to one of two things:

* *An object.*  When you comment on an object, the pin sticks to it.  Move, rotate or scale the
  object and the pin goes with it; move it to another page and the pin goes there too.  This is
  the usual case.
* *A point.*  When you click on empty pasteboard or an empty part of a page, the pin stays at
  that spot.  Use this for "the whole page feels crowded" and "add something here".

A thread is the opening comment plus any replies, in order.  Each comment shows its author,
when it was written, its text and any reactions.  A thread is either *open* or *resolved*
(<<resolving,Resolving a thread>>).

Pins are drawn on top of the artwork at a constant size, whatever the zoom, and never hide
what they point at: hover a pin to see the first line of its thread, click it to open the
thread.  Resolved threads' pins are hidden unless you turn them on
(<<showing-hiding,Showing and hiding pins>>).

== Adding a comment

To comment on an object or a spot:

. Choose the Comment tool in the Tools panel, or press kbd:[C].
. Click the object you want to talk about, or click an empty spot.  A pin appears there and a
  composer opens beside it.
. Type your comment.  To mention someone, type kbd:[@] and start typing their name, then pick
  them from the list (<<mentions,Mentioning people>>).
. Press kbd:[Cmd+Return] or click btn:[Comment].  Press kbd:[Esc] instead to discard it; the
  pin goes away with it.

The composer accepts plain text with line breaks; long comments are fine.  You can also comment
on the current selection without the tool: choose menu:Object[Add Comment…] with something
selected, and the pin attaches to the selected object.

To reply to a thread:

. Click its pin, or select it in the Comments panel.
. Type in the reply field at the bottom of the thread and press kbd:[Cmd+Return].

To move a pin, drag it with the Comment tool or the Pointer tool.  Drop it on another object to
re-attach it, or on empty space to make it a point pin.  You can move the pins of your own
threads; editors can move any pin.

== Editing and deleting your comments

Hover a comment of yours and use its menu (the small chevron at its right):

Edit:: Reopens the text for editing.  An edited comment shows _edited_ after its time.  The
change is visible to everyone at once.
Delete:: Removes the comment.  A deleted reply disappears from the thread; deleting the
*opening* comment of a thread with replies leaves the thread in place with "Comment deleted" at
the top, so the replies keep their context.  Deleting the opening comment of a thread with no
replies deletes the thread and its pin.

You can edit and delete only your own comments.  A document owner can delete anyone's comment
or an entire thread.  Deleted comments are not restorable from the History panel; think of a
delete as final.

[[mentions]]
== Mentioning people

Type kbd:[@] in a comment and start typing a name.  The list offers everyone who has access to
the document; pick one and their name appears as a tag in the comment.  In a team document the
list also offers the team itself ("@Marketing"), which mentions every member of the team who
has access.  Someone without access to the document cannot be mentioned -- invite them first
(xref:sharing.adoc[Sharing]).

Mentioned people are told (<<notifications,Being told about comments>>).  Mentioning someone
does not change their role.

== Reactions

Hover a comment and click the smiley to add a reaction from a small set: 👍, ❤️, 👀, ✅, ❓ and
🎉.  Reactions show under the comment with a count; click one you have added to take it back.
Anyone who can comment can react.

[[resolving]]
== Resolving a thread

When a thread has been dealt with, resolve it:

. Open the thread and click btn:[Resolve] at its top, or choose *Resolve* from the thread's
  row in the Comments panel.
. The pin disappears from the canvas (unless resolved pins are shown) and the thread moves to
  the *Resolved* list in the panel.  Nothing is deleted.

To reopen a resolved thread, open it from the Resolved list and click btn:[Reopen]; the pin
comes back.  You can resolve and reopen threads you started; editors and owners can resolve and
reopen any thread.  Replying to a resolved thread does not reopen it.

Open it with menu:Window[Comments] or click the Comments button in the toolbar, which shows the
number of unread comments.  The panel lists every thread in the document, newest activity
first.  Each row shows the pin number, the first line of the opening comment, the author, the
number of replies, the time of the latest reply, and a blue dot when something in the thread is
unread.

[[notifications]]
== Being told about comments

{product} keeps track, per document, of which comments you have seen.  A comment counts as
read once its thread has been open on your screen.

* The Comments button in the toolbar shows the number of unread comments in the document.
* Threads with unread comments show a blue dot in the panel, and their pins carry a small blue
  badge on the canvas.
* In the library window, a document with an unread comment that mentions you shows a dot
  beside its name.
* When you are mentioned while you have the document open, a brief notice appears at the top
  of the canvas: "Priya mentioned you" with btn:[Show].
* When you are mentioned while you do *not* have the document open, {product} emails you.
  Mentions in one document within a short stretch of time arrive as one email.  Turn this off
  with *Email me when I'm mentioned* in Preferences.

Replies to a thread you started, and a thread of yours being resolved, are counted as unread
but never emailed.

== Comments and exports

Comments never print and never appear in SVG, HTML, EPS or bitmap exports.  When you export a
PDF you can include them: turn on *Comments as annotations* in the *Interactive* section of the
export sheet (xref:export-pdf.adoc[Exporting PDF]).  Each open thread becomes a PDF comment
annotation on the object it is pinned to, with its replies, so a reviewer without {product} can
read them in Preview or Acrobat.  The option is off by default and is ignored for PDF/X.

Objects copied to another document take no comments with them.

== Comments on branches

A branch (xref:branches.adoc[Branches]) starts with a copy of the parent's comments, and you
can comment on a branch exactly as in the parent.  When the branch is merged, its threads come
into the parent as threads: a new thread made on the branch appears in the parent pinned to
the same object, and a reply made on the branch appears in the parent's copy of that thread.
Comments are never listed as conflicts in the Review Changes sheet.  If a thread was resolved
on one side and reopened on the other, the later of the two stands.

== Working with others

Comments are the most conversational part of a document, and they behave like a conversation:
a reply someone writes appears in the open thread within a moment, with a brief highlight; a
new thread's pin appears on your canvas as it is made; a thread resolved by someone else slides
out of the Open list as you watch.  Two people replying to the same thread at the same time
both land, one after the other -- nothing is lost and nobody is asked to choose.  Two people
resolving and reopening a thread in the same moment get the later of the two.

Comments work offline.  You can read every thread your Mac already has, start threads, reply,
resolve and react while offline; the pins show at once, and everything is sent when you
reconnect.  People you mention are told when your comment reaches the server, not before.
Reactions and read marks made offline catch up in the same way.

When someone deletes an object that has a comment thread on it, the thread stays.  Its pin
disappears with the object, and in the panel the thread shows the object's name with "(deleted)"
after it; if the object is restored, the pin comes back.  In the Review Changes sheet after an
offline session, an object you commented on that someone else deleted appears under _Edited
and deleted_ with the same btn:[Restore] as any other edit, so a comment on a deleted object
is never a dead end.

Presence shows comments too: while a collaborator is typing in a thread, the hover card on
their avatar reads _Commenting on Logo mark_ (xref:presence.adoc[Presence]).

ifdef::spec[]
== Specification

Comments are document data under a new well-known collection, `comments (0:12)`, and are the
first feature for which the server reads the *content* of changes rather than relaying them
blind: mention notifications, unread counters and the commenter role check all inspect ops
under `0:12`.  Everything else about comments -- pins, panel, tool, merge -- is the ordinary
node machinery of xref:crdt-model.adoc[CRDT model].  Comment text is a `RichText`
(MERGE_TEXT) so that concurrent edits of one comment merge by character and mentions are
Peritext marks that survive editing around them.

=== Data model

`comments (0:12)` is a well-known collection node like `swatches` or `symbols`
(xref:crdt-model.adoc[CRDT model]); its children are `comment_thread` nodes, ordered by
creation (position among siblings is informational; the panel sorts by activity).  Counters
`13`--`15` remain reserved.

[source,protobuf]
----
// proto/wiretuner/doc/v1/comments.proto (sketch, COLLAB-025)

// NodeProps gains:  CommentThreadProps comment_thread = 210;   (COLLAB block 210-219)

message CommentThreadProps {
  CommonProps common = 1;   // every kind carries it first
  // The object the pin is attached to.  Unset for a point pin.  When the target is deleted
  // or unknown the reference reads as unset and the pin falls back to `point`; the panel
  // still names the object (a deleted node keeps its CommonProps.name).  Never rewritten by
  // edits to the object: the pin follows the object because it references it, so a thousand
  // moves of the object write nothing here.
  NodeRef anchor = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).on_dangling = REF_FALLBACK_UNSET];
  // Pin position.  With `anchor` set: in the anchored node's local coordinate space (before
  // its transform), so the pin rides along with move, rotate and scale.  Without `anchor`,
  // or when it dangles: pasteboard coordinates (points).  ATOMIC: x and y are one position.
  Point point = 3 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  // Pasteboard position recorded when the thread was created or the pin last dropped, used
  // as the fallback when `anchor` dangles and the anchored node's transform is gone (it was
  // compacted).  ATOMIC for the same reason.
  Point fallback_point = 4 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  // The page the pin was on when created or last dropped; the panel's "by page" filter for
  // point pins and dangling anchors.  Anchored pins are grouped by the anchor's current page.
  NodeRef page = 5 [(wt.crdt.field).on_dangling = REF_FALLBACK_UNSET];
  // Open / resolved.  A register: concurrent resolve and reopen resolve by OpId.
  bool resolved = 6;
  // The opening comment is the first element; replies follow.  SEQUENCE: each comment is an
  // element with its own registers; concurrent replies are both kept.
  repeated Comment comments = 7 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_SEQUENCE];
}

message Comment {
  ElementId id = 1;
  // Server-verified on ingest: must equal the account bound to the change's replica.
  string author_account_id = 2;
  RichText body = 3 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_TEXT];
  int64 wall_time_ms = 4;                       // informational; display only
  // Edited-since-posted marker for the "edited" label: the wall time of the last edit.
  int64 edited_wall_time_ms = 5;
  // Account ids mentioned, or "team:<team id>" for a whole team.  SET: a concurrent edit
  // that adds a mention and one that removes another both apply.  The server notifies from
  // this field, not from the text.
  repeated string mentions = 6 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_SET,
                                (buf.validate.field).repeated.max_items = 50];
  // "<account id>:<emoji>" members; add-wins.  Emoji restricted to the six in the guide.
  repeated string reactions = 7 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_SET,
                                 (buf.validate.field).repeated.max_items = 500];
  // Soft delete.  The element's own `deleted` register is not used for comments so that a
  // deleted opening comment keeps its place at the head of the thread.
  bool deleted = 8;
}
----

A mention inside the text is a Peritext mark `mention(account_id)` over the name's characters
(xref:creating-text.adoc[Creating text] for `TextMarkValue`), which is how the tag renders and
follows edits; the `mentions` set is the authoritative list the server acts on and is written
by the same command.

`comments.proto` also adds the `docs.v1` service the client uses for read state and

  // The caller has seen a thread through a given comment.  Idempotent; monotone per thread.
  rpc MarkRead(MarkReadRequest) returns (MarkReadResponse);

  rpc ListMentionedDocuments(ListMentionedDocumentsRequest) returns (ListMentionedDocumentsResponse);

}

  wiretuner.doc.v1.ElementId comment = 2;

=== Merge semantics

Fields and policies as annotated above; the cases:

* *Concurrent replies* to one thread are two `ElementInsert`s after the same last element.
  Both are kept; their order is by fractional position then element id, the same on every
  replica, so everyone sees the same thread even if it is not wall-clock order.  The panel
  never re-sorts replies by time.
* *Concurrent resolve and reopen* is LWW on `resolved`: the greater OpId stands.  The loser is
  retained in the log like any losing register write but is *not* offered back by the review
  sheet (below).
* *Edit vs. delete of one comment*: `deleted` and the body's characters are independent
  registers.  A deleted comment with concurrent text edits stays deleted with the edited text
  inside it; since comments are never restored through the UI the edit is invisible, which is
  the intended outcome.  Deleting the opening comment of a thread with no replies sets the
  thread node's `deleted` (`SetDeleted`) as well; a reply inserted concurrently is kept on a
  deleted thread and *normalizes* the thread back to visible on read (below).
* *Anchor moves with the object, no writes*: the pin is drawn at `anchor`'s current transform
  applied to `point`.  Moving, rotating, scaling, re-layering or re-paging the object writes
  nothing under `0:12`, so comment nodes never become part of an artwork conflict.
* *Anchor deleted*: `anchor` reads unset (`REF_FALLBACK_UNSET`); the pin is not drawn; the
  panel shows `CommonProps.name` of the deleted node (still readable on the tombstone) with
  "(deleted)".  If the node is restored, the reference resolves again and the pin returns.
  If the node was compacted out (30 days), the reference is unknown: the panel shows the
  thread without an object name and the pin is drawn at `fallback_point`.
* *Re-pinning* (drag) writes `anchor`, `point`, `fallback_point` and `page` in one
  `SetFields`.  Two people dragging one pin: each field is LWW and all four come from the
  same op on each side, so the result is one person's drop, never a mix.
* *Reactions* are an add-wins set of `account:emoji`; concurrent add and remove of the same
  member keeps it.  A client removes its own members only; the server rejects a `SetRemove`
  of another account's member (role rule below).
* *Mentions* are a set so that concurrent edits that mention different people both notify.
  Removing a mention by editing the text removes the set member; the server never sends a
  second notification for a member re-added later within 24 hours.

Review sheet: `comments (0:12)` is excluded from the *local set* and *remote set* of the
divergence measurement (xref:reconcile.adoc[Reconciling offline edits]): comment threads are
never listed and never hold the outbox.  A commented object that was deleted remotely *is*
listed -- as _Edited and deleted_ on the *artwork* node -- because the client counts "has an
open thread anchored to it" as a local touch for the purpose of the edit-vs-delete row only.
*Restore* there is the ordinary `SetDeleted(false)`.

Branches: a branch's fork copies `0:12` with everything else; branch comment ops replay into
the parent as ordinary ops (new threads are `CreateNode` under `0:12`; replies are
`ElementInsert`s into threads that exist in both), which is exactly "branch threads merge into
the parent as threads".  `MergeBranch.excluded_nodes` never contains a comment node because
the sheet never lists one.  The `RestoreCommand` of xref:history.adoc[Version history] skips
`0:12`, so restoring a version neither deletes threads written since nor resurrects deleted
comments.

Read-time normalization:

* A `comment_thread` whose `deleted` is true but that has a non-deleted element after
  `comments[0]` is shown as a thread with a deleted opening comment.
* A thread whose `comments` sequence is empty (every element deleted) is not shown.
* A `reactions` member whose emoji is outside the allowed six, or whose account is not a
  member of the document, is not drawn.
* A `mentions` member naming an account that has since lost access is drawn as plain text.

=== Offline behavior

Every comment action is a change in the outbox and works offline; the outbox coalesces
repeated `SetFields` on one comment's body as it does for text.  `MarkRead` calls are queued
in `pending_calls` (COLLAB-024) and sent after the outbox drains; until then the local read
state is authoritative for badges.  Mention notifications are produced by the server when the
change is *sequenced*, so a mention written on a plane goes out on landing, dated then.
`GetUnread` is cached per document and served from the cache offline; counts may be stale by
whatever arrived meanwhile.  The email digest, the library-window mention dots and *Copy Link*
need nothing from the client beyond the sequenced change.

=== Server

The server does three things with comments beyond storing and relaying:

Mention notifications (COLLAB-030)::
On ingest, `SRV-004` already parses every op to validate it; an `ElementInsert` or
`SetFields` that writes a `Comment.mentions` member under a node in `0:12` enqueues a
`comment_notification` row per resolved account (a `team:` member expands to the team's
members who have access at that moment, excluding the author), and a `REPLY` row for the
thread's opener and prior repliers, and a `RESOLVED` row for the opener when `resolved` flips
to true.  Rows for accounts with a live session on the document are pushed as `CommentEvent`
on that session and marked `seen_at` when the client acknowledges with `MarkRead`.

Unread counters (COLLAB-030)::
`comment_read` per (account, document, thread); `GetUnread` evaluates against the
snapshotter's state (`wt-crdt`) at head, which the server keeps warm for open documents.
Rows are deleted with the document and with the account.

Email digest (COLLAB-031)::
A job, *Comment digest*, every 5 minutes: for each account with `MENTION` rows older than 10
minutes with no `seen_at` and no `emailed_at`, and with no live session on that document,
render one Qute mail per (account, document) listing the mentions with the thread's opening
line, the author and a `wiretuner://doc/<id>/thread/<node id>` link, honoring the *Email me
when I'm mentioned* preference read from the account's preferences.  Sends through the same
mailer as invitations (`mailpit` in the compose stack).

Commenter role (COLLAB-030)::
Effective-role evaluation gains a middle tier: a change from a `COMMENTER` session is accepted
if and only if every op in it targets a node under `0:12` (or creates one there), every
`Comment.author_account_id` written equals the caller, every `SetRemove` on `reactions`
removes a member prefixed with the caller's account id, and every `SetFields` on a `Comment`
targets an element the caller authored -- except `resolved`, which a commenter may write on a
thread whose `comments[0]` they authored.  Anything else is `ROLE_INSUFFICIENT`.  `EDITOR`
adds `resolved` and pin fields on any thread; `OWNER` adds `deleted` on any comment and
`SetDeleted` on any thread.  `VIEWER` sessions may not write under `0:12` at all.  This is
the one place the server's role check looks inside a change; it uses the same parsed ops as
validation and adds no second pass.

The server never renders or emails comment *bodies* beyond their first 200 characters (the
digest quotes the opening line only).

As built (COLLAB-030, COLLAB-031; `api/.../comments`):

* *The server's record of comments* is Postgres, not the snapshotter's merge state: SRV-007's warm
  `wt-crdt` state does not exist yet, and replaying a document's log into an engine on the ingest
  path for every commenter push would not hold the ingest target.  `CommentOps` reads every op of
  a change once (field paths starting at `NodeProps` field 210 are thread ops; `SetDeleted` and
  `MoveNode` targets are looked up), and after the change is accepted -- before it is fanned out --
  `CommentIndex` records it in `comment_thread` (opener, `resolved`) and `comment` (author,
  `deleted`, the first 200 characters as first typed), the two registers written last-writer-wins
  by op id with the replica compared unsigned.  Every statement is idempotent and runs again for
  an identical retry, which repairs a record that missed a change.  Unread (`GetUnread`) counts
  that record's comments.  A thread's *opener* is the account whose change created the thread
  node (the CreateThread command writes the opening comment in the same change).
* *The role rule* (`CommentRules`) applies to every role's ops on threads, not only a
  commenter's: an editor may resolve and move any pin but, like everyone, change only comments they
  wrote; the owner may also delete anyone's comment or thread.  A commenter's change may hold
  `Noop`s (they concern nothing).  Ops on comments and threads the server has no record of are
  refused for commenters; an editor's comment-shaped op on a node that is not a thread is not a
  comment (the engines ignore it) and passes.
* *Notifications*: a mention comes from the `mentions` set (on an inserted comment or a `SetAdd`)
  *and* from a `mention` mark, deduplicated per recipient and comment within 24 hours; recipients
  must be able to open the document (a `team:` member expands to the team's members who can).
  Replies notify the opener and earlier authors who can open the document.  `MarkRead` marks the
  thread's notifications up to the mark seen (a resolve notification with any mark).
* *API*: `docs.v1.CommentService` is `proto/wiretuner/docs/v1/comment.proto`;
  `ListMentionedDocuments` pages with `cursor`/`page_size` (at most 500, documents the caller can
  open, not trashed).  `CommentEvent` is `sync.v1.DocumentEvent` case 9 with a top-level
  `CommentEventKind` enum (buf's STANDARD lint wants enum values prefixed with the enum's name).
* *Digest*: *Email me when I'm mentioned* is the synced preference `sync.email_mentions` (bool,
  on when absent), stored by `AccountService.SetPreferences` (built with this task: per key newest
  `updated_at_ms` wins, the map as JSON in `account.preferences`).  A live session is a
  `LiveSessions` entry (Valkey, across nodes).  Mentions older than 7 days are no longer mailed.
  The mail quotes the first line of the thread's opening comment as first typed.

`CommentThreadModel` is a value built from the merged state (the app rebuilds it on each
`DocumentEvent`, as the panels do), not an `@Observable` list: every visible thread in creation
order with its pin number (its place among every thread ever created under `0:12`, so numbers
never shift), `Anchoring` (`point`, `object`, `deleted` -- pin not drawn, panel name with
"(deleted)" -- or `compacted` -- pin at `fallback_point`), the pin in pasteboard points (the
anchor's `Objects.pasteboardTransform` applied to `point`), the page (the anchor's top-level
object's page by the bounds rule of document-panel.adoc, else the stored `page` if live, else
the page under the pin), `byActivity`, `byPage` (nil = pasteboard), `pins(showResolved:)` and
an unread overlay (`unread:` comment ids per thread, `unreadCount`, `unreadTotal`).  The
read-time normalizations are applied there; `members:` (accounts with access, nil = unknown)
drops reactions and mention tags of accounts without access.  Commands: `CreateThread` and
`MovePin` take a pasteboard point and convert it into the anchor's local space; `Reply` takes
the opener's display name for its label ("Reply to Priya"); `EditComment` rewrites only the
characters between the common prefix and suffix, clears tags that no longer stand, marks new
ones and brings the `mentions` set in line in the same change (nothing is written for an
unchanged body); `DeleteComment` also deletes the thread for a reply-less opener, and
`DeleteThread` is the owner's *Delete Thread* (every comment's `deleted` and the thread's);
`SetResolved` writes nothing when the register already holds the value; `React` toggles when
`adding` is nil.  A body is sent in `TextInsert`s of at most 8,000 scalars.  Undo of a reply
is the engine's: the element is deleted and characters others typed into it stay.  Author and
account ids are the caller's account id, which the app supplies (WTModel knows no accounts).

* `WTModel/Comments`: `CommentThreadModel` (observable list of threads with resolved pin
  positions, page grouping and unread overlay), commands `CreateThread`, `Reply`,
  `EditComment`, `DeleteComment`, `SetResolved`, `MovePin`, `React`.  Each command is one
  change with a label (`Comment on Logo mark`, `Reply to Priya`, `Resolve thread 14`) and one
  undo step; *Delete* confirms and is undoable until the window closes.  The undo rule
  "never revert other people's work" applies unchanged.
* `WTRender`: `CommentPinLayer` above `PresenceOverlayLayer`, constant-size pins, unread badge,
  author color, hover preview; redrawn on comment changes and scroll/zoom, never invalidating
  document tiles.  Pins are hit-tested by the layer itself (`REND-003`'s spatial index is not
  involved): the Comment tool and the Pointer tool query it before the document.
* `WTApp/Comments`: `CommentTool` (kbd:[C]; click → `CreateThread` composer; drag →
  `MovePin`), `ThreadPopover` (SwiftUI; composer with `@` completion over `ListMembers`
  plus the team, reactions bar, Resolve/Reopen), `CommentsPanel` (filters, search, rows, row
  actions, Copy Link), `MentionNotice` (the in-canvas banner), the menu:View[Comments] switches
  bound to three preferences, the toolbar button with its count, and menu:Object[Add Comment…].
* `WTSync`: `CommentReadState` (local mirror of `comment_read` plus queued `MarkRead`s),
  `CommentEvent` handling, `ListMentionedDocuments` polling for the library window (on open and
  every 5 minutes).
* `WTInterchange`: the PDF writer's *Comments as annotations* option (COLLAB-033): one `/Text`
  annotation per open thread at the pin's page position, replies as `/Text` annotations with
  `/IRT` pointing at the opener, `/T` the author's display name, `/M` from `wall_time_ms`;
  dropped by the PDF/X validation pass with the existing "comments dropped" report line.
  The SVG, HTML, EPS and bitmap writers never see `0:12`.
* Presence: while the composer or a reply field has focus, `editing` carries the thread's
  node id, and the hover card renders a `comment_thread` id as _Commenting on <anchor name>_.

NOTE: As built by COLLAB-027's app glue and the UI halves of COLLAB-028 and COLLAB-029
(`WTApp/Collaboration/`: `CommentsFeatures`, `WindowComments`, `CommentTool`, `CommentThreadView`,
`CommentsPanel`).  The pins are drawn by the app, not by a `WTRender.CommentPinLayer`: each window
adds a `CanvasOverlayLayer` of its own above the presence layer (so a pin change or a scroll
repaints no document tile), redrawn on document changes and viewport changes; pins are numbered
circles in the opener's colour (a stable pick from `PresencePalette` by account id), grey when
resolved, with a blue badge while the thread holds unread comments, and the hovered pin's first line
beside it.  Hit testing is the layer's own (`WindowComments.thread(atView:viewport:)`).  Pins hide
while the Pen, Bezigon, Freeform or Pencil tool is active.  The three switches are
`PreferenceCatalog.Sync.showCommentPins`, `showResolvedPins` and `pinsFollowFilter`, listed under
*Sync and collaboration* and in menu:View[Comments].  The Comment tool (kbd:[C], added to the Tools
panel) starts a pending thread on the object under a click (`SelectionController.pick`) or at a
point; nothing is written until btn:[Comment] (`CreateThread`), and kbd:[Esc] discards the pending
pin.  A click on a pin opens the thread; a drag (a pin the caller may move) is `MovePin` onto the
object under the drop or empty space.  menu:Object[Add Comment…] pins the first selected object at
the selection's centre.  The popover is an `NSPopover` beside the pin with the thread, the composer
(`MentionDraft`: kbd:[@] completes over the document's roster and its team, the tags becoming
`CommentBody.Mention`s over the names still in the text), kbd:[Cmd+Return] to post, the per-comment
menu (Edit, Delete -- confirmed, undoable), the reactions bar and Resolve/Reopen, each shown only
when `CommentPermissions` allows it for the role (`LibraryDocument.Role` from the library cache; the
owner for a document without one).  The Comments panel (menu:Window[Comments], a *Comments* group
closed in the default layout) filters by state, page (*This page* is the active page), person and
*Mentions me*, searches, shows the unread count, and its rows select-to-scroll (double-click zooms)
with the row menu's Resolve/Reopen, Copy Link (`wiretuner://document/<id>?thread=<counter>.<replica>`)
and the owner's Delete Thread.  Unread is kept on this Mac: comments present when the window opened
count as read, a comment by someone else that arrives later is unread until its thread is displayed
(at once, not after one second).  A remote comment that mentions the caller (or the document's team)
shows "<name> mentioned you" with btn:[Show] in the bar above the canvas (the page notices'
`BannerAction`s).  Not built: `CommentReadState` against the server (`GetUnread`, `MarkRead`) and
`CommentEvent` handling, the toolbar Comments button with its count, the library window's mention
dots (`ListMentionedDocuments`), the Pointer tool querying pins, `editing` presence while the
composer has focus, and the UI tests (the app-hosted `CommentsFeatureTests` drive the same paths).
