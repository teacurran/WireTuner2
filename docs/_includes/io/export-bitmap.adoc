A bitmap export renders your artwork to pixels.  You choose the resolution, the smoothing,
whether the background is transparent, the color mode and the format's own settings; {product}
draws the page, area or selection exactly as on screen and writes PNG, JPEG, WebP, HEIC, AVIF,
GIF, TIFF, Photoshop, BMP or Targa.  Everything renders -- transparency, blend modes, live and
raster effects, placed images with their own transparency -- because pixels can hold anything.

To export a bitmap:

. Choose menu:File[Export…].
. Choose a bitmap format from the *Format* menu.
. Choose what to export: pages, the output area or the selection
  (xref:exporting.adoc[Exporting files]).  Each page becomes its own file.
. Click btn:[Options…].
. Set *Resolution*, *Scale*, *Anti-aliasing*, *Background* and *Color* (below), then the
  format's own options.
. Click btn:[OK], then btn:[Export].

== Options common to every bitmap format

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Effect

|*Resolution*
|Pixels per inch of the document's dimensions: *72* (screen), *144*, *300* (print), or any
value you type.  A 4-inch-wide page at 300 ppi is 1200 pixels wide.  The sheet shows the
resulting pixel size.

|*Scale*
|*1×*, *2×*, *3×*, or several at once.  Each scale writes its own file, with `@2x` or `@3x`
in the name (xref:exporting.adoc[Exporting files]).  Use it for Retina and mobile assets:
export at 72 ppi with *1× 2× 3×* checked and you get the three sizes an app needs.

|*Anti-aliasing*
|Edge smoothing: *None*, *2*, *3* or *4* (default).  Higher values render more samples per
pixel for smoother edges and finer detail in thin lines; *None* gives hard pixel edges for
pixel art and masks.

|*Background*
|*Transparent* writes an alpha channel where nothing is drawn (PNG, WebP, HEIC, AVIF, TIFF,
PSD, BMP, Targa).  *Page color* fills with the page's background color.  *White*.  Formats
without transparency (JPEG) offer only the last two.  GIF has its own transparency option.

|*Color*
|*RGB* (default), *Grayscale*, or *CMYK* for TIFF, JPEG and PSD.  CMYK exports embed the
document's CMYK profile (xref:color-profiles.adoc[Color profiles]).  *Embed color profile*
writes the profile into any RGB or CMYK format that can hold one; leave it on unless a
web tool objects.

|*RGB profile*
|*Automatic* (default): the document's Working RGB profile, or Display P3 when the artwork
holds colors outside sRGB -- Display P3, Lab or OKLab colors -- so those colors arrive intact
(xref:spot-process.adoc[Spot and process colors]).  *sRGB*, *Display P3* or *Working RGB*
force a space.  A wide-gamut export with *Embed color profile* off would be read as sRGB by
everything that opens it, so {product} pulls such colors into sRGB instead and the export
summary says how many.

|*Simulate overprint*
|Renders overprinting objects as they would print, rather than as they preview.

|*Include hidden layers*
|Mirrors the document setting (xref:printing.adoc[Printing]).
|===

Higher resolution and anti-aliasing take longer and use more memory; a 300 ppi export of a
poster with soft shadows can take a while.  The progress bar lets you keep working.

== Alpha channels and transparency

An alpha channel records how opaque each pixel is.  With *Background: Transparent*, objects'
edges, feathered effects and partially transparent fills all keep their exact opacity in the
exported file, so the image sits on any background in a web page, video or image editor with
no fringe.  Placed images that had transparency of their own keep it too.

Mask from a background layer::
For custom transparency that is not the shape of the artwork, draw the mask on a layer
below the artwork and select *Mask from layer* in the options, choosing the layer.  White
areas of that layer become opaque, black transparent, grays in between; the layer itself is
not drawn.  A non-printing background layer is a convenient place for it
(xref:layers.adoc[Layers]).

NOTE: Objects with effects keep their transparency in the export, and an imported image's own
alpha channel is preserved.

== PNG

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Effect

|*Bit depth*
|*8-bit palette* (256 colors, smallest), *24-bit* (millions of colors), *32-bit with alpha*
(default when the background is transparent), *48-bit* and *64-bit with alpha* (16 bits per
channel, for wide-gamut or further editing).

|*Interlaced*
|Progressive display while loading.  Slightly larger files.

|*Compression*
|*Smallest* or *Fastest*.  Both are lossless.
|===

== JPEG

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Effect

|*Quality*
|1 to 100 (default 85).  Lower discards more detail for smaller files; artifacts appear
around sharp edges and text below about 70.  The sheet shows the estimated file size.

|*Progressive*
|Displays a coarse image first while loading.

|*Chroma subsampling*
|*Auto* (4:2:0 below quality 90, 4:4:4 above), *4:4:4* keeps color detail sharp around
text and edges, *4:2:0* is smallest.
|===

JPEG cannot hold transparency; *Background* is *Page color* or *White*.

== WebP, HEIC and AVIF

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Effect

|*Lossless*
|Exact pixels (WebP and AVIF).  Off uses *Quality*.

|*Quality*
|1 to 100 (default 80).  These formats keep more detail than JPEG at the same size.

|*Speed / effort*
|AVIF only: slower encoding produces smaller files.
|===

All three keep alpha and can embed a color profile, and all three follow the *RGB profile*
rule: a document with Display P3 colors exports in Display P3 with the profile embedded,
which every current browser and Apple app reads correctly.

== GIF

GIF is limited to 256 colors and one fully transparent color.  Use it only where GIF is
required; PNG or WebP is better in every other respect.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Effect

|*Palette*
|*Adaptive* (default) picks the 256 colors that best fit the image.  *Exact* uses the
artwork's own colors when there are at most 256; unavailable otherwise.  *Web 216* uses the
old browser-safe palette.  *Grayscale*.  *Custom…* loads an Adobe `.aco` or `.act` palette
file.

|*Colors*
|The number of palette entries, 2 to 256.  Fewer colors, smaller file.

|*Dither*
|0 to 100%.  Simulates colors outside the palette with patterns of neighboring pixels;
smooths gradients at the cost of size and a grainy look.

|*Transparent background*
|Pixels where nothing is drawn become the transparent index.  Edges are hard: GIF cannot do
partial transparency.  *Matte* chooses the color anti-aliased edges blend toward, so the
image sits cleanly on a background of that color.

|*Interlaced*
|Progressive display while loading.
|===

The options sheet shows the resulting palette; unused entries are marked.

== TIFF

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Effect

|*Compression*
|*None*, *LZW* (default, lossless), *ZIP* (lossless, smaller), *JPEG* (lossy, with a quality).

|*Bit depth*
|*8-bit palette*, *24-bit*, *32-bit with alpha*, *48-bit*, *64-bit with alpha*.  CMYK is 32-bit
or 40-bit with alpha.

|*Byte order*
|*Mac* or *PC*.  Every current reader accepts both.
|===

TIFF is the right choice for print bitmaps: CMYK with the document's profile, lossless, and
accepted by every layout program.

== Photoshop (PSD)

A PSD export can keep the document's layers.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Effect

|*Layers*
|*From document layers* (default) writes each {product} layer as a Photoshop layer holding
that layer's objects rendered to pixels; hidden layers are skipped unless *Include hidden
layers* is on.  *Flattened* writes one layer.

|*Layer transparency*
|Each layer keeps transparent pixels where it draws nothing, so layers stack in Photoshop as
they do in {product}.  Layer blend modes and opacity from {product} are written as Photoshop
layer blend modes and opacity where they exist.

|*Bit depth*
|*8 bits per channel* or *16 bits per channel*.

|*Color*
|RGB, Grayscale or CMYK.
|===

A multi-page document writes one PSD per page.  Objects inside a layer are merged into that
layer's pixels; if you want each object on its own layer, put them on separate {product}
layers first.

== BMP and Targa

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Effect

|*Bit depth*
|BMP: *8-bit*, *16-bit*, *24-bit*, *32-bit with alpha*.  Targa: *8-bit*, *16-bit*, *24-bit*,
*32-bit with alpha*.

|*Compression*
|BMP: *None* or *RLE* (8-bit only).  Targa: *None* or *RLE*.
|===

== Bitmap export defaults

menu:{product}[Settings… > Export > Bitmap Export Defaults] sets what the options sheet starts
with for every bitmap format: *Default export resolution* (72 ppi), *Default anti-aliasing*
(4), *Default background* (Transparent) and *Embed color profile* (on).  Presets override
these (xref:exporting.adoc[Exporting files]).

== Exporting many pages or many sizes

Choosing *All pages* or a range writes one file per page; checking several scales multiplies
that by the number of scales.  The files are named by the pattern on
xref:exporting.adoc[Exporting files], and a summary of what was written appears when the
export completes.  Exporting the *output area* or a *selection* at several scales is the
quickest way to produce app icon and web asset sets.

== Working with others

Bitmap exports read the document and change nothing.  What renders is what your Mac shows
at the moment of export, including every collaborator's change that has arrived.  Placed
images someone added while you were offline may still be downloading when you reconnect;
the export sheet lists any that are not yet on this Mac and offers to wait, since an export
with a placeholder in it is rarely what you want.

`mask_layer` is the only reference into the document; when the layer no longer exists the
option reads as unset and the sheet says "Mask layer not found".

=== Merge semantics

None; exports write nothing.

=== Offline behavior

Works offline.  Placed images whose blobs are not cached render as placeholders; the sheet
lists them and, when online, offers *Wait for downloads* which blocks the export until the
blob queue delivers them (SYNC-008).

=== Server

Nothing.

=== Client

* Rasterization goes through `WTRender` into a `CGBitmapContext` of the target size times
  the anti-aliasing factor, then box-downsamples (the same path Preview mode uses, so the
  export matches the screen).  For anti-aliasing 1 the context is drawn with
  `setShouldAntialias(false)`.  Large outputs render in 2048-pixel tiles to bound memory
  (a 300 ppi A0 poster at 4× is 1.5 gigapixels of samples; tiling keeps peak memory under
  1 GiB).
* Color: the context's color space is the export's RGB space (`rgb_space`, resolved through
  `OutputContext.rgbExportSpace` from CMS-015 when `AUTO`) or the document's CMYK profile
  (CMS epic); every tagged `Color` in the display list converts into it as it is drawn, so
  *Convert* is done by drawing into a context of the target space.  When the resolved space
  is sRGB and the document has wider colors, the display list's colors are first passed
  through COLOR-024's gamut mapping (rather than ColorSync's per-channel clip) and the
  clipped count goes to the export summary.  Grayscale draws into
  `CGColorSpace.genericGrayGamma2_2` unless the document specifies a gray profile.  Profiles
  are embedded through `kCGImagePropertyProfileName`/ICC data on the destination; for
  Display P3 the bundled profile bytes from CMS-015.
* *Mask from layer*: the layer is rendered alone to a gray context at the same size; its
  luminance multiplies the artwork's alpha.
* Encoders: ImageIO `CGImageDestination` for PNG, JPEG, TIFF, GIF, BMP and HEIC; WebP through
  ImageIO where the running OS encodes it, otherwise bundled `libwebp`; AVIF through bundled
  `libavif` (with `libaom` or `SVT-AV1`); Targa and PSD by own writers.  GIF quantization is
  own code (median cut with optional Floyd–Steinberg dithering) because ImageIO's GIF encoder
  offers no palette control.
* PSD: version 1 PSD (or PSB above 30,000 pixels), layer records with `lsct` names from layer
  names, blend mode keys mapped from the document's blend modes (normal, multiply, screen,
  overlay, darken, lighten, difference, exclusion, hue, saturation, color, luminosity; others
  fall back to normal with a note), 8 or 16 bits, RLE-compressed channels, composite image
  written for readers that ignore layers, XMP and IIM image resources from IO-012.
* Undo: none.

NOTE: Deviations recorded by IO-021 (`WTInterchange/Bitmap`).  Tiles are at most 2048 samples a
side (so 512 output pixels at anti-aliasing 4) and output is produced a band of tiles at a time,
pulled by the encoder through a sequential `CGDataProvider` (the Targa writer reads bands
directly).  `PerformanceTests` (`WTINTERCHANGE_PERF=1`, release) reports rather than gates the
peak: an A3 page at 300 ppi, 2×, anti-aliasing 4 (7017 × 9925 pixels) exported in 25--26 s with a
process peak of 333 MiB as Targa and 384 MiB as PNG on the build Mac; the 300 ppi A0 at 2× case of
IO-021 (8× the pixels) was not run, and whether ImageIO's encoders stay within 1 GiB there is
unverified.  Every sample tile is drawn by WTRender's `CoreGraphicsRenderer` with
`rasterPreview = .document`; an export at 72 ppi with anti-aliasing 4 equals the reference
renderer's 4× bitmap box-downsampled within 1/255.  Until WTRender's display list carries colour
spaces (its `Color` is sRGB and its renderer clamps), *Automatic* picks Display P3 only for
extended-range colour values, which the renderer still draws clipped; the P3 tag and the clipped
count are in place for CMS-015.  *Working RGB* is sRGB and CMYK is Generic CMYK until the CMS epic
supplies document profiles.  Greyscale and CMYK have no alpha channel (Core Graphics bitmap
contexts do not support one), so a transparent background needs RGB.  *Mask from layer* receives
the layer's artwork as a `DisplayList` in the options.  Not available yet: 8-bit palette PNG and
TIFF (IO-022's quantizer), BMP 8 and 16 bit and RLE (ImageIO writes 24 and 32 bit only), a
byte-order choice for TIFF and a chroma-subsampling choice for JPEG (ImageIO decides both).  The
options sheets and *Bitmap Export Defaults* belong to the dialog task (IO-014/IO-015); goldens are
under `WTInterchangeTests/Goldens` (`WTINTERCHANGE_RECORD_GOLDENS=1`).
