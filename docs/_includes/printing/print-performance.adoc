{product} draws a printed page the way it draws the canvas, so a document that is quick to
redraw is quick to print, and a document that is slow on screen will be slow to spool.  The
printer then has its own limits: memory, and the speed of its interpreter on a complex page.
This page collects what to do about both, and describes the progress panel that lets a long
job be watched or cancelled.

== Drawing for fast output

Most of these also make the document lighter to edit and to sync.

Keep only what prints::
Delete objects that are hidden behind others, empty text blocks and unused colors;
menu:Extensions[Cleanup] has commands for the last two.  Everything on a printing layer is
processed even when it is covered.  Move reference material to a background layer, which never
prints (xref:layers.adoc[Layers]).

Use fewer points::
A path drawn with the Pencil or produced by tracing can carry ten times the points it needs.
menu:Modify[Alter Path > Simplify] reduces them (xref:editing-paths.adoc[Editing paths]).
Shape curves with handles rather than extra points; use straight segments where a curve is not
needed.

Raise flatness::
*Flatness* in the Print dialog's *Imaging* group lets the printer approximate curves with
fewer straight pieces.  1 to 3 is invisible at normal viewing distance on a 600 dpi printer;
per-path flatness in the Object panel targets only the paths that need it.

Go easy on effects::
Transparency, raster effects, lens fills and blends with many steps are rasterized at print
time at the document's raster effect resolution (xref:raster-effects.adoc[Raster effects]).
Lower that resolution for proofs and raise it for the final run.  Gradients themselves are
cheap -- they print as smooth shadings -- but a dozen stacked multi-color radial fills are not.

Prepare images before placing them::
Crop, scale and rotate bitmaps in an image editor so the printed job carries only the pixels
that show (xref:cropping-bitmaps.adoc[Cropping bitmaps], xref:bitmaps.adoc[Bitmaps]).  A
placed image prints at its native resolution, however small it appears on the page.

Simplify clipping paths::
A clipping path is applied to everything inside it; a complex clip over complex contents
multiplies the work.

Text::
Text on a path and the Zoom text effect are the slow ones; use them where they earn their
place.  Fewer typefaces mean fewer fonts to embed.  Object screens from the Halftones panel
add a rendering pass each when screening in {product}.

== Options for difficult printers

Two options in the *{product}* pane trade quality or file size for reliability:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Use it when

|*Rasterize output* (with a resolution)
|The printer runs out of memory, prints blank pages, or drops objects on a complex document.
The whole sheet becomes one image at the resolution you choose; 300 dpi is enough for a
proof, 600 for line art on a laser printer.  Marks and labels stay sharp.

|*Print text as outlines*
|One font prints wrongly on one printer (xref:print-fonts.adoc[Printing fonts]).
|===

Both affect this job only.  Neither changes the document.

== Watching and cancelling a job

Once you click btn:[Print], {product} draws the sheets in the background while you keep
working.  A progress panel shows the sheet being drawn (`Drawing sheet 3 of 8, Magenta`), a
bar, and btn:[Cancel].  Cancelling stops the job before anything reaches the printer; sheets
already spooled by macOS appear in the printer's queue, where they can be cancelled from the
Dock.  The document stays editable throughout; the job prints the document as it was when you
clicked btn:[Print], so later edits do not leak into it.

== Working with others

Printing never slows a collaborator down: a job is drawn from a snapshot on your Mac, and
nothing about it travels to them.  The cleanup steps above do change the shared document --
deleting hidden objects deletes them for everyone -- so mention it, or do them on a branch
(xref:branches.adoc[Branches]).

As on xref:printing.adoc[Printing].  A print job reads an immutable snapshot of the model

=== Client

* `NSPrintOperation.canSpawnSeparateThread = true`; `PrintPlanView` holds a snapshot of the
  document value and a display list built from it, so drawing runs off the main actor without
  touching the live model.
* Progress: `showsProgressPanel = false` for the system panel; {product} shows its own sheet
  attached to the document window, fed by `PrintPlanView` reporting `(sheetIndex, sheetName)`
  from `rectForPage(_:)`.  Cancel calls `NSPrintOperation.current?.destroyContext()` after the
  current sheet and marks the plan cancelled so no further `draw` runs; the screener and
  rasterizer check `Task.isCancelled` between tiles.
* Rasterize output: render the sheet through `WTRender` into a `CGBitmapContext` at
  `rasterize_dpi` (tiled in 4096-px bands to bound memory), then draw the image into the
  print context; marks and labels drawn afterwards as vectors.
* Raster effect resolution at print time is the document's setting (FX epic); the print path
  passes the target dpi to the effect rasterizer instead of the screen scale.
* A `WTInterchange/Print/Corpus` test target renders every corpus document through the
  composite, separated, tiled and rasterized paths to PDF and compares rasterized pages with
  the canvas renderer's output.

NOTE: *Rasterize output* is drawn by `PrintSheetRenderer` (PRINT-006's hook, built with it): the sheet's artwork -- composite
in RGB, a plate in gray -- rendered at `rasterize_dpi` over the printed area in bands of at most `bandHeight` (4096) rows,
each band placed as one image, the marks and labels drawn afterwards as vectors; `isCancelled` is polled between bands and by
the screener, and a cancelled sheet throws `CancellationError`.  The progress sheet, the snapshot hand-off and the print-time
raster effect resolution remain PRINT-013's.

NOTE: As built by PRINT-013 (package half; `WTInterchange/Print/PrintRun.swift`, `PrintSheetRenderer.swift`).  `PrintRun`
draws a job from its `PrintPlan` -- the snapshot -- sheet by sheet (`draw(sheet:into:)` for the print view, `pdf()` /
`pdfInBackground()` for a whole job off the caller's actor), reporting `PrintProgress` (`Drawing sheet 3 of 8, Page 1,
Magenta`, a fraction for the bar) before each sheet.  `cancel()` sets the flag the renderer polls between bands and
screener tiles, refuses every further sheet with `CancellationError` and drops the plan at once; the last sheet drawn drops
it too.  Sheets render raster effects at each object's own resolution, synchronously, whatever the window's *Raster effect
preview* (`PrintSheetRenderer.renderer(for:)` sets `rasterPreview = .document`, no progressive callback) -- the print-time
resolution hook.  `rasterBandBytes` bounds a rasterized sheet's own bitmap (216 MB for a tabloid sheet at 1200 dpi); the
print or PDF context still holds each placed band until the page ends, so the 512 MiB peak for that sheet is not
verified.

NOTE: As built by PRINT-013's app half (`WTApp/Print/PrintProgress.swift`, `PrintJob.swift`, `PrintFeatures.swift`).  The
Print dialog's operation runs with `showsProgressPanel = false`; once btn:[Print] starts the job, the print view hands each
spooled sheet (never the dialog's preview) to a `PrintSpooler`, which starts a `PrintRun` over the job's plan at its first
sheet and shows WireTuner's progress panel over the document window -- the sheet's label, a bar, btn:[Cancel] (kbd:[Esc]).
A cancel stops the run; the next sheet destroys the print context (`NSPrintOperation.current?.destroyContext()`) and closes
the panel, so nothing more reaches the printer, and the snapshot is released at once.  Deviation: the job is drawn on the
main thread, not with `canSpawnSeparateThread`: the print view is an `NSView`, main-actor code under Swift 6, which traps
when AppKit calls it from its print thread.  The panel is therefore redrawn, and its clicks and kbd:[Esc] delivered
(`PrintProgressPanel.pump`), at each sheet boundary, so a cancel takes effect before the next sheet rather than at the next
band of the sheet in progress.  The 200-sheet test is app-hosted, as elsewhere in this book
(`PrintSessionTests.aTwoHundredSheetJobKeepsTheWindowResponsiveAndIgnoresLaterEdits`, PRINT-013's rest): 50 separated pages'
200 sheets drawn from the plan's snapshot by `PrintRun.pdfInBackground` while twenty text blocks are typed into the document
-- they land while sheets are still being drawn -- and the 200-page PDF holds none of the typed text.

NOTE: As built by PRINT-015 (`client/Packages/WTInterchange/Tests/WTInterchangeTests/PrintCorpusTests.swift`, references in
`Tests/WTInterchangeTests/Goldens/PrintCorpus/`).  The corpus is a suite of the package's test target rather than a test
target of its own (it shares the export corpus and helpers; `swift test --filter PrintCorpusTests` runs it alone): one
case per option of the Printing, Output devices and Halftones pages, each naming what it exercises (a test holds the list
to the pages' options) -- the scale modes, offset, automatic and manual tiling with overlap and zero point, every mark,
bleed and page boundary, emulsion and negative, separations with process and spot plates and overprint, spot as process,
chosen plates with their angles and frequencies, *Screen in {product}* with document and object screens at a device
resolution, *Ignore object screens*, flatness and object flatness with text as outlines, rasterized output, output area,
selection and page range.  Each job is written through `PrintPDF.data` and every sheet rasterized by Core Graphics at 72
ppi and held to its reference PNG (at most 0.2% of pixels beyond 24/255); a drifting sheet fails naming the case, sheet
and sheet name and writes the fresh rendering and a difference image to `$WT_PRINT_CORPUS_OUT`.  *Deviation*: the
references are rasterized PNGs, not PDFs and checksums -- a PDF carries its creation date and Core Graphics' anti-aliasing
is not bit-stable across macOS releases, so a checksum would fail for no fault of the output.  The composite cases print
`Corpus.fixture`'s pages, the display lists `PDFTests` holds screen against PDF, and their sheets are held to the canvas
renderer's pixels too.  The CI job is proposed in the IO/PRINT handoff (`.github/` is not this task's to edit).
