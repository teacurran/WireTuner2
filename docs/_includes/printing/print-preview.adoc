The Print dialog shows, on its left, a preview of every sheet the job will produce, drawn with
the settings currently chosen.  Change the scale, turn on tiling, switch to separations or add
crop marks, and the preview redraws.  It is the place to check that a page fits the paper,
that marks are not being clipped, and that a tiled poster comes out as the number of sheets
you expect -- before any paper is used.

NOTE: The preview is the macOS Print dialog's own and is not draggable; the page is moved on
the paper with the *Offset* fields.

== Reading the preview

The preview is a true rendering of the sheet: the artwork as it prints, the paper edge, and
every mark and label.  A few things are drawn in addition, in light gray, and do not print:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Element |Meaning

|Paper outline
|The sheet chosen in *Paper Size*, in the chosen orientation.

|Printable area
|A dotted rectangle just inside the paper edge: the region the printer can reach.  Anything
outside it -- artwork, marks or labels -- is clipped, and a small warning appears under the
preview.

|Page outline
|The document page, or the output area's boundary when printing that
(xref:output-area.adoc[Defining an output area]).

|Bleed area
|A tinted band around the page, shown only when *Bleed* is greater than 0.

|Tile grid
|With automatic tiling, the grid of tiles over the page, with the current sheet highlighted
and the overlap shown as a hatched strip.
|===

Use the arrows beneath the preview to step through the sheets.  The counter reads `Sheet 3 of
8`; with separations on, each sheet is also named for its ink (`Page 1, Magenta`), and with
tiling, for its position (`Page 1, row 2 column 1`).

== Positioning the page on the paper

The page is centred on the paper by default.

To move it:

. In the *{product}* pane, enter values for *Offset X* and *Offset Y* in document units.
  Positive values move the page right and down.
. Watch the preview; the page moves as you type.
. Click btn:[Center] to return it to the centre of the paper.

The offset moves the printed page only; nothing in the document moves.  The offset is a
document print setting, so the whole team prints with the same placement until someone changes
it.

== Previewing at full size

For a closer look than the dialog allows, choose *Open in Preview* from the btn:[PDF] menu at
the bottom of the dialog.  Preview opens the exact PDF the printer would receive, every sheet
of it, at any magnification.  Nothing is sent to the printer; return to {product} and print
when satisfied.

== Working with others

The preview reflects the document as it is now, including changes collaborators make while
the dialog is open: an object someone moves onto the page appears in the preview a moment
later, and a bleed value someone changes redraws the bleed band.  What prints is what the
preview shows when you click btn:[Print].

== Specification

The preview is the print panel's own preview of the `NSPrintOperation` view; {product} adds
nothing to the panel's chrome beyond its accessory pane.  What this page specifies is what the
view draws in preview and how the pane keeps it current.

=== Data model

Adds `PrintSettings.offset` (a `Point`, ATOMIC), sketched on xref:printing.adoc[Printing].
No other fields.

=== Merge semantics

`offset` is one ATOMIC register: concurrent moves keep the later one whole.  It follows the
review-sheet exemption for print settings.  Read-time: an offset that places the page entirely
outside the printable area is honored (the user may want it) but the preview warns.

=== Offline behavior

Works offline.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

* `PrintPlanView.draw(_:)` checks `NSPrintOperation.current?.isPreview` (the panel sets the
  operation's context for preview) and, when true, draws the non-printing overlays after the
  sheet: paper outline, printable area (from `NSPrintInfo.imageablePageBounds`), page outline,
  bleed band, tile grid with the current tile highlighted.  Overlays are never drawn to the
  spooled job.
* The sheet name shown under the preview comes from the view's `printJobTitle` and the
  accessory's `localizedSummaryItems()`; the panel shows `Sheet n of m` itself.
* The pane declares `keyPathsForValuesAffectingPreview` for every setting including the
  offset; the panel invalidates and re-paginates on any change.
* Clipping detection: after pagination the plan computes whether any mark or label rectangle
  falls outside `imageablePageBounds` and publishes a warning string the pane shows beneath
  the preview.
* Remote changes to the document while the dialog is open post the same invalidation, so the
  preview redraws when artwork or settings change under it.

NOTE: As built by PRINT-004 (`WTApp/Print/PrintJob.swift`, `PrintSession.swift`).  AppKit has no `isPreview` on
`NSPrintOperation`; `PrintPlanView` draws `PrintSheetRenderer.drawPreviewOverlays` after each sheet while the accessory's
pane is on screen, which is while the panel shows its preview -- the job spools after the panel closes.  Each sheet's name
(`OutputSheet.name`) is the view's `pageHeader`; the pane shows the sheet count and, beneath its groups, the plan's
`clippingWarning`.  The preview repaginates through the accessory's `revision` key path, bumped on every commit and after
every document change (a collaborator's included) once the session has made the plan again.
