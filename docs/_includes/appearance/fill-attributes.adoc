A fill colors the inside of a path.  {product} offers seven kinds of fill, chosen from the
*Fill type* pop-up at the top of the fill editor in the Object panel: *Basic* (a solid color),
*Gradient* (a blend of colors, on its own page: xref:gradients.adoc[Gradients]), *Lens* (a
window that alters whatever lies beneath the object), *Custom* (a repeating ornamental
pattern), *Pattern* (an editable bitmap), *Textured* (a printed-texture look) and *Tiled*
(artwork of your own repeated across the interior).  Select the fill in the Attributes list
(xref:attribute-stack.adoc[Strokes, fills and the Attributes list]) and the editor below shows
the options for its kind.  Colors are chosen as described in xref:applying-color.adoc[Applying
color].

An open path is filled as if a straight line joined its two endpoints; only the fill treats it
as closed.  Where a path crosses itself or has several contours, the path's *fill rule*
(even-odd or non-zero winding, set in xref:editing-paths.adoc[Editing paths]) decides which
regions are inside, and every fill in the object's list obeys the same rule.

NOTE: There is no *PostScript* fill type, for the reason given on the
xref:stroke-attributes.adoc[Strokes] page.  Custom and Textured fills are drawn on screen.

== Basic fills

To apply a Basic fill to the selected fill:

. Choose *Basic* from the *Fill type* pop-up.
. Choose a color: pick from the color pop-up in the editor, drag a swatch from the Swatches
  panel onto the fill's row, drag a color from the Eyedropper tool onto the color box, or use
  the fill well in the Tools panel.

Check *Overprint* to print the fill on top of the colors beneath it rather than knocking them
out (xref:printing.adoc[Printing]).  A Basic fill whose color is *None* paints nothing; the
object can then only be selected by its stroke.

== Custom fills

A Custom fill repeats one of ten ornamental patterns across the interior.  The pattern is drawn
at a fixed size on the page and does not scale when you scale the object.  Four patterns are
*opaque* -- Black & White Noise, Bricks, Noise and Tiger Teeth hide whatever is behind the
object -- and six are *transparent*, drawing only their marks: Circles, Hatch, Random Grass,
Random Leaves, Squares and Top Noise.

To apply a Custom fill to the selected fill:

. Choose *Custom* from the *Fill type* pop-up.
. Choose a pattern from the pattern pop-up and set its options:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Pattern |Options

|Black & White Noise
|None.  Opaque black-and-white noise.

|Bricks
|*Color* of the bricks, *Mortar* color, brick *Width* and *Height* in document units, and
*Angle* (positive rotates clockwise, negative counterclockwise).

|Circles
|*Color*, *Radius*, *Spacing* between circle centers (more than twice the radius keeps circles
apart), *Angle*.

|Hatch
|*Color*, *Angle 1* and *Angle 2* for the two sets of parallel lines, *Spacing* between lines,
line *Width*.

|Noise
|*Whiteness* from 0% (black) to 100% (white); higher values are noisier.

|Random Grass
|*Count* of blades, 1 to 32,000.

|Random Leaves
|*Count* of leaves, 1 to 32,000.

|Squares
|*Color*, *Side length*, *Spacing* between centers (more than the side keeps squares apart),
*Angle*, outline *Width*.

|Tiger Teeth
|*Color* of the teeth, *Background* color, *Count* of teeth from 1 to 700, *Angle*.

|Top Noise
|*Gray* from 0% (black) to 100% (white).
|===

Random Grass and Random Leaves place their marks once per fill, so the picture is stable: it
looks the same every time it is drawn and on every collaborator's screen.

== Lens fills

A Lens fill turns the object into a window that changes the appearance of everything beneath
it.  Six lenses are available.  Lenses cannot be applied to placed EPS artwork, to clipping
paths, or to text that has not been converted to paths; a lens-filled object can itself be
pasted inside another object.

TIP: Stacking many lenses on top of one another makes every object beneath them expensive to
draw and print.  Use the *Snapshot* option to freeze a lens once it looks right.

To apply a Lens fill to the selected fill:

. Choose *Lens* from the *Fill type* pop-up.
. Choose a lens:
+
[horizontal]
Transparency:: Shows objects beneath through a tint of the chosen color, from 0 (fully
transparent) to 100 (fully opaque).
Magnify:: Enlarges objects beneath by a factor from 1 to 20.
Invert:: Replaces every color beneath with its CMYK complement, like a negative.
Lighten:: Lightens objects beneath; 0 does nothing, 100 is solid white.
Darken:: Darkens objects beneath; 0 does nothing, 100 is solid black.
Monochrome:: Shows objects beneath as tints of the chosen color, the tint set by each
original color's lightness.
. For Transparency and Monochrome, choose a color.
. For Magnify, enter a magnification or drag the slider; for Transparency, Lighten and Darken,
  enter an amount from 0 to 100 or drag the slider.
. Set any of the three lens options:
+
[horizontal]
Centerpoint:: Shows a handle at the lens's center.  Drag it with the Pointer tool to move the
center of magnification or the point the lens looks from; kbd:[Shift]-click it to return it to
the object's center.  The handle is visible only while the object is selected.
Objects only:: Applies the lens to objects beneath it only, leaving empty page areas untouched
(so an Invert lens over blank paper stays white instead of turning black).
Snapshot:: Captures what the lens currently shows and keeps showing it wherever the object is
moved.  Turning Snapshot off returns the lens to live.

Choosing a different lens clears Centerpoint, Objects only and Snapshot.  Spot colors seen
through a lens are output as process color; the Eyedropper can pick up the colors a lens
produces.

== Pattern fills

A Pattern fill repeats an 8-by-8 pixel bitmap in the fill's color across the interior.  It
uses the same editor and 64 built-in patterns as Pattern strokes
(xref:stroke-attributes.adoc[Strokes]); each pixel is a quarter point on the page and the
pattern does not scale with the object.

To apply a Pattern fill to the selected fill:

. Choose *Pattern* from the *Fill type* pop-up.
. Choose a color.
. Click a pattern in the palette at the lower right; drag the slider to scroll through the
  patterns.

To edit the pattern, click or drag pixels in the editing grid at the lower left, and use
btn:[Clear] and btn:[Invert] as for strokes.  Edits belong to this fill only.

== Textured fills

A Textured fill covers the interior with a fixed texture in one color.  Textures are not
editable and, like Custom fills, are drawn at one size on the page regardless of the object's
size.

To apply a Textured fill to the selected fill:

. Choose *Textured* from the *Fill type* pop-up.
. Choose a texture from the texture pop-up: Burlap, Denim, Gravel, Marble, Mesh, Oak, Sand
  or Stucco.
. Choose a color.

== Tiled fills

A Tiled fill repeats a piece of your own artwork -- any graphics or text you copy -- across the
interior.  The tile cannot be a placed EPS file, a bitmap image, another tiled-filled object or
a lens-filled object.

To create a Tiled fill for the selected fill:

. Select the artwork to use as the tile and choose menu:Edit[Copy].
. Select the object to fill.
. In the Object panel, select the fill to change, or click btn:[Add Fill].
. Choose *Tiled* from the *Fill type* pop-up.
. Click btn:[Paste In].  The tile appears in the preview and the fill is drawn.
. Set the tile's *Angle* by typing a value or dragging the dial.
. Set *Scale* x and y percentages to resize the tile.
. Set *Offset* x and y to shift the pattern within the object.  Positive x moves it right,
  negative left; positive y moves it up, negative down.

btn:[Copy Out] puts the tile artwork on the clipboard, so you can edit it and paste it back in
or use it in another fill.  A tile is a copy: editing the original objects on the page does not
change the fill.

To move an object without moving its tiled fill, turn off *Fills* in the Transform panel
(menu:Window[Transform]) before dragging; turn it back on to have the fill travel with the
object again (xref:transforming.adoc[Transforming objects]).

== Working with others

Fill settings are shared as you change them.  Two people can adjust different settings of one
fill -- one the color of a Hatch pattern, the other its angle -- and both changes stand.  When
both change the same setting, the later change stands and the review sheet lists both values
after an offline session.

A tile, a lens snapshot and an edited pattern bitmap are each a single picture: if two people
paste in different tiles at the same moment, one tile wins and the other person sees the fill
change under them.  Lens fills always show what is beneath the object *on your screen*, so a
collaborator moving objects under your lens changes what you see through it straight away; a
Snapshot freezes the picture you captured, on every screen.

// proto/wiretuner/doc/v1/fill.proto (sketch)

message BasicFill {
  ColorRef color = 1 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];   // None paints nothing
  bool overprint = 2;
}

message CustomFill {
  CustomFillPattern pattern = 1;
  ColorRef color = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];    // Bricks, Circles, Hatch, Squares, Tiger Teeth
  ColorRef color2 = 3 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];   // Bricks mortar; Tiger Teeth background
  double width = 4;                    // Bricks width; Hatch line width; Squares outline width
  double height = 5;                   // Bricks
  double radius = 6;                   // Circles
  double side = 7;                     // Squares
  double spacing = 8;                  // Circles, Hatch, Squares
  double angle = 9;                    // degrees, clockwise positive
  double angle2 = 10;                  // Hatch second set
  double whiteness = 11;               // Noise, Top Noise: 0 .. 100
  uint32 count = 12;                   // Grass/Leaves 1 .. 32000; Tiger Teeth 1 .. 700
  fixed64 seed = 13;                   // Random Grass / Leaves placement; fixed at creation
  bool overprint = 14;
}
enum CustomFillPattern {   // values carry the CUSTOM_FILL_PATTERN_ prefix in fill.proto
  CUSTOM_FILL_PATTERN_UNSPECIFIED = 0; BLACK_WHITE_NOISE = 1; BRICKS = 2; CIRCLES = 3;
  HATCH = 4; NOISE = 5; RANDOM_GRASS = 6; RANDOM_LEAVES = 7; SQUARES = 8; TIGER_TEETH = 9;
  TOP_NOISE = 10;
}

message LensFill {
  LensType type = 1;
  ColorRef color = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];   // Transparency, Monochrome
  double amount = 3;                   // Transparency opacity, Lighten, Darken: 0 .. 100
  double magnification = 4;            // Magnify: 1 .. 20
  bool centerpoint_shown = 5;
  // Object-local coordinates; unset = the object's bounds center.  A point is one value.
  Point centerpoint = 6 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  bool objects_only = 7;
  bool snapshot = 8;
  // The captured contents as an embedded subtree, in object-local coordinates.  One picture.
  Subtree snapshot_contents = 9 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
}
enum LensType { LENS_TYPE_UNSPECIFIED = 0; TRANSPARENCY = 1;   // LENS_TYPE_ prefix in fill.proto
  MAGNIFY = 2; INVERT = 3;
  LIGHTEN = 4; DARKEN = 5; MONOCHROME = 6; }

message PatternFill {
  ColorRef color = 1 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  PatternBitmap bitmap = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];  // stroke.proto
  bool overprint = 3;
}

message TexturedFill {
  Texture texture = 1;
  ColorRef color = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  bool overprint = 3;
}
enum Texture { TEXTURE_UNSPECIFIED = 0; BURLAP = 1;   // TEXTURE_ prefix in fill.proto
  DENIM = 2; GRAVEL = 3; MARBLE = 4;
  MESH = 5; OAK = 6; SAND = 7; STUCCO = 8; }

message TiledFill {
  // The tile artwork, embedded.  Not a node reference: the tile is a private copy that
  // is edited by Copy Out / Paste In, and a symbol would clutter the Library and let an edit
  // elsewhere silently change fills.  One picture, so ATOMIC.
  Subtree tile = 1 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  double angle = 2;                    // degrees
  double scale_x = 3;                  // percent; unset reads 100
  double scale_y = 4;
  // Shift of the pattern in object-local points; dragged as one value by the Transform
  // panel's Fills-off move.
  Point offset = 5 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  bool overprint = 6;
}

// A detached copy of nodes, used for tiles and lens snapshots.  Node ids are stripped;
// `parent` indexes into `nodes` (0-based) or is -1 for a root.  Bounded so a change stays
// within the 4 MiB limit.
message Subtree {
  repeated SubtreeNode nodes = 1 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC,
                                  (buf.validate.field).repeated.max_items = 20000];
}
message SubtreeNode {
  int32 parent = 1;
  // An encoded NodeProps.  Bytes, not the message: NodeProps imports every kind, including the
  // appearance stack that imports fill.proto, and protobuf forbids import cycles.
  bytes props = 2 [(buf.validate.field).bytes.max_len = 2097152];
}

=== Merge semantics

Scalar options::
Every scalar in `CustomFill`, `LensFill`, `TexturedFill` and `TiledFill` is its own register;
concurrent edits of different options both apply, same option later wins.  `centerpoint`,
`offset`, `bitmap`, `tile` and `snapshot_contents` are ATOMIC.

Lens type change vs. option toggle::
The command that sets `type` also writes `centerpoint_shown`, `objects_only` and `snapshot`
false in the same change.  A concurrent toggle by another user of one of those flags applies
on top (later OpId), which yields the new lens with that option on -- acceptable and visible.

Snapshot::
Taking a snapshot writes `snapshot = true` and `snapshot_contents` in one change.  Two
concurrent snapshots: later wins, both are complete pictures.  A snapshot whose subtree exceeds
20,000 nodes or 2 MiB encoded is refused with an explanation ("too much artwork under this
lens to freeze"), never truncated.

Tile::
Paste In writes `tile` atomically.  Copy Out reads only.  The tile's node props may reference
swatches and symbols by `NodeRef`; those resolve at render time with their own fallbacks, so
deleting a swatch used inside a tile recolors the tile through the cached value like any
other object.

Read-time normalizations::
* Amounts clamp to their ranges; `magnification` below 1 reads 1; `scale_x`/`scale_y` of 0
  read 100.
* A `LensFill` with `snapshot = true` but no `snapshot_contents` renders live.
* A `TiledFill` with no tile renders as None.
* A `Subtree` whose parent indexes form a cycle or point out of range is rendered as far as the
  well-formed prefix; the remainder is ignored.
* A lens fill on a node that may not carry one (an image, a clipping path, live text) renders
  as a Basic fill of `color` at `amount` percent tint; the command layer never writes such a
  state.
* An unknown `CustomFillPattern` or `Texture` renders as a Basic fill of `color`.

=== Offline behavior

Nothing beyond the general rules.  Lens snapshots and tiles are ordinary registers; no blob
upload is involved.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

Rendering (`WTRender`)::
* Basic: fill with the path's rule.
* Custom and Textured: procedural generators produce a display list or a `CGPattern` per
  pattern at fixed page size, clipped to the path.  Grass and Leaves place marks from a PCG
  generator seeded with `seed`.  Noise patterns use a hashed page-coordinate noise so they are
  stable across redraws and zoom.
* Pattern: as for strokes (ATTR-014), clipped to the path.
* Lens: the display list is rendered in two passes.  Objects below the lens object (in the
  same canvas, by stacking order) are drawn into an offscreen backdrop for the lens's bounds;
  the lens then composites: Transparency = color at `amount` alpha over backdrop; Magnify =
  backdrop sampled scaled about `centerpoint`; Invert = per-channel CMYK complement; Lighten and
  Darken = white or black at `amount` alpha; Monochrome = backdrop luminance mapped to tints of
  `color`.  `objects_only` masks the effect to the union of the objects' painted coverage.  A
  Snapshot renders `snapshot_contents` instead of the backdrop.  Nested lenses recurse with a
  depth cap of 8, beyond which the inner lens renders as Basic.
* Tiled: the tile subtree's display list is rendered into a `CGPattern` cell sized to the
  tile's bounds × scale, rotated by `angle`, phased by `offset`, in object-local space so the
  fill follows the object's transform when *Fills* is on.

Hit testing:: Lens and Custom transparent fills hit anywhere inside the path.

Object panel editors:: SwiftUI forms per `FillKind`; the custom-fill form shows only the
options the chosen pattern uses (table above); lens form with sliders and the three checkboxes;
tiled form with preview, btn:[Paste In], btn:[Copy Out], an angle dial (`NSSlider` circular),
scale and offset fields.  Paste In validates the pasteboard contents against the exclusions
and explains refusals.

Canvas:: The lens centerpoint handle is drawn by the Pointer tool's overlay when a selected
object has a lens fill with `centerpoint_shown`; dragging writes `centerpoint`, kbd:[Shift]-click
clears it.

Undo:: Paste In, Copy Out (no change), Snapshot and every option edit are single steps.

NOTE: As built by ATTR-018 (`WTRender/ProceduralFills.swift`, `PaintDrawing.swift`).  Bricks,
Circles, Hatch (one pattern per set of lines) and Squares are `CGPattern`s in canvas space
rotated by their angle.  Zero lengths read as defaults: Bricks 16 × 8 with mortar 12% of the
smaller side (at least 0.5 pt), Circles radius 3 at spacing 10, Hatch spacing 6 and width 1,
Squares side 5 at spacing 10; a Squares outline width of 0 fills the squares.  Tiger Teeth covers
the object's bounds, rotated by the angle, in the background colour with `count` teeth hanging
three quarters of the way down.  Grass and Leaves place `count` marks over the object's local
bounds from PCG32 seeded with `seed` (five draws per mark), in fixed greens and browns, as the
table gives them no colour.  The noise fills sample hashed canvas-space cells of 0.5 pt (Top
Noise marks 30% of them), and the eight textures are procedural lightness fields over
canvas-space value noise (weave, twill, pebbles, veins, grid, grain, grit and blotches) shading
the colour from 55% to a third of the way to white.  Sampled paints are evaluated on a grid
anchored at their space's origin at the device resolution rounded to a power of two (¼ to 16
samples per point; 4 per point in PDF, where they are images rather than vector) over the part
of the clip that needs them, so tiles and repeated renders sample the same points.

NOTE: As built by ATTR-019 (`WTRender/LensRendering.swift`).  The backdrop is everything drawn
before the lens object in its canvas (earlier siblings keep their groups' clips and opacity; the
lens object's own lower attributes are not beneath it), rendered by Core Graphics over white
paper at the device pixels of the lens's clip, or at 4 pixels per point in PDF.  Invert takes the
complement in the naive CMY space, `1 − r, 1 − g, 1 − b`: the four-channel CMYK complement maps
white and black both to black, which is not a negative.  Monochrome uses the Rec. 709
luminance of the encoded sRGB colour.  Objects only masks the result with the alpha of the
backdrop rendered without paper.  Magnify re-renders the backdrop through the magnification
about the centerpoint, so it stays sharp.  A lens inside a tile, a brush symbol or a snapshot has
no backdrop and renders as Basic, as does one nested deeper than 8; each of those logs and counts
(`LensRendering.depthCapCount`).  Snapshot contents and tiles arrive as display items in
object-local space; WTModel decodes the `Subtree`.  A change repaints the whole bounds of every
lens its dirty rectangles touch, transitively up a stack of lenses (`InvalidationMapper`,
`DisplayList.lensIndices`), since a magnified lens shows what changed elsewhere in its bounds.

NOTE: As built by ATTR-021 (`WTRender/PaintDrawing.swift`).  The pattern cell is the tile's
geometric bounds -- its paths' control bounds, strokes not included -- so a stroke overhanging
the tile is clipped at the cell edge.  An empty tile, or one without area, renders as None.  A
1,000-node tile over a Letter page draws in 5--8 ms in a release build (40 ms in debug); the
16 ms budget is enforced in release builds.

NOTE: As built by ATTR-013 and ATTR-017 (`WTApp/Inspectors/FillEditors.swift`, `PatternEditor.swift`).
The pattern grid is an `NSView` (`PatternGridView`) with per-pixel hit testing; a press toggles its
pixel, a drag paints that value, and the picture is written at mouse-up as one change, with the
drag's own picture shown meanwhile over whatever arrives.  The palette shows eight presets at a time,
scrolled by its slider.  The Custom fill form shows the options of the chosen pattern (the table
above); numbers clamp to their ranges.  Lens sliders are one undo step per drag.  *Gradient* is
listed in the *Fill type* pop-up but chosen and edited by the gradient ramp editor (ATTR-025, on
ATTR-024's commands); choosing it here shows a note.  *Snapshot* writes the flag only (turning it off
also clears `snapshot_contents`): capturing the lens's backdrop into `snapshot_contents` is
ATTR-020's Snapshot command, and a snapshot without contents renders live as specified.  The Tiled
fill's btn:[Paste In] and btn:[Copy Out] are built here on WTModel's `Subtrees.tile` and
`Subtrees.payload` (the capture and expansion half of ATTR-020): the objects pasteboard's nodes with
their props, parents before children, moved so the artwork starts at the origin; a node WTModel does
not know as a path, shape or group (images, placed files, text), or one with a tiled or lens fill,
is refused with the message, as is more than 20,000 nodes.  The angle dial is a circular
`NSSlider` writing on mouse-up; the preview draws the tiled fill itself.
