A master page is a page layout that other pages inherit: its size, orientation and bleed, plus any
objects placed on it -- running heads, folios, frames, a logo.  Pages that follow a master are its
*child pages*.  Editing the master updates every child at once.  A document can have as many
master pages as it needs, and master pages are managed alongside symbols in the Library panel
(xref:library.adoc[Symbols and the Library]).

Master pages differ from templates: a template starts a *new document* with a set of defaults
(xref:templates.adoc[Templates and starting points]); a master page applies a layout to pages
*inside* a document.

== How master pages behave

* A child page takes the master's size, orientation and bleed and cannot change them itself.
* The master's objects appear on each child page beneath the page's own objects, layer by layer:
  what the master has on the *Foreground* layer sits at the bottom of the child's *Foreground*
  layer, and so on.  You cannot select or edit master objects on a child page; open the master to
  change them.
* Objects that run past the master's edge into its bleed are printed on child pages up to the
  bleed rectangle.
* Guides on a master page are shown on its children, and can be moved or removed only on the
  master (xref:grid-guides.adoc[Grid and guides]).
* Master pages appear in the Library panel with a page icon.  Choose *Show Master Pages* from the
  Library panel's Options menu to show or hide them there.  They can be renamed, duplicated,
  grouped and deleted like symbols, and exported to and imported from other documents
  (xref:library.adoc[Symbols and the Library]).

== Creating a master page

To create an empty master page:

. Do one of the following:
* In the Document panel, open the Options menu and choose *New Master Page*.
* In the Library panel, open the Options menu and choose *New Master Page*.
. The master page opens in a new tab of the document window, titled with its name (*Master 1*,
  *Master 2*, …).  It starts with the size, orientation and bleed of the active page -- or of the
  active page's master, if it has one.
. Place text and graphics on it.
. Close the tab, or click the document's tab, to return to the pages.  Nothing needs saving.

To convert an existing page into a master page:

. Select the page (xref:pages.adoc[Working with pages]).  If it is a child of another master,
  release it first (below).
. In the Document panel's Options menu, choose *Convert to Master Page*.

The page's objects move onto the new master, and the page becomes the master's child, so it looks
the same as before; other pages can now follow the same layout.

== Applying a master page to pages

To make pages children of a master:

. Select one or more pages with the Page tool.
. Do one of the following:
* In the Document panel, choose the master from the *Master Page* pop-up.
* Drag the master's icon from the Library panel and drop it on a selected page.

Only the selected pages become children.  Choosing *None* in the pop-up detaches the selected
pages: they keep their size but lose the master's objects (this is different from releasing,
below, which keeps copies of the objects).

To add new child pages:

* In the *Add Pages* sheet, select *Make child of master page* and choose the master.
* Or select an existing child page and choose *Duplicate* from the Document panel's Options menu.
* Or, with the Page tool, kbd:[Option]-drag a child page to an empty part of the pasteboard.

== Editing a master page

To edit a master page:

. Do one of the following:
* Double-click the master's icon in the Library panel.
* Select a child page and click btn:[Edit] next to the *Master Page* pop-up in the Document panel.
. The master opens in its tab.  Edit it as you would any page: its size, orientation and bleed
  in the Document panel, its objects with the ordinary tools.
. Close the tab or switch back to the document tab.  Child pages update as you work.

To rename a master page, double-click its name in the Library panel.  To delete one, select it in
the Library panel and choose *Delete* from the Options menu; its children become ordinary pages of
the same size.

== Releasing a child page

Releasing turns a child page back into an ordinary page while keeping what it inherited.

To release child pages:

. Select one or more child pages with the Page tool.
. In the Document panel's Options menu, choose *Release Child Page*.

The master's objects are copied onto each released page as one group per layer, so the page looks
unchanged but is now independent: it can be resized, and the copied objects can be edited.  Later
changes to the master no longer affect it.

== Importing and exporting master pages

Master pages travel with symbols: *Import…* and *Export…* in the Library panel's Options menu list
master pages next to symbols, and an exported master page carries its objects and settings
(xref:library.adoc[Symbols and the Library]).  Copying a page between documents with a master
also brings the master along.

== Working with others

Several people can edit a master page at the same time, just as they can edit a page: each sees
the other's objects arrive, and every child page in every window updates live.  Opening a master
page marks you as present on it, so others can see who is working on the layout.

Collisions worth knowing about:

* *One person releases a child page while another is editing the master.*  The released page
  gets a copy of the master *as the releaser saw it*; the master edits made at the same moment are
  not in that copy.  {product} tells the releaser ("Priya changed Master A while you released
  page 4") and offers *Update the copies*, which replaces the copied objects with the current
  master's.  The offer is repeated in the review sheet after offline work.
* *One person deletes a master while another assigns it to pages.*  The pages simply behave as
  ordinary pages; the review sheet lists the deleted master with *Restore*.
* *Two people resize the master.*  As with any page, the later size stands and the other person
  is offered *Reapply mine*.
* *Someone converts a page to a master while another person draws on that page.*  The new
  objects land on the child page, not on the master, and the page still looks right.

== Specification

=== Data model

`MasterPageProps` and `PageProps.master` are on xref:document-panel.adoc[The Document panel].  A
master's objects are ordinary objects under `layers` with `CommonProps.canvas` set to the master's
node id (xref:crdt-model.adoc[CRDT model]); coordinates on a master canvas are relative to the
master's top-left corner (the master has no pasteboard position).  A master's guides are its own
`guides` sequence.

Commands:

New Master Page:: `CreateNode` under `masters` (0:3) with `geometry` and `bleed` from the active
page or its master; name `Master N` where N is one more than the largest existing.
Convert to Master Page:: One change: `CreateNode(master)`, then for every top-level object on the
page a `SetFields(canvas = master, transform translated by -page.origin)`, then
`SetFields(page.master = master)`.
Apply:: `SetFields(page.master)` per selected page.  The page's `geometry` and `bleed` registers are
left as they were; they are *masked* while `master` is set (below).
None:: `SetFields(page.master = unset)`.  The page's own `geometry` register reappears; the command
first writes the master's geometry into it so nothing jumps.
Release:: One change: `SetFields(page.master = unset)` and `SetFields(page.geometry, bleed)` from
the master, then for each layer that has master objects a `CreateNode(group)` on that layer at the
bottom of the layer's stacking order with `canvas` unset, containing deep copies of the master's
objects on that layer, translated by `+page.origin`.  The change's label is "Release page N from
<master>" and it records `released_from = master id` in `Change.label` metadata for the review
sheet (the label is free text; `WTSync` parses a trailing `[master:<id>]` tag).
Delete master:: `SetDeleted(master, true)`.  Children keep their `master` reference; it dangles and
reads unset.  The objects on the master canvas stay with the master.

Effective page geometry:: `page.master` set and resolving → the master's `geometry` and `bleed`;
otherwise the page's own.  This is a read rule, not a write: a child's own registers are never
touched by master edits, so releasing many children is cheap and a master edit is one small
change.

=== Merge semantics

Master edit vs. child release::
Release copies the master's objects as of the releaser's state; concurrent master edits apply to
the master's nodes, not to the copies.  Mechanically both apply and the result is consistent -- a
released page holding a slightly stale copy.  `WTSync` treats a release change as *touching* the
master's subtree (it reads the `[master:<id>]` tag), so any concurrent write to a node on that
master's canvas is an overlap of kind `released stale master`.  The review sheet lists it under the
page with *Update the copies* (delete the copied groups and re-copy from the current master, one
change) or *Keep my copies*.  Live, the same choice is a notice.

Delete master vs. apply master::
`page.master` points at a deleted node; `REF_FALLBACK_UNSET` makes the page ordinary, showing its
own `geometry` (which the apply command left untouched, so it is the pre-apply size).  Review:
edit-vs-delete on the master with *Restore*.

Two people edit the master's geometry::
ATOMIC later-wins, listed as same-register loss; all children follow the winner.

Convert to master vs. draw on the page::
The converter's change moves the objects it saw; a concurrently created object stays on the page
(canvas unset) and is drawn above the master content.  No review entry: nothing was lost.

Release vs. release of the same page::
Both changes create copy groups.  Normalize on read: nothing to normalize mechanically (two sets of
copies), so `WTSync` detects `page.master` written by both sides and the second set of groups is
listed as "duplicate release" with *Remove duplicates* (deletes the groups from the later change).

Master deleted while someone edits an object on its canvas::
Ordinary edit-vs-delete on the master node's canvas objects; *Restore* on the master.

Read-time normalizations:

* `page.master` dangling → page reads as ordinary, with its own registers.
* A `page.master` that points at a *page* node (wrong kind, only possible through a malformed op)
  reads unset.
* An object whose `canvas` names a deleted master is not rendered anywhere (it is the master's
  content), but is restorable with the master.

=== Offline behavior

Standard.  A master page opened offline is editable; children update locally.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client
