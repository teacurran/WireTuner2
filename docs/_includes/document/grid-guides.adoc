The grid and guides are non-printing alignment aids.  The grid is a regular lattice of dots
behind the whole pasteboard; guides are horizontal and vertical lines you place on pages.  Objects
can snap to either, and to the points of other objects.  Grid spacing and the guides themselves
are part of the document and shared; whether *you* see them, and whether *your* dragging snaps,
are personal settings.  Guides that appear on their own while you drag -- smart guides -- are a
different thing, described with xref:moving.adoc#smart-guides[Moving objects].

== Colors

The grid and guides have their own colors so they stand out from artwork.  To change them, choose
menu:{product}[Settings… > Colors] (kbd:[Cmd+,]) and click the *Guide color* or *Grid color*
well.  Guides are blue and the grid is light gray by default.  The colors are your preference and
do not affect anyone else's view.  Smart guides have their own well, *Smart guide color*
(magenta), so the two kinds of guide are never confused.

== The grid

The grid is drawn as dots at every intersection of its horizontal and vertical lines, spaced by
the *grid size*.  With snapping on, dragged, drawn and resized objects settle onto the grid.

To show or hide the grid::
Choose menu:View[Grid > Show].  A check mark means the grid is visible.

To turn grid snapping on or off::
Choose menu:View[Grid > Snap to Grid].  A check mark means snapping is on.  Snapping works whether
or not the grid is visible.

To set the grid size and snapping style:

. Choose menu:View[Grid > Edit…].
. Type the *Grid size* in the document's units.
. Choose how objects snap:
* *Relative grid* selected: an object keeps its offset within a grid cell, so an object that
  started 3 points right of a grid line lands 3 points right of a grid line.  Useful for moving
  things by whole cells.
* *Relative grid* off: the object's snapping point lands exactly on a grid intersection.
. Click btn:[OK].

The grid size and the *Relative grid* choice are document settings; showing and snapping are
yours.

== Guides

Guides are lines on a page.  They can be dragged out of the rulers, placed precisely with the
*Guides* sheet, moved, locked, and turned into ordinary paths.  Guides exist only on pages -- a
guide dragged onto the pasteboard is removed -- and a guide on a master page shows on the master's
children but is edited only on the master (xref:master-pages.adoc[Master pages]).

To show or hide guides, do one of the following:

* Choose menu:View[Guides > Show].
* Click the visibility check mark beside the *Guides* layer in the Layers panel
  (xref:layers.adoc[Layers]).

=== Adding guides by dragging

. If the rulers are hidden, choose menu:View[Page Rulers > Show].
. Drag from the top ruler onto a page for a horizontal guide, or from the left ruler for a
  vertical one.  The pointer shows the guide's position in the document's units while you drag.
. Release over the page.  Releasing over the pasteboard cancels.

Hold kbd:[Option] while dragging from a ruler to add a guide on every page the pointer crosses at
the same position relative to each page.  Hold kbd:[Shift] to snap the guide to the grid.

By default the window does not scroll when you drag a guide past its edge.  To make it scroll,
turn on *Dragging a guide scrolls the window* in menu:{product}[Settings… > General].

=== Moving and removing guides

* To move a guide, drag it with the Pointer tool.  Guides must be unlocked (below).  The position
  is shown while dragging; kbd:[Shift] snaps it to the grid.
* To remove a guide, drag it off the page onto the pasteboard, or select it and press
  kbd:[Delete].
* To move a guide precisely, double-click it and type the position in the *Guides* sheet.

=== Adding guides precisely

. Choose menu:View[Guides > Edit…], or double-click an existing guide.  The *Guides* sheet lists
  the guides on the active page with their axis and position.
. Click btn:[Add].
. Choose *Horizontal* or *Vertical*.
. Choose how to place them:
* *Count*: a number of guides evenly spaced between a first and last position.
* *Increment*: guides every so many units between a first and last position.
. Type the *First* and *Last* positions, measured from the page's zero point.
. Set the *Pages* range the guides are added to (the active page by default).
. Click btn:[Add]; repeat for more; then click btn:[OK].

=== Editing, releasing and removing guides in the sheet

. Choose menu:View[Guides > Edit…], or double-click a guide.
. Select one or more guides in the list: kbd:[Cmd]-click for several, kbd:[Shift]-click for a
  range.
. Do one of the following:
* Click btn:[Edit] and type a new position.
* Click btn:[Release] to turn the guides into ordinary paths on the current layer.  The path is a
  line across the page that you can style or edit like any other.
* Click btn:[Delete] to remove them.
. Click btn:[OK].

To turn a path into a guide, move it to the *Guides* layer in the Layers panel
(xref:layers.adoc[Layers]).  Such guide objects keep their shape -- a circle on the Guides layer
is a circular guide -- and objects snap to their paths.

=== Snapping to guides

Choose menu:View[Guides > Snap to Guides] to turn snapping on or off; it is on by default.  As
you drag an object near a guide, the pointer shows a small triangle -- horizontal or vertical --
telling you the object will land on the guide if you release.  How close counts as "near" is the
*Snap distance* preference (xref:selecting.adoc[Selecting objects]).

=== Locking guides

Locked guides cannot be dragged or deleted from the canvas; they can still be edited in the
*Guides* sheet.  Locking is part of the document, so it protects the guides for everyone.

* Choose menu:View[Guides > Lock] (a check mark means locked), or
* click the padlock beside the *Guides* layer in the Layers panel.

Guides are unlocked by default.

== Snapping to points and objects

menu:View[Snap to Point] snaps the point you are dragging to anchor points and handles of other
objects; menu:View[Snap to Object] snaps to the nearest point along their paths.  Both are
personal settings and combine with grid and guide snapping.  When several snaps compete within
the snap distance, a point wins over a path, a path over a guide, a guide over a smart guide, and
a smart guide over the grid (xref:moving.adoc#smart-guides[Smart guides]).  Hold kbd:[Control]
while dragging to suspend all snapping for that drag.

When you rotate the canvas (xref:document-view.adoc[Setting the document view]), the grid,
the guides and the rulers turn with the pages: a horizontal guide stays parallel to the top of
its page, whichever way the page is facing on screen.

== Working with others

Guides, the grid size and the guide lock are part of the document: a guide someone adds appears
on your page as they drop it, and a change to the grid size re-spaces the dots in every window.
Showing, hiding and snapping are your own; turning the grid on does not turn it on for anyone
else.

If two people drag the same guide, the later position stands.  If one person removes a guide
another is dragging, the guide is removed; the dragger sees it vanish and, after offline work, the
review sheet offers to restore it.  Guides added by two people at once are all kept.  If one
person locks the guides while another is mid-drag, the drag finishes but nothing further can be
moved until unlocked.

== Specification

=== Data model

`Guide`, `PageProps.guides`, `MasterPageProps.guides`, `GridSettings` and
`SettingsProps.guides_locked` are on xref:document-panel.adoc[The Document panel].

Guide positions are stored in points from the page's top-left corner (`axis = GUIDE_AXIS_HORIZONTAL` →
`position` is a y offset, `VERTICAL` → an x offset), *not* from the zero point, so moving the zero
point does not move guides.  The Guides sheet converts to zero-point coordinates for display.

Guide objects (paths on the Guides layer) are ordinary objects owned by the LIB epic's layer model;
this page only consumes them for snapping.

Local view state (`view` table): show grid, snap to grid, show guides, snap to guides, snap to
point, snap to object.  Preferences: *Guide color*, *Grid color*, *Dragging a guide scrolls the
window*, *Snap distance* (owned by the selecting page).

Why `guides_locked` is shared:: The lock exists to keep a layout from being disturbed; a lock that
only bound the person who set it would not do that.  Show and snap toggles are about how one
person works and are local.

=== Merge semantics

`guides` (SEQUENCE)::
* Add on both sides: both guides exist.  Adding "the same" guide (same axis and position) twice
  yields two coincident guides; normalize on read: coincident guides on one page render and hit
  as one, and the Guides sheet shows one row that edits both.
* Move vs. move of one guide: `position` is a register, later wins; listed as same-register loss.
* Delete vs. move: the element's `deleted` register wins; the review sheet lists edit-vs-delete
  with *Restore*.
* Release vs. move: release deletes the element and creates a path in one change; concurrent
  move applies to the deleted element.  Review lists edit-vs-delete; *Restore* brings the guide
  back beside the released path.
* Guides added on a page that was removed concurrently: they stay on the (deleted) page, restored
  with it.

`grid.size`, `grid.relative`:: Independent registers; later wins each.

`guides_locked`:: Register, later wins.  A guide `position` write from a replica that had the lock
off, concurrent with a lock, still applies: the lock is a UI restraint, not a merge rule.

Read-time normalizations:

* A guide whose position lies outside the page (after a resize) is kept and drawn clipped to the
  bleed rectangle; it is listed in the sheet.
* A guide with `GUIDE_AXIS_UNSPECIFIED` reads as horizontal.

=== Offline behavior

Standard.  Guide drags coalesce.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

* Guide objects snap as `SnapCandidate.guideObject(Contour)`: along the path, at guide priority.

* Grid rendering (`WTRender`): dots at intersections, drawn as a tiled pattern layer below the
  pages' content and above the page background; skipped when dots would be closer than 4 px on
  screen (subdivision hidden, every k-th line kept so the visible lattice is at least 8 px).
* Snapping (`WTGeometry`, GEO-005): candidates from grid (relative or absolute), page guides of
  every page within the snap distance, guide objects (nearest point on path), object points, and
  smart guides from OBJ-038; priority point > path > guide > smart guide > grid; kbd:[Control]
  suspends.  The snap result carries which candidate won so the pointer can show the triangle,
  point badge or smart-guide line.
* Grid dots and guide hairlines are pasteboard-space geometry drawn through the view transform,
  so canvas rotation (BASIC-034) rotates them with the pages at no extra cost; the grid's
  zoom-dependent thinning uses the view scale only, never the angle.
* Guides in the canvas (`WTApp`): drawn in the guide color as hairlines across the page (clipped to
  the bleed rectangle); hit-tested with the snap distance; dragging a guide is a temporary tool
  that writes `position` on mouse-up (coalesced), deletion by drop on the pasteboard.  Ruler drag
  creates the element on mouse-up.  kbd:[Option] from a ruler creates elements on each page whose
  rectangle the pointer crossed at the same relative offset.
* Guides sheet: SwiftUI table over the active page's `guides`; *Add* computes positions from
  count/increment and writes one `ElementInsert` per page in the range in one change; *Release*
  creates a `PathNode` line spanning the page (bleed to bleed) with the default guide stroke of
  none and a hairline preview, on the current layer.
* Menu commands in the View menu from the command registry (APP-005): Grid > Show / Snap to Grid /
  Edit…; Guides > Show / Snap to Guides / Lock / Edit…; Snap to Point; Snap to Object.
* Undo: each guide add/move/delete is one change; sheet operations are one change per button
  press.

NOTE: As built by DOC-016 (`WTRender/Pages/GridRendering.swift`, `SnapSources.swift`).  Grid:
`GridSpec(size:origin:relative:)` and `GridRendering.item(_:in:zoom:color:)`, one fill of
one-device-pixel squares at the intersections inside an area; `stride(size:zoom:)` is 1 while dots
are 4 px or more apart, else the smallest k putting every k-th line 8 px apart; an area holding
more than 250,000 dots draws nothing.  The grid's origin is the active page's zero point
