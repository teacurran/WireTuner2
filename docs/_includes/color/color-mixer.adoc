The Color Mixer is where colors are made.  Pick a mode, move the sliders or type numbers, watch
the color well, and when it is right either apply it directly -- as an unnamed color -- or add
it to the Swatches panel as a named color.  It is also the place to inspect a color: drop any
swatch or object color on it and the sliders show the components.  Show or hide the panel with
menu:Window[Color Mixer].

== The panel

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Element |Purpose

|Mode buttons
|*CMYK*, *RGB*, *P3*, *OKLCH*, *HLS*, *Grayscale* and *System*.  The first six change the
sliders; *System* opens the macOS Colors panel.  *RGB* is sRGB; *P3* is Display P3, the wider
space of every Mac display since 2016 (xref:spot-process.adoc[Spot and process colors]).

|Sliders and fields
|One per component.  Drag a slider or type a value and press kbd:[Return].  kbd:[Up] and
kbd:[Down] in a field step by one; with kbd:[Shift], by ten.  Each slider's track shows the
colors that component would give at the current mix; in *P3* and *OKLCH* the part of a track
that no display can show is hatched.

|Color field (RGB, P3 and OKLCH modes)
|A six-digit hex triplet, with or without `#`, is an sRGB color; three digits are expanded
(`f03` → `ff0033`).  The field also takes the forms style sheets use: `p3(0.9 0.22 0.27)` or
`color(display-p3 0.9 0.22 0.27)` for Display P3, `oklch(62% 0.22 25)` for OKLCH, `lab(54 63
32)` for Lab.  Typing a form from another space switches the Mixer to that space.  The field
always shows the current color in the current mode's form, so you can copy it into CSS.

|Gamut indicator
|Under the color well: *sRGB* when the color can be shown on any screen, *P3* when it needs a
wide-gamut display, *Out of gamut* when even Display P3 cannot show it (possible in OKLCH,
where the sliders reach beyond every screen).  When the display this window is on cannot show
the color, the indicator adds *clipped on this display*: the well and the canvas show the
nearest color your screen can make, while the color itself is exactly what you mixed and
shows fully on a screen that can.

|btn:[Web Safe] (RGB mode)
|Snaps the current color to the nearest of the 216 browser-safe colors.

|Color well
|Split in two: the *original* color on the left (the one you started from -- the selection's
color, or the swatch you dropped), the *new* color on the right.  Drag from either half to an
object, a panel, or the Swatches panel.  Clicking the left half copies the original back to
the right.

|btn:[Add to Swatches]
|Names the new color and adds it to the color list.  kbd:[Cmd]-click skips the naming sheet.

|btn:[Apply]
|Applies the new color to the selected objects' fill, stroke or both, according to the
Swatches panel's Fill / Stroke / Both selector.
|===

Prefer a single, larger well?  Turn off the *Color Mixer and Tints use a split color well*
preference (xref:preferences.adoc[Preferences]); the well then shows only the new color.

== Mixing a color

To mix a CMYK, RGB or Display P3 color:

. Click *CMYK*, *RGB* or *P3*.
. Drag the sliders or type values.  CMYK takes percentages, 0--100; RGB and P3 take 0--255, or
  a value in the color field.  The same numbers mean a more vivid color in P3 than in RGB:
  P3 255/0/0 is a red that sRGB cannot reach.
. Watch the right half of the color well and the gamut indicator.
. Apply the color (drag it, or click btn:[Apply]) or add it to the Swatches panel.

The Mixer opens in *RGB* or *P3* according to the *Default color space for new colors*
preference (xref:preferences.adoc[Preferences]); Display P3 unless you change it.

To mix a color by lightness, chroma and hue:

. Click *OKLCH*.  Three sliders: *L* (lightness, 0--100%), *C* (chroma, 0 for gray up to the
  most colorful your screens can show) and *H* (hue, 0°--360°, red near 25°, yellow near
  100°, green near 145°, blue near 265°).
. Set the lightness first, then the hue, then push the chroma up until the color is as vivid
  as you want.  Where a slider's track turns hatched the color leaves what Display P3 can
  show; the gamut indicator says *Out of gamut*.
. Apply or add.  OKLCH colors are stored as OKLab and the Swatches panel shows them with the
  *OKLab* badge.

OKLCH does what HLS promises: equal moves of a slider look like equal changes, and changing
the hue or the chroma leaves the color exactly as light as it was, whereas in HLS a yellow
and a blue at the same lightness number look nothing alike in brightness.  Use it to build a
palette of colors that belong together -- one lightness, several hues -- or a ramp of one hue.

To mix a color by hue, lightness and saturation:

. Click *HLS*.  The sliders are replaced by a color wheel and a lightness slider.
. Set the hue by dragging the point around the wheel, or type 0°--360° in the *H* field.  Red
  is 0°, then orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue and magenta around the circle.
. Set the lightness with the vertical slider or the *L* field: 0% is black, 100% is white.
. Set the saturation by dragging the point toward the centre (grayer) or the rim (purer), or
  type 0--100% in the *S* field.
. Switch to *RGB* or *P3* at any time to see the same color as red, green and blue.  HLS
  colors are RGB colors in your default color space; the Swatches panel shows them with that
  space's badge.

To mix a gray:

. Click *Grayscale*.
. Set the single slider, 0% (white) to 100% (black).  The result is a CMYK color with only
  black in it, so it separates to the black plate alone.

To use the macOS Colors panel:

. Click *System*.  The Colors panel opens beside the Mixer.
. Pick with any of its tabs -- wheel, sliders, palettes, image, pencils -- or with its
  eyedropper anywhere on the screen.
. The Mixer follows the panel as you pick.  On a Mac with a Display P3 screen the panel picks
  P3 colors and the color is stored as Display P3; if your default color space is sRGB, or
  the screen is not P3, it is stored as sRGB -- pulled into sRGB if it has to be, which the
  gamut indicator shows.

== Inspecting a color

To load an existing color into the Mixer, do one of the following:

* kbd:[Option]-click a swatch in the Swatches panel.
* Drag a swatch, an Object panel color well, or an Eyedropper pick onto the Mixer's color
  well.
* Select an object: the Mixer shows the color of its topmost fill (or stroke, if the Swatches
  panel's *Stroke* selector is pressed).

The Mixer switches to the color's own space and shows its components; a Lab color, which has
no mode of its own, is shown in OKLCH and becomes an OKLab color if you change it.  Adjusting
components edits a *copy*: the object or swatch does not change until you apply or add.  To redefine the
swatch itself, drag the new color back onto it, or use the Swatches panel
(xref:editing-colors.adoc[Editing colors]).

== Working with others

The Mixer is your own workbench: nothing you do in it is visible to anyone until you apply
the color to an object or add it to the Swatches panel.  Your sliders are not shared, and a
collaborator's Mixer is not affected by yours.  If you loaded a swatch into the Mixer and
someone else then edits that swatch, the *original* half of the well updates to their new
color so you can see what you are departing from; your *new* half is untouched.

The gamut indicator's *clipped on this display* note is about your screen, not the document.
A collaborator on an sRGB monitor sees the same Display P3 color clipped and the same note;
the document holds one value for both of you, and the color prints and exports in full.

== Specification

=== Data model

None.  The Mixer's state -- mode, current and original colors -- is view state on this Mac
(`local_only` in the BASIC epic's view message, so it survives relaunch).  Applying writes a
`ColorRef.inline` (xref:applying-color.adoc[Applying color]); adding writes a swatch node
(xref:swatches.adoc[Swatches]).

=== Merge semantics

No document fields of its own.  btn:[Apply] to N objects is one change of N `ColorRef`
writes, each an ordinary ATOMIC LWW.  A slider drag bound to a selection (the Mixer in
"live" mode, when the *Apply as you mix* toggle in the panel's menu is on) writes coalescing
changes: one per drag, undone as one.  When a swatch loaded into the Mixer is changed
remotely, the Mixer re-reads the original from the model; the working copy is local.

=== Offline behavior

Nothing here needs the network.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

* `WTApp`: SwiftUI panel body.  Sliders are custom (gradient-backed tracks showing the
  component's range at the current mix; the P3 and OKLCH tracks are drawn in a
  Display P3 context and hatch the out-of-P3 run, found by sampling the track at 64 points
  through COLOR-002's in-gamut test).  The HLS wheel is a custom view with a lightness
  slider.  *System* opens `NSColorPanel` in continuous mode and observes `changeColor(_:)`;
  the Mixer takes the `NSColor` through `usingColorSpace(.displayP3)` and stores it as
  Display P3, or gamut-maps it to sRGB (COLOR-024) when the default space is sRGB.  The
  gamut indicator combines COLOR-002's in-gamut tests (sRGB, P3) with COLOR-024's display
  query for the *clipped on this display* note; the well and the canvas simply draw the
  tagged `CGColor` and let the window server clip.  The color field is one `NSTextField`
  whose parser is COLOR-002's CSS Color 4 parser; a parse that yields another space switches
  the mode.  The `ColorRef` pasteboard type (`com.villagecompute.wiretuner.colorref`: the
  encoded `ColorRef` plus its resolved `Color` and name) and the split-well / drag-source
  views are defined here and reused by the Tints panel, the Swatches panel and the Object
  panel.  The `Color` in the pasteboard carries its space; the `NSColor` written for other
  applications is in Display P3 when the color is, else sRGB.
* The *Default color space for new colors* preference (Colors, synced; sRGB or Display P3,
  default Display P3) is read here for the opening mode, HLS results, System results and
  pasted `NSColor`s; the Eyedropper reads it for bitmap picks
  (xref:applying-color.adoc[Applying color]).
* Color arithmetic from COLOR-002; gamut mapping and the display query from COLOR-024.
* Undo grouping: nothing in the Mixer is undoable until applied; live mode groups per drag.
