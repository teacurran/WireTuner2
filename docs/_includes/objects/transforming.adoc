Transforming is moving, rotating, scaling, skewing and reflecting.  {product} offers three
ways to do each: the transformation tools, for dragging about a point you choose; the
Transform panel, for exact numbers and for making transformed copies; and the transform
handles, which appear around a double-clicked object and let you do all five without
changing tools.  Whichever you use, the object stays what it was -- a rotated rectangle is
still a rectangle with a corner radius you can edit.

Transformations can also be applied as live effects that stay editable

== The transformation tools

The Tools panel holds Rotate, Scale, Skew and Reflect under one slot; press and hold the
slot, or press its key, to choose.

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
|Tool |Key |Does

|Rotate |kbd:[R] |Turns the selection about the point you press.
|Scale |kbd:[S] |Enlarges or reduces the selection about the point you press.
|Skew |kbd:[K] |Slants the selection along an axis through the point you press.
|Reflect |kbd:[F] |Flips the selection across a line through the point you press.
|===

To transform with a tool:

. Choose the tool.
. Press where the center of the transformation should be -- on the object or anywhere
  else.
. Drag.  The farther from the center you drag, the finer the control.  A dashed outline
  previews the result; release to apply.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Key while dragging |Effect

|kbd:[Shift]
|Constrains: rotation and reflection to 45° steps from the *Constrain angle*; scaling to
proportional; skewing to horizontal or vertical.

|kbd:[Option]
|Leaves the original and transforms a copy.  Press before releasing.

|kbd:[Esc]
|Cancels.
|===

Double-clicking a transformation tool opens the Transform panel on that tab.

== The Transform panel

The Transform panel (menu:Window[Transform], kbd:[Cmd+M]) has five tabs -- Move, Rotate,
Scale, Skew, Reflect -- and shows, after any transformation, the values it used.  All but
Move work about a *center*, whose coordinates are shown in the panel's *X* and *Y* center
fields and can be typed.

To transform with the panel:

. Select the objects.
. Open the panel on the tab you want (menu:Modify[Transform > Rotate], and so on, also
  opens it).
. Tick the options that apply:
+
[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Effect

|Contents
|The contents of a clipping path transform with it.  Off, only the clipping shape moves
(xref:clipping-paths.adoc[Clipping paths]).

|Fills
|Gradient and tiled fills transform with the object.  Off, the fill stays put behind the
moving shape.

|Strokes (Scale only)
|Stroke widths scale with the object.  Off, strokes keep their width.  For groups, this is
governed by the group's *Transform as unit* setting instead
(xref:grouping.adoc[Grouping]).
|===
. Enter the values:
+
[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Tab |Fields

|Move
|*X* and *Y* distance; positive is right and up.

|Rotate
|*Angle*: positive turns counter-clockwise.

|Scale
|*X* and *Y* percentages, or one value with *Uniform* ticked.  Values under 100 reduce.

|Skew
|*H* (positive slants right) and *V* (positive slants up), in degrees.

|Reflect
|*Axis* angle: 0° flips across a horizontal line... that is, top-to-bottom; 90° flips
left-to-right; anything between flips across a tilted line.
|===
. Enter *Copies*: `0` transforms the selection itself; a larger number leaves the original
  and makes that many copies, each transformed one step further than the last (three copies
  at 30° give 30°, 60° and 90°).  Reflect makes at most one useful copy.
. Set the center by typing *X* and *Y*, or by clicking on the canvas with a transformation
  tool while the panel is open, or by dragging the center point of the transform handles.
. Click the tab's button (btn:[Rotate], btn:[Scale] ...).

== Transform handles

To transform freely:

. With the Pointer tool, double-click an object or a multiple selection.  Eight handles
  appear around it and a small circle marks the center.  (If nothing appears, turn on
  *Double-click enables transform handles* in menu:WireTuner[Settings… > General].)
. Move the pointer around the selection; it changes to show what a drag will do:
+
[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Pointer over |Drag to

|Inside the selection (not on the center) |Move.
|The center circle |Move the center of rotation, scaling and skewing.  kbd:[Shift]-click the center to put it back.
|A handle |Scale.  Corner handles and kbd:[Shift] scale proportionally.
|Just outside a handle |Rotate.  kbd:[Shift] for 45° steps.
|The dotted edge between handles |Skew.  kbd:[Shift] constrains to one axis.
|===
. Hold kbd:[Option] as you drag to leave the original and transform a copy; a plus sign
  appears beside the pointer.
. Double-click away from the selection, or press kbd:[Esc], to put the handles away.

While you drag a handle, smart guides show when an edge or the centre of the selection lines
up with a nearby object or the page, and a size badge appears when the width or height you
are making matches a neighbour's; release on the guide to snap to it
(xref:moving.adoc#smart-guides[Smart guides]).  Rotation and skew show no smart guides;
kbd:[Shift] is the constraint there.

To transform one member of a group or clipping path: double-click the container to show its
handles, then kbd:[Option]-click the member -- the handles now belong to it.  Press kbd:[~] to
go back up to the container without moving the center.

To transform some points of a path: select the points and double-click one of them.  The
handles surround the selected points and transform only them.  kbd:[~] switches the handles
to the whole path.

== Repeating a transformation

menu:Modify[Transform > Transform Again] (kbd:[Cmd+Shift+T]) applies the last transformation
once more to the current selection -- another object, or the same one.  Combined with
menu:Edit[Duplicate] this is power-duplicating (xref:copying.adoc[Copying]).

== Constrain angle

The *Constrain angle* (menu:WireTuner[Settings… > General], default 0°) is the baseline
from which kbd:[Shift] constraints are measured -- for transforming, for moving, and for
drawing.  Set it to 30° to work on an isometric grid.

== Other transformation tools

The *3D Rotation* tool and the *Mirror* tool are extension tools on the Extensions toolbar
(menu:Window[Toolbars > Extensions]).

3D Rotation::
Foreshortens a selection as though turned in space.  Double-click the tool to set *Easy* or
*Expert* mode, the rotation point (mouse click, selection center, center of gravity, or the
origin) and, in Expert mode, the *Distance* and projection point.  Drag on the canvas to
rotate; the result is a flat, skewed-and-scaled copy of the shapes, editable like any path.

Mirror::
Reflects copies of a selection across one or more axes as you click.  Double-click the tool
for *Horizontal*, *Vertical*, *Horizontal & Vertical* or *Multiple* mirroring, the number of
copies, whether the axes are set by *Closed Path*, *Rotate* or *Reflect*, and *Auto
Rotate*.

NOTE: These tools are extensions; see xref:extensions.adoc[Extensions].

== Working with others

A transformation is one change per object, however you made it.  Collaborators see the
object jump to its transformed state when your change arrives; while you are still dragging,
they see only your selection outline.

Each object remembers its position, rotation, scale and skew together, as one thing.  So if
you rotate an object while someone else scales it, the two do not blend into a shape nobody
intended: the later of the two is kept and the other is set aside, and after an offline
period the review lists the object under *Position* with both states, offering *Use mine*,
*Use theirs* and *Keep both copies*.  Reshaping and recoloring are separate from this: your
rotation and their point edit both apply.

Making copies with the Transform panel creates new objects, which never conflict with
anything.

== Specification

=== Data model

Every drawable node carries `CommonProps.transform`, an ATOMIC 2×3 affine matrix mapping
the node's own coordinate space into its parent's (xref:crdt-model.adoc[CRDT model]).  Paths
keep their points in their own space; rectangles and ellipses keep their parametric
description; text blocks keep their frame; groups compose.  *Every* transformation command
composes a matrix onto this one register and writes it once:

[source,protobuf]
----
// From common.proto (PROTO-003).  Restated here because this page depends on its policy.
message Transform {
  // Column-major affine: [a c tx; b d ty].  Points in points (1/72 in), y down.
  double a = 1; double b = 2; double c = 3; double d = 4; double tx = 5; double ty = 6;
}
// CommonProps.transform = 4 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
----

Why one ATOMIC register, and not baked geometry::
* Merging a rotation from one user with a scale from another *per matrix component* would
  produce a shear nobody drew; merging them by whole matrix produces one of the two intended
  states.  That is the ATOMIC rationale from the CRDT model, and it holds for the whole
  transform history of an object.
* Baking a transformation into points would spread one gesture across N point registers.
  A concurrent point edit by another user would then merge into a shape where some points
  moved and some did not -- a torn outline.  With a transform register, a point edit and a
  transformation are different registers and both apply, intact.
* Rectangles, ellipses, polygons and text blocks stay their kind after transformation.
* One op per object per gesture keeps changes small and coalescing effective.
Consequences::
* Hit testing, snapping, alignment and the Object panel's X/Y/W/H all go through the
  transform chain (GEO-005).  Point editing inverse-maps mouse positions into node space.
* Stroke widths are nominal and *not* scaled by the matrix unless the command bakes them
  (leaf objects, *Strokes* ticked: each `BasicStroke.width` is multiplied by
  `sqrt(|det|)`) or the group's *Transform as unit* says so at render time.  Fills and
  effects are in node space and follow the matrix; *Fills* unticked makes the command write
  a compensating inverse onto the fill's axis/origin registers (ATTR fields), so the fill
  stays put.  This requires ATTR to define gradient axes and tile origins in node space.
* A transformation of *selected points* is not a transform: it writes point registers.
* Export flattens (bakes) at write time; the document never needs to.
* Boolean and offset operations flatten inputs before computing (xref:combining-paths.adoc[Combining paths]).
* Ungroup bakes the group's matrix into each member's register (one write per member).

Copies (`Copies > 0`) are `CreateNode`s with `transform = Mᵏ × T` for `k = 1…n`, cloned from
the source (all registers, fresh element ids), positioned above the source.

The transformation center, the last transformation (for *Transform Again* and
power-duplicating) and handle visibility are UI state, not document data.

=== Merge semantics

* Any two transformations of one node: LWW on `transform`, whole matrix.  The loser is
  retained; the review sheet shows *Position* (`same register`) with both states rendered in
  the Mine/Theirs preview.
* Transformation vs. point edit, vs. attribute edit, vs. reparent: independent registers,
  all apply.
* Transformation vs. delete: applies to the deleted node; back on restore.
* Transform panel with *Strokes* or *Fills* affecting other registers: those writes are in
  the same change and merge independently.  A concurrent stroke-width edit by someone else
  can therefore win over the bake while the matrix stands -- a slightly thicker or thinner
  stroke than intended, never a broken shape; listed by the review sheet as `same register`
  on the width.
* Copies: creations; no conflicts.
* Transforming a group vs. ungrouping it: see xref:grouping.adoc[Grouping].

Read-time normalization: a non-invertible matrix (zero scale from a degenerate drag) renders
the node with zero area and hit-tests by its transformed bounds so it can still be selected
and fixed; commands never write one (scale is clamped to ±0.01%).

=== Offline behavior

Fully offline.  Successive transformations of the same objects coalesce to one op each
(same register, same replica); the labels of the change boundaries are kept so history reads
`Rotate`, `Scale`, `Rotate`.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

* `TransformCommand(nodes:, matrix:, about:, options:)` in `WTModel/Commands/Transform.swift`
  composes `M_about = T(c) · M · T(-c)` in the parent space of each node onto its
  `transform`; provisional application during drags; commit on release; one change per
  gesture, label from the kind (`Rotate 3 objects`).  `MoveCommand` is the translation case.
* `PointTransformCommand` for sub-selections writes point registers.
* Tools: `RotateTool`, `ScaleTool`, `SkewTool`, `ReflectTool` on the APP-003 protocol; the
  center is the mouse-down point; kbd:[Shift] and kbd:[Option] are read on every drag event
  (modifier change mid-drag is delivered by APP-003).
* Transform handles: an overlay mode of the Pointer tool (`HandlesOverlay`) with hit regions
  in screen space (handles 8 px, rotate zone 12 px outside, skew zone along edges).  Scale
  drags query the smart-guide engine (OBJ-038) for edge, centre and size-match guides through
  `ToolContext.snapping`; the overlay for them is OBJ-039 (xref:moving.adoc#smart-guides[Smart
  guides]).  On a rotated canvas the handles are drawn around the selection's pasteboard-space
  bounds transformed to the view, so they turn with the artwork.
* Transform panel: SwiftUI body with five tabs; the center fields bind to the overlay's
  center; the *Fills*/*Strokes* toggles and *Contents* are persisted in `UserDefaults`.
* `WTModel.LastTransform` records the matrix and options of the last committed
  transformation for *Transform Again* and `DuplicateMemory`.
* Extension tools (3D Rotation, Mirror) implement the extension tool protocol from
  xref:extensions.adoc[Extensions] and emit ordinary `TransformCommand`/`CreateNode`
  changes.
* Preferences: *Double-click enables transform handles* (on), *Constrain angle* (0°).
