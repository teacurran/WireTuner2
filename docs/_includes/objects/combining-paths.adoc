New shapes come from combining old ones.  *Join* fuses paths into one composite path whose
sub-paths cut holes in one another; *Union*, *Divide*, *Intersect*, *Punch*, *Crop* and
*Transparency* build new paths from the way selected paths overlap.  All of them replace the
paths you started with, unless you ask to keep them.

union, difference, intersection or exclusion of its members while every member remains an
ordinary path you can move and reshape, and btn:[Expand] on the effect produces the same
result these commands do whenever you are ready to commit.

== Keeping the originals

By default a combine command consumes the paths it was given.  To keep them:

* Hold kbd:[Shift] while choosing the command, for this one time; or
* turn off *Path operations consume original paths* in menu:WireTuner[Settings… > Object],
  for every time.  Holding kbd:[Shift] then consumes them for this one time.

Kept originals stay exactly where they were, under the new path.

== Join

Join makes two or more paths into a composite path: one object with several sub-paths.
Where closed sub-paths overlap, the overlap is a hole (or not, depending on fill direction).
The composite takes the stroke and fill of the backmost path.

To join:

. Select two or more paths.
. Choose menu:Modify[Join] (kbd:[Cmd+J]).

Two *open* paths are joined end to end into one open path if their ends touch.  With *Join
non-touching paths* on (menu:WireTuner[Settings… > Object]), ends within the *Snap distance*
are joined too; off, they must touch.  menu:Modify[Split] (kbd:[Cmd+Shift+J]) takes a
composite apart again.

The composite's holes are governed by its *Even/odd fill* setting in the Object panel and by
each sub-path's direction; menu:Modify[Alter Path > Correct Direction] and *Reverse
Direction* fix a hole that came out filled (xref:editing-paths.adoc[Editing paths]).

== Union

Union merges closed paths into one path covering everything any of them covered.  Paths that
do not touch the others become sub-paths of a composite.  The result takes the attributes of
the backmost path.

To apply Union: select the paths and choose menu:Modify[Combine > Union], or click *Union*
on the Path Operations toolbar.

== Divide

Divide cuts the selection into pieces along every overlap.  Open and closed paths may be
mixed.  Each region that was inside several paths becomes its own path with the attributes
of the frontmost of them; the pieces are grouped.

To apply Divide: select the paths and choose menu:Modify[Combine > Divide].

== Intersect

Intersect keeps only the area common to *all* the selected closed paths, as one path with
the backmost path's attributes.  If the paths share no common area, the selection is deleted
(unless originals are kept), because there is nothing to keep.

To apply Intersect: select the paths and choose menu:Modify[Combine > Intersect].

== Punch

Punch uses the frontmost closed path as a cutter: its shape is removed from every closed path
below it, and the cutter itself is discarded.  A hole entirely inside a path makes that path
a composite.  The punched paths keep their attributes.

To apply Punch: select the paths, with the cutter on top, and choose menu:Modify[Combine >
Punch].

== Crop

Crop is the opposite of Punch: the frontmost path is a cookie cutter, and everything below it
is trimmed to its outline.  The cutter is discarded; the trimmed paths keep their attributes.
Crop works on vector objects only -- to crop a bitmap, use the Crop tool
(xref:cropping-bitmaps.adoc[Cropping bitmaps]).

To apply Crop: select the paths, with the cutter on top, and choose menu:Modify[Combine >
Crop].

== Transparency

Transparency creates a new path in the overlap of two closed paths, filled with a mix of
their two fill colors, so the top one appears to be see-through.  It is a drawing trick, not
real transparency; for that, see xref:transparency.adoc[Transparency].

To apply Transparency:

. Select two overlapping filled paths.
. Choose menu:Modify[Combine > Transparency].
. In the sheet, set how much of the top color shows in the overlap (0--100%) and click
  btn:[OK].

== Working with others

A combine command replaces the paths it used with new ones.  Collaborators see the originals
vanish and the result appear in the same instant.

If someone was editing one of those originals at the same moment -- reshaping it, recoloring
it -- their edit lands on a path that no longer exists, so it has no visible effect.  Nothing
is lost: after an offline period, the review lists it as an edit to something that was
deleted and offers *Restore*, which brings back the original with their edit, alongside your
result.  When both of you are online, the vanished path is simply gone, and their next look
at the canvas shows your result; an undo on their side does nothing to it.

If two people combine overlapping selections at the same moment, both results appear and all
originals are consumed.  The two results may overlap; undo either.

== Specification

=== Data model

No fields.  A composite path is a `PathProps` with several `contours` (a SEQUENCE); every
combine result is a fresh path node (or, for Divide, a fresh group of fresh path nodes)
created with `CreateNode`.  The inputs are `SetDeleted(true)` when consumed.  Results carry
copies of the attribute stack they inherit (backmost or frontmost input) under fresh element
ids.

The result of a combine has a new node id and no reference to its inputs.  We do not model
a combine as an edit of the backmost input (moving contours into it) because the merge
semantics of "the input survived but changed shape completely" would be worse than
"the input was deleted": a concurrent point edit would then land on a contour that no
longer means what it meant.

=== Merge semantics

* A combine is one change `Union 3 paths` (etc.): `CreateNode` for each result (children
  after parents for Divide's group), positioned where the frontmost input was, then
  `SetDeleted(true)` per consumed input.
* *Combine vs. edit of an input*: the edit lands on the deleted input; retained.  The review
  sheet lists `edit vs delete` with *Restore*.  Restoring brings back the input with the
  edit, next to the result; the user then decides.
* *Combine vs. move of an input*: as above -- the result was computed from the pre-move
  geometry.
* *Combine vs. combine (overlapping inputs)*: both results are created, every input is
  deleted by whichever change deletes it; nothing conflicts at the register level.  The
  review sheet shows nothing (no shared registers), which is right: both results are
  present and either can be undone.
* *Combine vs. delete of an input*: the input is deleted twice; converges.
* *Undo of a combine* sets the results deleted and restores the inputs (`deleted = false`),
  unless someone else has since deleted them again.
* *Kept originals*: no deletion, pure creation.

Read-time normalization: none for this page.  (A composite path is an ordinary path.)

=== Offline behavior

Fully offline.  Boolean operations are computed locally (GEO-002).

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

* `WTGeometry.Boolean` provides `union`, `intersection`, `subtracting`/`punch`, `crop`, `transparency`, `divide` (returning `DividedPiece` with covering operand indices), `exclusiveOr` and `normalize`.  Open contours are closed by their chord for filling; open paths as cutting lines in Divide are not supported by GEO-002.

* `CombineCommand(operation:, keepOriginals:)` in `WTModel/Commands/Combine.swift` over
  `WTGeometry.Boolean` (GEO-002): `union`, `divide`, `intersect`, `punch`, `crop`,
  `transparency`.  Inputs are flattened to pasteboard space through their transform chains
  before the operation; results are created with identity `transform` and pasteboard-space
  contours, positioned at the frontmost input's slot under its parent.
* Join: contour concatenation with the open-path end-matching rule (touching, or within
  *Snap distance* when *Join non-touching paths* is on); Split: one path per contour.
* Transparency color mix: linear interpolation of the two fills' colors in the document's
  working space, by the sheet's percentage; non-basic fills are converted to their average
  color first.
* Menu items validate on selection: at least two paths (Join), all closed (Union, Intersect,
  Punch, Crop), exactly two filled (Transparency).  kbd:[Shift] modifies the menu item's
  title (`Union (keep originals)`).
* Preferences: *Path operations consume original paths* (on), *Join non-touching paths*
  (off).

greedily (any end to any end, reversing as needed; the joint is one point at the ends' midpoint keeping the arriving in
