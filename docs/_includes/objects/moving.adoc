You can move a selection by dragging it, by nudging it with the arrow keys, or by typing
where it should go.  While you drag, the Info toolbar reads out the distance moved and the
pointer's position, and the selection snaps to points, guides, smart guides and the grid when
snapping is on.

== Dragging

To move a selection by dragging:

. With the Pointer tool, press on a selected object -- on its fill, or on its path if it has
  no fill -- and drag.  With another tool active, hold kbd:[Cmd] first to use the Pointer.
. Release where you want it.

Modifiers while dragging:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Key |Effect

|kbd:[Shift]
|Constrains the move to horizontal, vertical or 45° from the start point, measured from the
*Constrain angle* (xref:transforming.adoc[Transforming]).

|kbd:[Option]
|Drags a copy and leaves the original (xref:copying.adoc[Copying]).  Can be disabled with the
*Option-drag copies* preference.

|kbd:[Cmd] (held before pressing)
|Uses the Pointer temporarily while another tool is active.

|kbd:[Esc]
|Cancels the drag and puts the selection back.
|===

Pausing briefly before you start to drag shows the object's outline as it moves; dragging
immediately shows only its bounding box until you pause.  Either way, the move applies when
you release.

== Nudging with the arrow keys

Press kbd:[Left], kbd:[Right], kbd:[Up] or kbd:[Down] to move the selection by the *Arrow key
distance*; hold kbd:[Shift] to move by the *Shift-arrow key distance*.  Holding a key
repeats.  A run of nudges in one direction is one step in the Undo menu.

To set the nudge distances:

. Choose menu:WireTuner[Settings… > General].
. Enter *Arrow key distance* (default 1 pt) and *Shift-arrow key distance* (default 10 pt),
  1--864 pt, in the document's unit.

NOTE: These distances are your own preference, not a property of the document, so a shared
document does not change how everyone's arrow keys behave.

== Moving numerically

To move a selection by a distance:

. Choose menu:Modify[Transform > Move], or open the Transform panel (menu:Window[Transform])
  and click btn:[Move].
. Enter the horizontal distance in *X* (positive moves right) and the vertical distance in
  *Y* (positive moves up).
. Click btn:[Move].  Enter a number in *Copies* to leave the original and make that many
  moved copies instead (xref:transforming.adoc[Transforming]).

To move a selection to a position:

. Open the Object panel (menu:Window[Object]) and select the object's root row.
. Enter the new *X* and *Y* -- the position of the bounding box's bottom-left corner relative
  to the ruler zero point (xref:rulers.adoc[Rulers]).  Press kbd:[Return].

Both fields accept units and arithmetic (xref:object-panel.adoc[Object panel]).

== Snapping

While you drag, the selection can snap to other objects' points, to paths, to guides and to
the grid.  The *Snap distance* preference (menu:WireTuner[Settings… > General], default 3
pixels) sets how close you must come before a snap takes hold; the pointer changes shape when
it does.

[cols="2,1,3"]
|===
|Snap |Toggle |Snaps the dragged point to

|Snap to Point
|menu:View[Snap to Point], or the Tools panel button
|Anchor points of other objects.  The point that snaps is the one you grabbed, or the
selection's nearest point to the target when you grabbed a fill.

|Snap to Object
|menu:View[Snap to Object], or the Tools panel button
|Any position along another object's path.

|Snap to Guides
|menu:View[Snap to Guides]
|Ruler guides (xref:grid-guides.adoc[Grid and guides]).

|Snap to Grid
|menu:View[Snap to Grid]
|Grid intersections.
|===

When several snaps are within range, points win over paths, paths over guides, guides over
smart guides (below), and smart guides over the grid.  All four are toggles: choosing an
enabled one turns it off.  Hold kbd:[Control] while dragging to suspend every kind of snapping
for that drag.

To snap a selection to a point:

. Turn on Snap to Point.
. Drag the selection until the pointer changes near the target point.
. Release.

== Smart guides

Smart guides are alignment lines that appear only while you are dragging, resizing or drawing
and vanish when you let go.  They show you, without any setup, when what you are moving lines
up with something nearby, and they let you snap to it.  They are drawn in magenta so they are
never mistaken for the ruler guides you place yourself
(xref:grid-guides.adoc[Grid and guides]).

// TODO screenshot: a rectangle being dragged with a magenta centre line to a neighbouring
// object and an equal-spacing hint between three objects

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Guide |Appears when

|Edge
|The left, right, top or bottom edge of what you are moving lines up with an edge of a nearby
object, or with a page edge.  The line runs from your object to the one it matches.

|Center
|Your object's horizontal or vertical centre lines up with a nearby object's centre or the
page's centre.

|Equal spacing
|Your object would be the same distance from a neighbour as that neighbour is from the next
object along, in either direction.  Small arrows mark each equal gap, with the distance beside
them.  With two objects already in a row, the hint shows the position that makes a third.

|Size match
|While resizing or drawing, the width or height you are making matches a nearby object's.  A
small badge beside the pointer shows the matched size.
|===

"Nearby" means objects within the part of the pasteboard you can see.  Locked objects and the
pages themselves count; hidden objects and objects on hidden layers do not.

To turn smart guides on or off::
Choose menu:View[Smart Guides] (kbd:[Cmd+U]).  A check mark means they are on; they are on
by default.  This is your own setting and applies to every document you open.  The same
switch is *Smart guides* in menu:WireTuner[Settings… > General].

To snap to a smart guide::
Drag until the guide appears and release.  Within the *Snap distance*, a smart guide pulls the
object exactly onto the alignment.  Smart guides take part in the usual snap order: a point,
path or ruler guide within reach wins over a smart guide, and a smart guide wins over the grid.
Hold kbd:[Control] to suspend snapping, smart guides included, for one drag.

To change the guide colour::
Choose menu:WireTuner[Settings… > Colors] and click the *Smart guide color* well.  Magenta is
the default.

=== Measuring distances

To see distances without dragging anything:

. Select an object.
. Hold kbd:[Option] and move the pointer over another object.  Dimension lines show the gap
  between your selection and that object on each side that faces it, in the document's unit.
. Move the pointer over an empty part of the page instead to see the distances from the
  selection to the four page edges.
. Release kbd:[Option] to clear the measurements.  The pointer's kbd:[Option]-click still
  subselects as usual; measuring is a hover, not a click.

Turn this off with *Show measurements while holding Option* in
menu:WireTuner[Settings… > General] if you prefer kbd:[Option] to do nothing until you click.

== The Info toolbar

The Info toolbar (menu:Window[Toolbars > Info]) shows the pointer's position, the horizontal
and vertical distance moved since the drag began, the total distance and the angle, and the
name of the object under the pointer.  Values are in the document's unit.

== Moving between documents

Dragging a selection from one document window into another copies it there
(xref:copying.adoc[Copying]).  The original stays where it was.

== Working with others

When a collaborator moves an object, you see it slide to its new position as soon as their
change arrives; while they are still dragging, you see their selection outline, not the
motion.  If you are dragging an object at the same moment they move it, both moves are made,
but one position must win: the later one is kept, and the other person's move is set aside.
If yours was set aside you see the object jump to their position when you release.

Moving an object never conflicts with someone else changing its shape, color or text: their
edit lands on the object at its new position.

Smart guides and measurements are drawn for you alone, from the objects as you currently see
them.  If a collaborator moves an object while you are lining up against it, the guide follows
the object's new position as soon as the change arrives.

Offline, every move you make is kept locally and sent when you reconnect.  If someone moved
the same object while you were away, the review that opens lists it under *Position* with
both places and offers *Use mine*, *Use theirs* or *Keep both copies*.

== Specification

=== Data model

A move writes the node's `CommonProps.transform` register (ATOMIC, defined in
xref:crdt-model.adoc[CRDT model]).  Nothing else changes: point coordinates are in the
object's own space and are untouched by moving.  For a *sub-selection* of points, a move
writes the point registers instead (`PathProps.contours[i].points[j].anchor` and handles),
which is a point edit, not a transform (xref:editing-paths.adoc[Editing paths]).

No fields are added by this page.  Smart guides and measurements are purely client state:
they are computed from the display list for the duration of a gesture and write nothing, not
even `local_only` view state.  The *Smart guides* toggle is the preference
`general.smart_guides` (xref:preferences.adoc[Preferences]), not a per-document field.

=== Merge semantics

* *Move vs. move* of one object: same register, LWW by OpId.  The losing matrix is retained in
  the log; the review sheet shows a *Position* row (`move vs move`) with both positions and
  offers *Keep both copies*.
* *Move vs. shape edit*: different registers (`transform` vs. point elements); both apply.
* *Move vs. delete*: the move applies to the deleted node; if it is restored, it is at the
  moved position.
* *Move vs. group*: the move writes the node's `transform`; grouping reparents the node under
  a group whose `transform` is identity at creation, so the node's matrix is still correct
  relative to the group.  Both apply cleanly.
* *Nudge runs*: consecutive arrow presses write the same register repeatedly; the outbox
  coalesces them to one op, and undo grouping makes them one step.

No read-time normalization.

=== Offline behavior

Fully offline.  A drag produces one op after coalescing; a day of moving the same object is
one op per change boundary.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

* `MoveCommand(nodes:, delta:)` in `WTModel` composes a translation onto each node's current
  `transform` and emits one change labelled `Move` / `Move N objects`.  During a drag the
  command is applied *provisionally* on each mouse-move (so the canvas renders the motion)
  and committed once on mouse-up; APP-011 groups the drag into one undo step.
* Snapping resolution is GEO-005 with priorities point > path > guide > smart guide > grid;
  the snap candidate query uses the REND-003 R-tree within *Snap distance* converted to
  pasteboard units at the current zoom.  The smart-guide kind sits between ruler guides and
  the grid because it is derived from real object geometry that the user can see, while a
  grid line is not; kbd:[Control] suspends all kinds.
* Smart guides (`WTRender/SmartGuides`, OBJ-038): `SmartGuideEngine` is built when a gesture
  starts and rebuilt when the viewport or the display list changes during it.  Its candidate
  set is a REND-003 R-tree query for the viewport rectangle on the current canvas, minus the
  nodes in the gesture, hidden nodes and nodes on hidden layers, capped at the 500 candidates
  nearest the gesture's start point; plus every page whose rectangle intersects the viewport.
  From each candidate it takes the transformed bounds' four edges and two centre lines in
  pasteboard coordinates, sorted per axis, so a query during a drag is a binary search per
  edge of the moving bounds.  Equal spacing scans the sorted edges on each axis for a pair of
  neighbours whose gap equals the moving object's gap to the nearer of them within the snap
  distance; size match compares the in-progress width and height with candidate widths and
  heights.  The engine returns `[SmartGuide]` (axis, position, span, kind, participating node
  ids, label value) and feeds GEO-005 a `smartGuide` snap candidate per guide.  Budget: under
  0.2 ms per mouse move at 500 candidates, so it never touches the frame budget.  Smart guides
  are pasteboard-axis lines, so on a rotated canvas they are drawn through the view transform
  and turn with the artwork.
* Smart-guide overlay (`WTApp/Canvas/SmartGuideOverlay`, OBJ-039): draws the engine's result
  into the tool overlay layer in *Smart guide color* -- 1 px lines, spacing arrows with the
  gap value, size badges -- and clears on mouse-up or kbd:[Esc].  Measurements: on
  `flagsChanged` with kbd:[Option] down and no mouse button, `mouseMoved` hit-tests the
  hovered node (REND-003); the overlay draws dimension lines between the selection's bounds
  and the hovered node's bounds on each facing side, or to the four edges of the page under
  the pointer when nothing is hit, labelled in the document's unit; kbd:[Option] up or a
  mouse-down clears it.  Drawing and transform tools reach the engine through
  `ToolContext.snapping`; each tool adopts it in its own task (the Pointer's move and the
  transform handles here and on xref:transforming.adoc[Transforming]; the drawing tools in the
  DRAW epic).
* Preferences: *Arrow key distance* (1 pt), *Shift-arrow key distance* (10 pt), *Snap
  distance* (3 px), *Smart guides* (on), *Show measurements while holding Option* (on),
  *Smart guide color* (magenta, local).
* Nudge: `NSResponder.moveLeft(_:)` etc. on the canvas view; key repeat produces successive
  provisional moves grouped into one undo step until 500 ms without a key event.
* Drag-and-drop between windows uses `NSDraggingSession` with the native pasteboard type
  (xref:copying.adoc[Copying]); dropping inside the same window is a move, not a copy.
* Info toolbar: `WTApp/Toolbars/InfoToolbar` observes the active tool's drag state.
* Preferences: *Arrow key distance* (1 pt), *Shift-arrow key distance* (10 pt), *Snap
  distance* (3 px).

NOTE: As built by OBJ-008 and OBJ-009.  `MoveObjects(nodes, by:)` composes the pasteboard-space delta,
converted into each node's parent space, onto its `transform` ("Move" / "Move N objects"); locked
objects are skipped.  The Pointer previews the move as outlines in the overlay and performs one
command on mouse-up (client.adoc's tool rule, rather than provisional application per drag event);
kbd:[Option] with *Option-drag copies paths* performs a `DuplicateObjects` offset by the drag
("Copy").  Nudging is `ObjectEditing.nudge(by:)`, reached from the canvas through
`ToolManager.nudge` for arrow keys without Command, Option or Control: a burst is one undo group
until 500 ms pass without a press, and a point sub-selection moves its points (`MovePoints`).
Snapping goes through the `SnappingContext` placeholder (GEO-005), and smart guides and the Info
toolbar's move readout are OBJ-038/039's.

NOTE: As built by OBJ-038 (`WTRender/SmartGuides/SmartGuideEngine.swift`).  `SmartGuideEngine(displayList:index:viewport:
excludedItems:excludedNodes:hiddenLayers:pages:start:)` takes the top-level items whose bounds meet the viewport (through the
hit tester's R-tree when given), less the gesture's items and nodes, the Guides layer's items and any hidden layer's run,
keeps the 500 nearest the start point and adds the pages in view.  `guides(moving:tolerance:)` returns a `SmartGuideMatch`:
the offset per axis that lands the moving bounds on the nearest edge-on-edge, centre-on-centre or equal-spacing alignment
within the tolerance (the *Snap distance* in pasteboard units), and every `SmartGuide` that holds at the offset bounds
(axis, position, span, kind, node ids, labelled value and, for spacing, each equal gap).  `sizeGuides(width:height:tolerance:)`
does the size match.  `SmartGuideMatch.snapGuides(for:)` turns the match into lines for the gesture's snapping point, which go
into DOC-016's `SnapSources.smartGuides` and so into the `Snapper` at the `smartGuide` kind GEO-005 already defined between
guide and grid (no WTGeometry change was needed).  Deviations: candidates are top-level display items, so a group counts as one
object (its members are not separate candidates); hidden objects and hidden layers are absent from the display list already,
and `excludedNodes` / `hiddenLayers` cover callers whose list still carries them.  A row for equal spacing is the objects
overlapping the moving bounds on the other axis; the engine detects continuing a row after (or before) its two nearest
objects and sitting centred between the nearest neighbours on both sides, and pages take no part in spacing or size matches.
The 0.2 ms budget is a `PerfBudget` in `SmartGuideEngineTests` (500 of 50,000 candidates), held in the perf run.  The overlay
and the tools' use of it are OBJ-039's.
