Raster effects give a vector object the kinds of treatment a photo editor gives a picture: a
beveled or embossed edge, a blur, a drop shadow or glow, a sharpened edge.  They are live
effects like the vector ones on xref:live-effects.adoc[Live vector effects] -- attributes in the
Object panel's appearance list that never alter the path -- but their result is made of
pixels.  {product} draws them at a *raster effects resolution* you set for the document (or for
one object), so they look right on the device they are meant for and print at that resolution.
This page covers the resolution settings, the preview quality preference, each raster effect
and every one of its options, and what happens to raster effects on the way to a printer or an
exported file.  Transparency is a raster-family effect with its own page,
xref:transparency.adoc[Transparency].

NOTE: {product} draws these effects with Core Image.

== Resolution

A raster effect is drawn into a bitmap that covers the object plus the effect's spread, at the
raster effects resolution, then composited into the artwork.  Higher resolutions give smoother
results and larger files; lower ones draw faster.  A common approach: draw at 72 ppi, set the
document to the output device's resolution (300 ppi for offset printing) before you print or
export.

To set the raster effects resolution for the document:

. Choose menu:File[Document Settings > Raster Effects…].  The Raster Effects sheet opens.
. Enter a *Resolution* in pixels per inch that matches the device the drawing is for: 72 for
  screen, 150--300 for print.
. Select *Optimal CMYK rendering* to draw the effects in CMYK, bypassing the document's color
  management (xref:color-management.adoc[Color management]).  Use it only when every color in
  the document is a CMYK color.
. Click btn:[OK].

The document setting is part of the document and applies to everyone who opens it.  The same
settings appear on the Document panel (xref:document-panel.adoc[The Document panel]).

To give one object its own resolution:

. Select the object.
. From the Object panel's options menu (the gear at the top right), choose *Raster Effect
  Settings…*.
. Deselect *Use document raster effects resolution* and enter a *Resolution*.
. Click btn:[OK].  The object draws at its own resolution from then on, whatever the document
  setting.

=== Preview quality

Drawing every raster effect at 300 ppi while you sketch would make the canvas sluggish, so the
on-screen preview can use a lower resolution than the one that prints.  The *Raster effect
preview* preference (menu:{product}[Settings… > Redraw]) chooses:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Setting |Effect

|*Screen resolution* (default)
|Effects are drawn on screen at the screen's pixel density at the current zoom, capped at the
document resolution.  Fast, and what you see is what the screen can show anyway.

|*Document resolution*
|Effects are drawn on screen at the document (or object) resolution and scaled.  Slower,
exact.

|*Draft*
|Effects are drawn at half the screen resolution.  For very large documents.

|*Off*
|Raster effects are not drawn on screen; the object shows without them and a small badge marks
it.  Printing and export are unaffected.
|===

This preference is yours alone; it does not change the document or what collaborators see.
Fast Preview and Keyline view modes (xref:document-view.adoc[Viewing the document]) also skip
raster effects.

== Bevel and Emboss

A bevel gives the object a raised, sloped rim, lit from a direction you choose.  An emboss
makes the object look pressed into or raised out of the page, keeping the page's own color.

To apply a bevel:

. Click btn:[Add Effect] in the Object panel and choose menu:Bevel and Emboss[Outer Bevel] or
  menu:Bevel and Emboss[Inner Bevel].  An outer bevel builds the rim outside the shape, an
  inner bevel inside its edge.
. Set its options:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |What it does

|*Bevel color*
|Outer bevel only: the color of the rim, from the pop-up color palette (drag a swatch onto it
or click it).  An inner bevel uses the object's own fill.

|*Width*
|The width of the rim, in points.

|*Contrast*
|0--100: how strong the highlight and shadow sides of the rim are.

|*Softness*
|0--10: how blurred the transition across the rim is, without changing its width.

|*Angle*
|Direction of the light, 0--360°.  Type a value or drag the dial.

|*Edge shape*
|The cross-section of the rim: *Flat*, *Smooth*, *Sloped*, *Frame 1*, *Frame 2*, *Ring* or
*Ruffle*.

|*Button preset*
|A lighting preset for button-like artwork: *Raised*, *Highlighted*, *Inset* or *Inverted*.
Presets change the highlight and shadow tones only; the other options stay as set.
|===

To apply an emboss:

. Click btn:[Add Effect] and choose menu:Bevel and Emboss[Raised Emboss] or
  menu:Bevel and Emboss[Inset Emboss].  The object's own fill and stroke disappear; what
  remains is the page (or whatever lies behind) shaded into a relief.
. Set *Width* (the depth of the relief), *Contrast*, *Softness* and *Angle* as for a bevel.

== Blur

Blur softens the object.  *Basic* is a box blur: an out-of-focus look.  *Gaussian* weights
nearby pixels more, giving a hazy, frosted-glass look at the same radius.

To apply a blur:

. Click btn:[Add Effect] and choose menu:Blur[Basic] or menu:Blur[Gaussian Blur].
. Set *Radius*, 0--250 pixels at the raster effects resolution.  Larger radii spread further
  and take longer to draw.

Blurring a fill only (attach the effect to the fill in the appearance list) keeps the stroke
crisp above the softened interior.

== Shadow and Glow

A drop shadow makes the object float above the page; an inner shadow makes it a hole cut into
the page.  A glow is a halo of one color around the outside, an inner glow a halo inside the
edge.

To apply a shadow or glow:

. Click btn:[Add Effect] and choose menu:Shadow and Glow[Drop Shadow], menu:Shadow and
  Glow[Inner Shadow], menu:Shadow and Glow[Glow] or menu:Shadow and Glow[Inner Glow].
. Set its options:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |What it does

|*Color*
|The shadow or glow color, from the pop-up color palette.  Shadows default to black, glows to
the object's fill color.

|*Offset*
|Shadows: how far the shadow is displaced from the object, in points, in the direction set by
*Angle*.  Glows: the width of the halo.

|*Opacity*
|0--100.  Lower values are more see-through.

|*Softness*
|0--30: how blurred the shadow's or glow's edge is, without changing its distance or width.

|*Angle*
|Shadows only: the direction the shadow falls, 0--360°, by value or dial.  A drop shadow at
315° falls down and to the right.
|===

== Sharpen

Sharpening increases the contrast between neighboring pixels.  *Basic* sharpens the whole
object evenly; *Unsharp Mask* finds edges and sharpens only those, which is the better choice
for placed photographs (xref:bitmaps.adoc[Working with bitmaps]).

To apply a sharpen effect:

. Click btn:[Add Effect] and choose menu:Sharpen[Basic] or menu:Sharpen[Unsharp Mask].
. Set its options:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |What it does

|*Amount*
|0--500 (percent): the strength of the effect.

|*Pixel radius*
|Unsharp Mask only, 0.1--250: how far around each pixel the effect looks for an edge.  Larger
radii sharpen broader features.

|*Threshold*
|Unsharp Mask only, 0--255: how different two neighboring pixels must be before they count as
an edge.  `0` sharpens everything; `255` sharpens almost nothing.
|===

== Raster effects and color

* Raster effects are computed in RGB and displayed through the document's color management.
  Spot colors inside a raster effect are converted to their process equivalents for the
  effect's pixels; the object's own vector fills and strokes keep their spot colors.  A blurred
  spot-color shape therefore prints as a process-color bitmap on the CMYK plates, not on the
  spot plate (xref:spot-process.adoc[Spot and process color]).
* *Optimal CMYK rendering* computes the effect directly in CMYK, which keeps pure blacks and
  avoids gamut shifts when everything in the document is already CMYK.
* Custom PostScript fills (xref:fill-attributes.adoc[Fill attributes]) cannot be rasterized on
  the Mac and are drawn as their preview color inside a raster effect.

== Printing and exporting raster effects

When you print or export, each object with a raster effect is rendered to a bitmap at the
document's raster effects resolution (or the object's own), in the document's color space, and
placed in the output in the object's position:

* PDF and PostScript output embed the bitmap with the object's vector outline as a clip where
  the effect is inside the shape (inner bevel, inner shadow, inner glow, sharpen, blur of a
  fill); drop shadows and glows extend beyond the outline and carry their own alpha.
* Bitmap exports (PNG, TIFF, JPEG, GIF) simply include the effect in the rendered image at the
  export resolution, resampling if it differs from the raster effects resolution
  (xref:export-bitmap.adoc[Exporting bitmaps]).
* SVG and other vector-only formats receive a bitmap for the effected object, or, where the
  format cannot embed one, the object without its raster effects and a warning in the export
  report (xref:export-vector.adoc[Exporting vector formats]).
* Vector effects on the same object are applied before the raster stage and remain vector in
  the output.

Print resolution higher than the raster effects resolution does not sharpen the effect; set the
document resolution before the final print.  See xref:printing.adoc[Printing].

== Working with others

* Raster effects merge exactly as vector effects do (xref:live-effects.adoc[Live vector
  effects]): concurrent additions both stay, concurrent edits to different options both
  apply, the same option edited twice keeps the later value and shows the other in the merge
  review.
* The document raster effects resolution is one shared setting.  If two of you change it at
  once the later value wins for everyone, and the other value is listed in the merge review
  after an offline session.  A change by someone else redraws every raster effect on your
  screen; on a large document you will notice a moment of redraw.
* An object's own resolution override is part of the object and travels with copies of it.
* Your *Raster effect preview* preference is not shared.  If a collaborator's screen looks
  crisper or blurrier than yours, compare that setting before comparing documents.

=== Merge semantics

Effects:: As on xref:live-effects.adoc[Live vector effects]: sequence elements, STRUCT
settings, LWW per field, `style` changes concurrent with option edits keep both.

Document resolution:: `RasterEffectSettings.resolution_ppi` and `optimal_cmyk` are two
registers on the settings node.  Concurrent writes: later wins.  Because a lost write here is
"the user's visible work changed under them" only in a weak sense (the artwork is the same, the
pixels differ), the review sheet lists it under a *Document settings* row rather than as an
object conflict, with *Use mine* re-asserting the value.

Object override:: `raster_dpi` is a register on the appearance; nothing special.

Read-time normalizations::
* `resolution_ppi` of 0 (never written) reads as 72.
* An out-of-range `radius`, `offset` or `width` clamps to its documented range for rendering.
* `style` unspecified renders as no effect, panel shows "Unsupported effect".

=== Offline behavior

Nothing beyond the general rules.  Raster effect bitmaps are never stored in the document or
uploaded; every client renders its own.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.  The server never rasterizes.

=== Client

Package:: `WTRender/Raster/`: `RasterEffectStage`, one `CIFilter` graph builder per effect
kind, `RasterEffectCache`.  Document sheet and Object panel options item in `WTApp`.

Raster stage:: The effect pipeline (FX-006) splits a chain at each raster effect.  The stage:

. Takes the vector result so far (geometry plus fills and strokes) and the effect's *spread*
  (blur radius, shadow offset + softness, bevel width, glow width) and computes the bitmap
  rect: object bounds expanded by the spread, in pasteboard points.
. Renders the vector result into a `CGContext` at `ppi = raster_dpi ?? document ppi`, scaled by
  the preview preference for on-screen rendering (`min(screen ppi × zoom, document ppi)` for
  *Screen resolution*; document ppi for *Document resolution*; half screen for *Draft*).
  Printing and export always use the document/object ppi.
. Applies the `CIFilter` graph: bevels and embosses are a height-field from the alpha channel
  (distance transform, `width`) lit with `CIShadedMaterial`-style shading at `angle` with
  `contrast`, blurred by `softness`, shaped by the edge-shape curve and tinted by preset;
  blur is `CIBoxBlur` / `CIGaussianBlur`; shadows are alpha extraction → offset along `angle`
  → gaussian by `softness` → tint → composite under (drop) or clipped inside (inner);
  glows are the same with a dilation of `offset` and no displacement; sharpen is
  `CISharpenLuminance` / `CIUnsharpMask`.
. With `optimal_cmyk`, the working color space is the document's CMYK profile and the CI
  context is created with that working space; otherwise the document RGB working space.
. Hands the bitmap plus remaining vector chain back to the pipeline.  Vector effects after the
  raster stage operate on the object's outline for geometry and the bitmap is transformed
  with them (a Transform effect after a Blur moves the blurred pixels).

Cache:: Keyed by `(node id, appearance state hash, ppi, color settings hash)`.  REND-004's dirty
mapping evicts the entry for any change to the node's appearance, transform or geometry, and
*all* entries when `RasterEffectSettings` or the preview preference changes.  Entries are
`CIImage`s backed by Metal textures; memory is capped (256 MiB default) with LRU eviction.

Rendering off-thread:: Core Image work runs on the render queue (REND-001), never the main
actor; a tile whose raster stage is pending draws the vector result with a "rendering" badge and
is repainted when the bitmap is ready.

Output:: `WTInterchange` receives, per effected object, the final bitmap at document ppi and
its placement rect plus a clip path where the effect is interior; PDF output embeds it as an
image XObject with SMask.  Spot colors in the bitmap are converted through the document
profile; the object's vector parts keep their spot separations.

Undo:: Same grouping as vector effects; the document resolution sheet's btn:[OK] is one change
labelled "Change raster effects resolution".

NOTE: As built by FX-009, FX-010 and FX-011 (`WTRender/Effects/Raster/`: `RasterEffectStage`,
`RasterFilters`, `RasterEffectCache`, `RasterImage`, `RasterPixels`).  *Core Image*: one
`CIContext` renders each graph into a CPU bitmap (RGBA floats), not into an `MTLTexture` through
`CIContext(mtlDevice:)`; the result reaches both renderers as a *device layer* -- a bitmap
aligned with the destination's pixels in which the raster images are resampled bilinearly per
pixel -- which Core Graphics copies pixel for pixel and the Metal renderer composites as a
texture, the per-item fallback the ATTR paints use (docs/spec/client.adoc), so the two agree by
construction.  Core Image runs the blurs (`CIBoxBlur`, `CIGaussianBlur` with σ = the radius),
`CIMorphologyMaximum`, the compositing and `CISharpenLuminance`; the distance fields (the exact
Euclidean transform on the pixel grid), bevel lighting, the Unsharp threshold arithmetic and
Feather are loops over the same buffer, having no Core Image filter.  *Units*: Blur and Unsharp
radii are pixels at the object's resolution, so a blur keeps its physical size at every preview
resolution; offsets, widths and the feather radius are points; Shadow softness is the blur's
reach in points (σ = softness/3); Bevel softness blurs the height field with σ = softness × width
/ 20 (at least 0.75 px); the spread adds 1 pt.  *Bevel*: the height field is the edge shape of
`distance / width` inside the outline (outside it for Outer bevel); edge shapes: Flat x, Smooth
sin(πx/2), Sloped smoothstep, Frame 1 a step with a ledge at ½, Frame 2 a ridge then a groove,
Ring sin(πx), Ruffle x + ¼ sin(4πx); the light is at `angle` 45° above the page; highlight and
shadow strengths per preset: Raised 1/1, Highlighted 1.5/0.6, Inset 1/1 inverted, Inverted 0.6/1.5
inverted; contrast scales both.  Inner bevels shade the object, Outer bevels draw a shaded rim in
the rim colour under it, embosses leave only the relief.  *Cache*: keyed by the raster node's
value (its content in pasteboard space, operations and settings) and the resolution, LRU under a
256 MiB byte cap; an edit changes the key, so the old entry ages out; `removeAll()` is for a change
of the document resolution or the preview preference (WTApp calls it).  *Optimal CMYK* renders
without colour management (no working or output space) until the CMS epic supplies a CMYK
working space.  *Off-thread*: with `rasterEffectsReady` set, a miss renders on a background queue
and the tile draws the vector content with the badge -- a grey disc six device pixels across at
the content's top-right corner, also drawn for *Off* -- until the callback names the rectangle to
repaint.  *PDF*: raster images are placed at the object's resolution with their own alpha (no clip
path for interior effects).  The reviewed screenshots of FX-010 are the golden renders
(`WTRenderTests/Goldens/effects*`), not images under `docs/images`.

NOTE: As built by FX-012 (`WTInterchange/Flatten/Flattener.swift`, `PDF/PDFWriter.swift`,
`DXF/DXFExporter.swift`).  The hook is the export flattener (IO-017), which receives each effected
object from the snapshot's display list; no bitmap is handed over by WTRender, the flattener
renders it with WTRender's Core Graphics renderer at *Document resolution*.  An object whose raster
effects the target cannot keep live (every target but SVG's blur, drop shadow and glow filters) is
rendered at the object's raster effects resolution (`Appearance.raster`, the document's or its own)
rather than the export's raster resolution, and only what must be pixels becomes pixels: an effect
on one fill or stroke renders that element alone, the others staying vector; when every active
object effect lies under the object (drop shadow, glow, outer bevel) the image holds the effect's
pixels alone -- rendered with and without the effects on one grid and separated as S = (F − C) /
(1 − a~C~) in premultiplied colour -- beneath the vector object; when every one lies inside it
(inner shadow, inner glow, inner bevel, embosses, sharpen) and the object has no stroke, the image
is clipped by the object's outline; anything else renders the whole object with its own alpha.
The export summary names each object ("raster effects of “Card” rendered at 150 ppi").  Spot
colours: the pixels hold WTRender's process alternates, and PDF writes spot colours of the vector
parts as `/Separation` colour spaces with a DeviceCMYK tint transform (*Preserve spot colors*, on by
default and kept by PDF/X-1a; off or under *Convert to RGB* the alternates are written), so a blurred
fill lands on the process plates and its stroke on the spot plate (checked with Ghostscript's
`tiffsep` device).  EPS still writes no separations.  Bitmap exports already render raster effects at
*Document resolution* and resample them into the export resolution; DXF, which holds no images,
drops raster effects and lists the objects ("raster effects left out (DXF holds no images) on “Logo”,
2 unnamed objects").  The PDF drop-shadow case is held to the reference renderer's *Document
resolution* output (at most 0.2% of pixels beyond 24/255 at 2×); printing from Preview goes through
the same Core Graphics PDF renderer and was not separately recorded.
