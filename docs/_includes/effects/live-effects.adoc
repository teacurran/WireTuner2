A live effect changes how an object looks without touching the object itself.  The path you
drew stays exactly as you drew it; the effect is an attribute stacked on top of it, next to its
strokes and fills, and you can adjust it, reorder it or remove it at any time.  {product} has
two families of live effects.  *Vector effects* -- Bend, Combine, Corners, Duet, Expand Path,
Ragged, Sketch and Transform -- produce new vector outlines, so they stay crisp at any zoom and
on any printer.  *Raster effects* -- bevels, blurs, shadows, glows and sharpening -- produce
pixels and are covered in xref:raster-effects.adoc[Live raster effects]; the Transparency
effect has its own page, xref:transparency.adoc[Transparency].  This page covers where effects
live, how you manage them, and every option of the eight vector effects.

The tools and commands that reshape a path permanently (Roughen, Fisheye Lens, the Bend tool,
Smudge, Emboss and their relatives) are on xref:path-effects.adoc[Path-shaping tools and
operations].  Where a live effect and a destructive tool do the same thing -- Bend, Expand Path,
Ragged versus Roughen -- prefer the effect when you may want to change your mind later, and the
tool when you need the resulting points to edit by hand.

== Effects in the Object panel

Effects are attributes of an object, like its strokes and fills.  The Object panel
(menu:Window[Object], kbd:[Cmd+3]) shows the selected object's *appearance list* in its upper
half: the object itself at the top, then each stroke, fill and effect in the order they are
applied.  Selecting an item in the list shows its settings in the lower half of the panel.  The
appearance list is described in full in xref:attribute-stack.adoc[The attribute stack];
xref:object-panel.adoc[The Object panel] describes the panel itself.

// TODO screenshot: Object panel with an object carrying a fill, a stroke and a Ragged effect selected

An effect can be attached at three levels:

The object:: The effect applies to the object as a whole -- to the combined result of all its
strokes and fills.  A Ragged effect on the object roughens every stroke and fill together.
A fill:: The effect applies to that fill only.  A Transform effect on a fill lets you offset or
scale the fill while the stroke stays put, a Bend on a fill bends the fill under an unbent
stroke.
A stroke:: Likewise for a single stroke.

Order matters.  Effects are applied from the top of the list downward, each working on the
result of the one above it: a Bend followed by a Ragged roughens the bent shape, whereas a
Ragged followed by a Bend bends the roughened one.  Drag an effect up or down the list to change
the order.  Effects attached to a fill or stroke sit indented under it and only reorder among
that fill's or stroke's effects.

To add a live effect:

. Select the object on the canvas, or select a fill or stroke in the Object panel's appearance
  list to attach the effect to that attribute alone.
. Click btn:[Add Effect] at the top of the Object panel and choose an effect from the menu.
  Vector effects are listed first, then the raster effects in their submenus, then
  Transparency.
. Adjust the effect's settings in the lower half of the panel.  Every change previews on the
  canvas as you make it.

To change which effect an item is:

. Select the effect in the appearance list.
. Choose a different effect from the *Effect* pop-up menu at the top of the settings area.  The
  new effect starts with its default settings.

To remove a live effect:

. Select the effect in the appearance list.
. Click btn:[Remove Item] (the minus button), or press kbd:[Delete].

To reorder effects:

* Drag the effect up or down in the appearance list.  A horizontal line shows where it will
  land; dropping it onto a fill or stroke attaches it to that attribute.

To hide an effect without removing it:

* Click the eye icon beside the effect in the appearance list.  A hidden effect keeps its
  settings and is skipped when the object is drawn, printed or exported.  Click again to show
  it.

TIP: Effects are copied with the object, are part of graphic styles
(xref:styles.adoc[Styles]), and can be pasted onto other objects with
menu:Edit[Paste Attributes] (xref:default-attributes.adoc[Default attributes]).

=== Effects and the rest of the object

* Hit testing uses the effected outline: with an Expand Path effect, clicking anywhere in the
  widened band selects the path.  The object's own points and handles are still where you
  drew them, and that is where the Pointer and Subselect tools show them.
* Bounds shown in the Object panel and used for alignment are the object's own, not the
  effected result.  Choose menu:View[Show Effect Bounds] to see the effected bounds instead.
* Transforming an object with the transformation tools transforms its effects with it: a Bend's
  centre point rotates along with the object, an Expand Path width scales with a uniform scale.
* Text can carry effects as a text block; a paragraph or a run cannot.  The effect is applied
  to the laid-out glyph outlines (xref:text-effects.adoc[Text effects]).
* Effects on a group apply to the group as one shape.  Ungrouping drops group-level effects
  after a confirmation.  The Combine effect (below) only makes sense on a group and is the
  one effect that changes how the group's members are drawn.
* Keyline view (menu:View[Keyline]) draws the object's own outline, not its effects.

== Bend

Bend pulls every point of the outline toward or away from a centre point, which bloats the
shape into a cushion or pinches it into a star.  It is the live counterpart of the Bend tool.

To apply a Bend effect:

. Add the effect, or select an existing effect and choose *Bend* from the Effect pop-up menu.
. Set its options:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |What it does

|*Size*
|How far the outline moves, in points.  Positive values push the outline away from the centre
(bloat); negative values pull it toward the centre (pinch).  `0` leaves the shape unchanged.

|*Center X*, *Center Y*
|The centre of the distortion, in the document's units, relative to the object's own centre.
You can also drag the centre handle on the canvas while the effect is selected in the Object
panel.
|===

== Combine

Combine turns a group into one shape by adding, subtracting, intersecting or excluding the
outlines of its members -- the same operations as menu:Modify[Combine] on
xref:combining-paths.adoc[Combining paths], but live.  The members stay separate, editable
objects inside the group: move a circle and the union follows it; reshape a point and the hole
changes shape.  The group draws with its *own* fills and strokes, as if it were a single path,
and the members' fills and strokes are not drawn while the effect is on.  Add the effect to a
group; on a single object it does nothing.

To combine a group live:

. Select the group, or select the objects and group them (menu:Modify[Group], kbd:[Cmd+G]).
. Add the effect, or choose *Combine* from the Effect pop-up menu.
. Give the group a fill and a stroke in the Object panel.  They apply to the combined shape.
. Choose the operation:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |What it does

|*Union*
|Everything any member covers, as one outline.

|*Subtract*
|The bottom member with every member above it cut away.  Stacking order decides which member is
the base; reorder in the Layers panel or with menu:Modify[Arrange].

|*Intersect*
|Only the area every member covers.

|*Exclude*
|The areas covered by an odd number of members: where two members overlap becomes a hole.

|btn:[Expand]
|Replaces the group with an ordinary path of the combined outline, keeping the group's fills,
strokes and other effects.  This is what menu:Modify[Combine] would have produced; afterwards
the members are gone and the shape is edited point by point.  Undo brings the group back.
|===

Members can be paths, rectangles and ellipses, text (its glyph outlines are used), symbol
instances and nested groups; a nested group with its own Combine effect is combined first, then
takes part as one shape.  Open paths and members with no closed outline contribute nothing.
Other effects on the group apply to the combined shape, so a Combine followed by a Ragged
roughens one outline, not each member.

== Corners

Corners rounds, scoops or bevels the corners of any path -- a star, a polygon, a hand-drawn
shape, a rectangle -- without adding or moving points.  The path keeps its corner points where
they are; the effect draws each corner as an arc or a cut of the radius you choose.  You can
treat every corner of the path or only the points you select, and adjust corners by dragging
on the canvas as well as from the Object panel.

To round corners by dragging:

. Choose the Subselect tool and click the path.  A small round widget appears just inside each
  corner point -- each point where two segments meet at an angle.  Curve points have no widget.
. Drag any widget toward the inside of the shape.  With no points selected, every corner
  follows; with some points selected, only those change.  The first drag adds a Corners effect
  to the object; later drags adjust it.
. kbd:[Option]-click a widget to cycle its style, or double-click it to show the effect in the
  Object panel.

Choose menu:View[Show Corner Widgets] to hide or show the widgets.

To set corners in the Object panel:

. Add the effect, or choose *Corners* from the Effect pop-up menu.
. Set its options:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |What it does

|*Radius*
|The corner radius in points.  A radius too large for a corner -- one whose rounding would
reach past the middle of either neighboring segment -- is reduced for that corner only, so
corners never overlap.

|*Style*
|*Round* draws a convex arc; *Inverted round* scoops the corner out with a concave arc;
*Chamfer* cuts it off with a straight line.

|*Corners*
|*All* treats every corner of the path.  *Selected points* treats only the points you had
selected with the Subselect tool when you chose it; btn:[Add Selection] and btn:[Remove
Selection] change that set later, and dragging a widget while points are selected does the same.
|===

// TODO screenshot: a star with Round, Inverted round and Chamfer corners side by side, widgets visible

Rectangles drawn with the Rectangle tool have their own corner radius
(xref:rectangles-ellipses-lines.adoc[Rectangles, ellipses and lines]), and that field stays the
quickest way to round a rectangle.  Add a Corners effect to a rectangle when you want inverted
or chamfered corners, or want the rounding to be one of several effects.  While a rectangle
carries a Corners effect the effect takes over: the rectangle's own radius is ignored, its
*Radius* field in the Object panel is dimmed with a note saying so, and the corner handles drag
the effect's radius instead.  Remove the effect and the rectangle's own radius comes back.

== Duet

Duet clones the shape one or more times, rotated around or reflected across a centre point, and
draws the original and its clones as one compound shape.  It is the fastest way to turn a single
motif into a rosette or a symmetrical emblem while keeping only one path to edit.

To apply a Duet effect:

. Add the effect, or choose *Duet* from the Effect pop-up menu.
. Set its options:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |What it does

|*Reflect*
|Draws one mirror image of the shape.  The mirror axis passes through the centre point; drag
the centre handle or edit *Center X*/*Center Y* to move the axis, and rotate it by dragging the
handle's arm.

|*Rotate*
|Draws *Copies* duplicates evenly rotated around the centre point.  Two copies give a 180°
pair, six copies a hexagonal rosette.

|*Center X*, *Center Y*
|The centre of rotation or the point the mirror axis passes through, relative to the object's
centre.

|*Copies*
|Number of clones for *Rotate*, 1--100.  Ignored for *Reflect*, which always makes one.

|*Joined*
|Connects the original and its clones into one continuous path instead of leaving them as
separate contours.  Use it when a stroke should run unbroken from one copy to the next.

|*Closed*
|With *Joined* off, closes each clone.  With *Joined* on, closes the single combined path.

|*Even/Odd fill*
|Where clones overlap, alternate filled and unfilled areas instead of filling the union.
|===

== Expand Path

Expand Path draws the outline a stroke *would* have if the path were stroked at a given width,
and treats that outline as the object's shape -- so a two-point line becomes a long closed
rectangle you can fill with a gradient, and a closed shape grows a band around it.  The path
itself keeps its points.  Compare menu:Modify[Alter Path > Expand Stroke]
(xref:expand-stroke.adoc[Expanding a stroke]), which replaces the path with the outline.

To apply an Expand Path effect:

. Add the effect, or choose *Expand Path* from the Effect pop-up menu.
. Set its options:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |What it does

|*Direction*
|*Inside* expands only into a closed shape, *Outside* only outward, *Both* on both sides of
the path (the way a stroke sits).  Open paths always expand on both sides.

|*Width*
|The total width of the band, 0--50 points.

|*Cap*
|How an open path's ends are finished: *Butt* stops at the end point, *Round* adds a
semicircle, *Square* extends past the end by half the width.

|*Join*
|How corners are drawn: *Miter* (sharp), *Round*, or *Bevel* (cut off).

|*Miter limit*
|1--57.  A mitered corner sharper than this ratio of miter length to width is drawn beveled
instead, so acute corners do not spike.
|===

== Ragged

Ragged adds points along the outline and moves them in and out by a random amount, for a torn,
hand-cut or wobbly look.  Unlike the Roughen tool the randomness is *stable*: the effect looks
the same every time the document is opened, on every Mac, and the same on the printed page.

To apply a Ragged effect:

. Add the effect, or choose *Ragged* from the Effect pop-up menu.
. Set its options:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |What it does

|*Size*
|The furthest an added point may stray from the original outline, in points.

|*Frequency*
|How many points are added per inch of outline.  Higher values give finer, busier edges.

|*Copies*
|How many additional randomized outlines are drawn on top of the original, 0--10.  Several
copies of a thin stroke give a scribbled look.

|*Rough*
|Added points are corner points: a jagged, torn edge.

|*Smooth*
|Added points are curve points: a wavy, hand-drawn edge.

|*Uniform*
|Every added point moves by exactly *Size*, alternating in and out, instead of by a random
amount.  Produces a regular zigzag or scallop.

|*Reseed*
|Picks a new random pattern.  The pattern is stored with the effect, so everyone sees the same
one until you click this again.
|===

== Sketch

Sketch redraws the outline as if with a pencil: several slightly wrong copies of the outline,
each offset by a random amount, drawn with the object's stroke.  Like Ragged, the randomness is
stored so the drawing is stable.

To apply a Sketch effect:

. Add the effect, or choose *Sketch* from the Effect pop-up menu.
. Set its options:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |What it does

|*Amount*
|How far the sketched strokes may wander from the outline, in points.  Larger values look
looser.

|*Copies*
|How many pencil strokes are drawn, 1--20.

|*Closed*
|Joins the ends of each sketched stroke so it forms a closed loop; off, each stroke starts and
ends a little short, like a real pencil pass.

|*Reseed*
|Picks a new random pattern.
|===

== Transform

Transform scales, skews, rotates and moves the object's appearance, optionally repeated as a
chain of copies, without changing the object's actual geometry.  The transformation tools on
xref:transforming.adoc[Transforming objects] do the same things to the object itself; use the
effect when you want the original to stay put -- for example to give a fill a nudged, scaled
duplicate as a fake shadow, or to build a spiral of copies from one shape.

To apply a Transform effect:

. Add the effect, or choose *Transform* from the Effect pop-up menu.
. Set its options.  They are applied in this order: scale, skew, rotate, move, all about the
  centre point:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |What it does

|*Scale X*, *Scale Y*
|Percentages; 100 is unchanged.  *Uniform* locks Y to X.

|*Skew H*, *Skew V*
|Skew angles in degrees.  Positive horizontal skew leans the top to the right; positive
vertical skew raises the right side.

|*Rotate*
|Degrees; positive is counterclockwise.

|*Move X*, *Move Y*
|Offset in the document's units.  Positive X moves right, positive Y moves up.

|*Center X*, *Center Y*
|The centre of scaling, skewing and rotation, relative to the object's centre.  Positive X and
Y move the centre right and up.  A centre handle on the canvas can be dragged instead.

|*Copies*
|`1` transforms the original only.  Higher values draw that many copies, each transformed once
more than the one before it -- with *Rotate* 30° and *Copies* 12 you get a full ring, with
*Scale* 90% and *Move* you get a receding trail.
|===

NOTE: Scale, skew and rotate compose in the fixed order above, so two people adjusting
different fields of the same Transform effect at once get a predictable result (see below).

== Working with others

Live effects are attributes of the object, so collaborating on them works the way collaborating
on strokes and fills does.

* When someone else adds, removes, reorders or adjusts an effect on an object you are looking
  at, the canvas and the Object panel update within a moment.  If the Object panel is showing
  that object, the list refreshes in place; a field you are typing into keeps your keystrokes.
* If two of you add effects to the same object at the same time, both effects end up on the
  object, one above the other.  Whoever's arrived later at the server sits lower in the list;
  drag to reorder if that is not what you wanted.
* If you both change the same setting of the same effect -- two Bend sizes -- the later change
  wins and the other is offered back in the merge review after a long offline stretch.  Changes
  to *different* settings of the same effect both survive: your Size and their Center both
  apply.
* If someone removes an effect you are adjusting, your adjustment lands on the removed effect
  and the effect disappears.  After an offline session the merge review lists it as an edit of
  something that was deleted and offers to restore it.
* Reseeding a Ragged or Sketch effect changes what everyone sees, because the random pattern is
  part of the document.
* Corners: if you and a collaborator each add different points to a Corners effect's *Selected
  points*, both sets are kept.  If you both change its radius, the later change wins.  If
  someone deletes a point you had rounded, that corner simply goes with the point; the rest of
  the effect is unchanged, and undoing the deletion brings the rounded corner back.
* Combine: the members are ordinary objects, so someone can reshape a member while you switch
  the operation, and both changes show.  If someone clicks btn:[Expand] while you are editing
  a member, your edit lands on the group that was just replaced by a path; live, it disappears
  with the group, and after an offline session the merge review lists it with *Restore*.

After being offline, all of this is merged automatically; nothing about effects needs the
network.

=== Data model

The appearance stack (xref:attribute-stack.adoc[The attribute stack]) reserves
`repeated Effect effects = …` with `MERGE_SEQUENCE`.  This page defines `Effect` and the vector
effect messages; xref:raster-effects.adoc[Live raster effects] and
xref:transparency.adoc[Transparency] define the rest.

[source,protobuf]

// proto/wiretuner/doc/v1/effects.proto (sketch)

// One entry in Appearance.effects (MERGE_SEQUENCE: inserted, moved, deleted individually;
// order = fractional position).  Kind-specific settings are plain STRUCT fields rather than a
// oneof: `kind` selects which one renders, the others are retained.  A oneof would turn
// "change this effect's type" into a write that discards every concurrent edit to the old
// settings, and would need a rule for two replicas setting different cases.
message Effect {
  ElementId id = 1;              // required by MERGE_SEQUENCE
  bool hidden = 2;               // eye toggle: kept but not rendered/printed/exported
  // Fill or stroke element (in the same Appearance) this effect applies to.  Unset: the whole
  // object.  ATOMIC by default.  Reads as unset when the element is deleted (see Merge).
  ElementId attached_to = 3;
  EffectKind kind = 4;           // which settings message is live
  BendEffect bend = 10;
  DuetEffect duet = 11;
  ExpandPathEffect expand_path = 12;
  RaggedEffect ragged = 13;
  SketchEffect sketch = 14;
  TransformEffect transform = 15;
  CornersEffect corners = 16;
  CombineEffect combine = 17;
  BevelEmbossEffect bevel_emboss = 20;   // raster-effects.adoc
  BlurEffect blur = 21;
  ShadowEffect shadow = 22;
  SharpenEffect sharpen = 23;
  TransparencyEffect transparency = 24;  // transparency.adoc
}

  EFFECT_KIND_DUET = 2;

  EFFECT_KIND_SKETCH = 5;
  EFFECT_KIND_TRANSFORM = 6;
  EFFECT_KIND_BEVEL_EMBOSS = 7;
  EFFECT_KIND_BLUR = 8;
  EFFECT_KIND_SHADOW = 9;
  EFFECT_KIND_SHARPEN = 10;

  double scale_y = 2;
  bool uniform = 3;              // UI lock only; when true scale_y is written equal to scale_x
  double skew_h = 4;             // degrees
  double skew_v = 5;
  double rotate = 6;             // degrees, counterclockwise positive
  Point move = 7 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];     // document units, y up

// Rounds, scoops or chamfers corner points of the effected outline without changing them.
message CornersEffect {
  double radius = 1 [(buf.validate.field).double.gte = 0];   // points; clamped per corner on read
  CornerStyle style = 2;
  // The PathPoint element ids this effect treats.  SET (add-wins): two people choosing
  // different points both keep theirs.  Empty = every eligible corner of the path.  A member
  // whose point is deleted, is a curve point, or belongs to another node drops out on read;
  // the set is never rewritten for it, so restoring the point restores the corner.  On a
  // `rect` node the set is ignored: a rectangle's corners have no element ids and all four
  // are treated.  (MERGE_SET over ElementId members, compared by value -- crdt-model.)
  repeated ElementId points = 3 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_SET];
}

  CORNER_STYLE_ROUND = 0;            // convex arc
  CORNER_STYLE_INVERTED_ROUND = 1;   // concave scoop
  CORNER_STYLE_CHAMFER = 2;          // straight cut
}

// node; anywhere else it renders as no effect (see Merge).  Operand order = stacking order,
// bottom member first.
message CombineEffect {
  BooleanOp op = 1;
}

enum BooleanOp {
  BOOLEAN_OP_UNION = 0;
  BOOLEAN_OP_SUBTRACT = 1;           // bottom member minus every member above it
  BOOLEAN_OP_INTERSECT = 2;
  BOOLEAN_OP_EXCLUDE = 3;            // regions covered an odd number of times
}

`LineCap` and `LineJoin` are the enums xref:stroke-attributes.adoc[Stroke attributes] defines;
`Point` is from `common.proto`.  `CornersEffect` and `CombineEffect` are part of the initial
effect vocabulary, not a later kind, so they do not bump the feature level.

Adding a *new effect kind* later bumps the document's feature level
(xref:crdt-model.adoc#evolution[Schema evolution]): an older client that meets an unknown
`EffectKind` renders the object without that effect and opens read-only.

=== Merge semantics

Sequence:: `Appearance.effects` is a SEQUENCE, so adding, removing, hiding, reordering and
editing effects are independent registers.  Two concurrent *Add Effect* calls on one object
produce two elements; their relative order is their fractional positions (both appended after
the same last element, so the tie is broken by element id -- deterministic, and shown to the
user as "later arrival lower").  Concurrent reorder and edit both keep.

`kind` vs. settings:: A change of `kind` (ATOMIC enum) concurrent with an edit of the old
kind's settings keeps both: the object renders the new kind, and the old settings are retained
invisibly in their own STRUCT.  Switching back later shows the concurrent edit.  Nothing is
lost, nothing is flagged.

`attached_to`:: ATOMIC.  If the fill or stroke element it names is deleted, or is an element of
a different sequence, the effect *reads as unset*: no, it does not jump to the object level --
an effect attached to a deleted fill is skipped exactly as the fill is.  (`ElementId` is not a
`NodeRef`, so this is a read-time normalization declared here, not an `on_dangling` option.)
Dropping an effect onto a fill in the Object panel writes `attached_to` and moves the element's
position to the fill's group; a concurrent reorder within the old group is overridden by the
later write of `position`, and the effect always renders under whichever `attached_to` reads.

Same setting, two writers:: Plain LWW per field.  The review sheet lists the object with the
losing value beside the winning one ("Bend size: mine 12 pt, theirs 18 pt") and offers *Use
mine* / *Use theirs*; *Keep both copies* duplicates the whole object.

Edit vs. remove:: An `ElementDelete` and a concurrent `SetFields` on the same element both
apply; the element stays deleted.  Reviewed as "edited something that was deleted" with
*Restore*, which re-inserts the element (it is a tombstone with its registers intact).

Seeds:: `seed` is a register like any other.  Two concurrent reseeds: later wins; the losing
seed is not worth reviewing and is filtered out of the review sheet (it is listed under
"Reviewed automatically" only).

Corners:: `radius` and `style` are LWW registers (*same register* in the review when both
sides wrote one).  `points` is a SET: concurrent adds of different points keep both; a
concurrent add and remove of the same point keeps it, which is what the person adding expects
to see.  A point deleted from the contour concurrently -- or at any later time -- drops out of
the set on read and returns if the point is restored; nothing is rewritten, so the outcome does
not depend on who saw what.  A Corners effect on a `rect` takes precedence over
`RectProps.corners` on read: the derived path is built with zero radii and the kernel treats
all four corners.  The rectangle's own registers are untouched and read again when the effect
is removed or hidden (xref:rectangles-ellipses-lines.adoc[Rectangles, ellipses and lines]
lists the same rule from its side).

Combine:: `op` is LWW.  The members are ordinary children of the group and merge as objects
do; the effect reads whatever the group holds after the merge, so a member added concurrently
joins the boolean and a member deleted leaves it.  *Expand* is one change: `CreateNode` of a
`path` at the group's slot under its parent, carrying the combined outline in pasteboard space
with identity transform and a copy of the group's appearance stack minus the Combine element,
then `SetDeleted(true)` on the group.  Concurrent edits to members land under a deleted node:
*edit vs delete* in the review, with *Restore* bringing the live group back beside the path --
the same rule as xref:combining-paths.adoc[Combining paths].  Two concurrent Expands: two paths,
the group deleted twice, nothing at the register level; either can be undone.

Read-time normalizations::
* `kind` unspecified or unknown → the element renders as no effect and the Object panel shows
  it as "Unsupported effect (update {product})".
* `attached_to` dangling → skipped (above).
* Out-of-range values (a `width` of 80 from a hand-built change) are clamped to the documented
  range when rendering; the panel shows the stored value in red.
* A `seed` of 0 renders with seed 1 so that a document built by an older writer is stable.
* `CornersEffect.points` members that are dangling, curve points, or elements of another node
  are ignored; a `radius` whose tangent length would pass the midpoint of either adjoining
  segment is reduced for that corner only.
* A `CombineEffect` on a node that is not a `group`, or attached to a fill or stroke, renders
  as no effect; the Object panel shows "Combine applies to groups".

=== Offline behavior

Nothing beyond the general rules.  Dragging a Bend centre for a minute coalesces to one op
(same register, same replica).  A Reseed while offline is one op.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

Package:: Effect rendering lives in `WTRender` (`EffectPipeline`), the vector kernels in
`WTGeometry` (`Effects/` directory: `BendKernel`, `CombineKernel`, `CornersKernel`,
`DuetKernel`, `ExpandKernel`, `RaggedKernel`, `SketchKernel`, `TransformKernel`).  The Object
panel editors live in `WTApp/Inspector/Effects`.

Pipeline:: For each object, the display list builder resolves the appearance into a list of
*drawables* (fill or stroke plus its attached effects), then applies object-level effects to the
union.  A vector effect is a function `(outline geometry, settings) → outline geometry`; a
chain of vector effects is composed lazily and cached per `(node id, appearance state hash)`
in the same cache that REND-004 invalidates.  Raster effects in the chain break it into
vector-before / raster / vector-after stages; see xref:raster-effects.adoc[Live raster effects]
for the raster stage.  Hidden effects are skipped without breaking the chain.

Kernels::
* *Bend*: for each point at distance `d` from the centre, displace along the radial by
  `size × (1 − d/d_max)` where `d_max` is the farthest point; control handles are displaced
  with their anchors and re-scaled so the curve stays smooth.  Same kernel as the Bend tool
  with the drag distance replacing `size`.
* *Duet*: builds clones with `WTGeometry` affine transforms, optionally joins contours end to
  start (`joined`) and closes; `even_odd` sets the fill rule of the resulting multi-contour
  path.
* *Expand Path*: GEO-003 offsetting; *Inside* uses the negative offset of a closed contour,
  *Outside* the positive, *Both* the stroke outline.
* *Ragged*: samples the outline by arc length at `1/frequency` inch intervals, displaces each
  sample by a `SplitMix64` stream seeded with `seed` (identical in every replica and on the
  Java side, which never renders but is documented so the seed contract is unambiguous), and
  rebuilds corner or curve points.
* *Sketch*: `copies` traversals of the outline with per-copy seeded noise, drawn as open (or
  closed) strokes with the object's stroke attributes.
* *Transform*: matrix chain per copy, `copies` drawables appended.
* *Corners*: for each eligible point (a corner or connector point whose adjoining segments are
  not collinear, filtered by `points`), the tangent length is `t = radius / tan(θ/2)` for
  straight neighbors and the same distance measured by arc length along a curved neighbor,
  where the neighbor is split with GEO-001's de Casteljau split; `t` is capped at half the
  neighbor's length.  *Round* replaces the corner with a circular arc through the two tangent
  points (one cubic per ≤ 90° of sweep, kappa as in `rectPath`); *Inverted round* uses the
  arc with its centre on the far side of the corner; *Chamfer* joins the tangent points with
  a line.  A `rect` node enters the kernel as its zero-radius derived path.
* *Combine*: members are flattened to pasteboard space through their transform chains
  (as OBJ-025 does for the command), each member's *effected* outline is taken (nested Combine
  groups first, text as laid-out glyph outlines, instances as their artwork, open contours
  dropped), and the operands are folded in stacking order with GEO-002: `union`, `punch` for
  Subtract, `intersect`, and Exclude as union minus intersection.  The result carries the
  group's fill rule.

Combine in the pipeline:: When the display-list builder meets a `group` whose object-level
effects include a live, unhidden Combine, it emits no member drawables; it emits the group's
own fills, strokes and remaining effects over the combined outline, cached per
`(group node id, subtree version, effect state hash)` so a member drag recomputes one boolean,
not the members' own lists.  Hit testing treats the group as one shape via the combined
outline; the Subselect tool still reaches members through the group as usual.

Hit testing:: REND-003 tests against the effected outline for fill/stroke hits and against the
raw path for point and segment hits.  Bounds reported to the spatial index are the effected
bounds, which are also what REND-004 dirties.

Controls:: Effect settings are SwiftUI forms in the Object panel host with `NSStepper`-style
numeric fields; the Bend, Duet and Transform centre handles and the Duet axis arm are canvas
overlays drawn by the tool layer while the effect is selected in the panel.

Corner widgets:: Drawn by the Subselect tool for the selected path's eligible corners, on each
corner's bisector at the current arc midpoint (a fixed 8 px inset when the radius is 0), and
hit-tested after points and handles.  A drag maps distance along the bisector to `radius`; with
no points selected it writes `radius` (and `points` to empty if the effect is new), with points
selected it adds the selection to `points`.  kbd:[Option]-click cycles `style`, double-click
selects the effect in the Object panel.  On a `rect` carrying a Corners effect, DRAW-009's
radius handles drive the effect's `radius` instead of `RectProps.corners`.  menu:View[Show
Corner Widgets] is a view option (REND-005), on by default.

Undo:: Each field commit is one change; a canvas drag of a centre handle or a corner widget is
one undo group (APP-011).  Add, remove, reorder and kind change are single changes labelled
"Add Bend effect", "Remove effect", "Reorder effects", "Change effect to Ragged"; btn:[Expand]
is one change labelled "Expand Combine".
