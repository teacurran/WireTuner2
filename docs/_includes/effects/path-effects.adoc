The tools and commands on this page reshape objects for good.  Where a live effect
(xref:live-effects.adoc[Live vector effects]) is an attribute you can adjust or remove later,
everything here *changes the path itself*: Roughen moves the points, the Shadow tool makes a
real copy behind the object, Emboss builds real facets, the 3D Rotation tool rewrites the
outline.  That is the point of them -- the result is ordinary paths you can edit point by point
-- and it is also the caution: once applied, the only way back is Undo.

Most of these tools share pop-up slots in the Tools panel (xref:toolbars.adoc[The Tools
panel]); the commands live in the *Extensions* menu and on the *Operations* toolbar
(menu:Window[Toolbars > Operations]).

NOTE: The tools sit in the Tools panel; the commands are extensions.  See
xref:extensions.adoc[Extensions].

Every tool that has options opens them when you double-click the tool in the Tools panel.  Tool
options are remembered on your Mac between sessions and are not part of the document.

== Adding points

Adding points makes a path more detailed without changing its shape, so a later tool or effect
has more to work with -- a Roughen on a four-point rectangle can only wobble at the corners.

To add points to a selected path:

* Click btn:[Add Points] on the Operations toolbar, or choose menu:Extensions[Distort > Add
  Points].  One point is inserted at the middle of every segment.  Repeat to double again.

== Roughening

The Roughen tool adds points along a path and moves them in and out at random, for a jagged or
hand-drawn edge.  Its options set how many points and what kind; the drag sets how far.

To set Roughen options:

. Double-click the Roughen tool.
. Set *Amount*: points added per inch, 0--100.
. Set *Edge*: *Rough* uses corner points for a jagged edge; *Smooth* uses curve points for a
  wavy one.
. Click btn:[OK].

To roughen a selected path:

. Select the Roughen tool from its pop-up.
. Press on the path and drag.  Dragging further from the starting point roughens more; the
  outline previews as you drag.  Release to apply.

To roughen text, convert it to paths first (xref:text-to-paths.adoc[Converting text to
paths]).

== Fisheye lens

The Fisheye Lens tool distorts the selection as if seen through a round lens: *convex* bulges
the middle outward, *concave* sinks it.  Symmetrical shapes and grids show it best.

To set Fisheye Lens options:

. Double-click the Fisheye Lens tool.
. Set *Perspective* from −100 (fully concave) to 100 (fully convex), by field or slider.  The
  preview shows the distortion.
. Click btn:[OK].

To apply the lens:

. Select the Fisheye Lens tool from its pop-up.
. Drag over the selection to draw the lens.  The drag's start is the lens centre and its
  length the radius; kbd:[Option]-drag draws the lens from its centre outward.  Release to
  apply.

== Bending and bloating

The Bend tool spikes or bloats a shape around a centre point.  *Spiked*: the segments curve
inward while the points move outward, giving a star.  *Bloated*: the segments curve outward
while the points move inward, giving a cushion.

To set Bend tool options:

. Double-click the Bend tool.
. Set the amount of distortion, 1--10, by field or slider.
. Click btn:[OK].

To bend or bloat a selected path:

. Select the Bend tool from its pop-up.
. Press where the centre of the distortion should be.
. Drag *up* for a spiked result, *down* for a bloated one.  The further you drag, the
  stronger the distortion.  Release to apply.

== Smudging

The Smudge tool drags out a trail of fading copies behind an object, blending from the object's
own colors to the smudge colors -- a quick soft shadow or a motion trail.  The copies and the
original are grouped.  Smudging an already smudged group smudges every copy in it, so the
count multiplies; {product} refuses when a smudge would create more than 1,000 objects.

To set Smudge options:

. Double-click the Smudge tool.
. Click the *Fill* color box and choose the color the fill fades toward; click the *Stroke*
  color box for the stroke.  For a drop shadow, pick a slightly darker tint of the background
  as the fill and *None* for the stroke.
. Click btn:[OK].

To smudge a selected object:

. Select the Smudge tool from its pop-up.
. Press on the object and drag outward.  A line links the preview to the original.
* Hold kbd:[Option] while dragging to smudge outward from the centre in every direction, for a
  soft halo.
. Release to apply.

== Adding a drop shadow with the Shadow tool

The Shadow tool places a copy of the object behind it as a shadow and groups the two.  Three
kinds: *Hard Edge* (a flat copy), *Soft Edge* (a copy that fades into a background color) and
*Zoom* (a copy scaled and blended back toward the original, like a beam of light).  The tool
does not apply to bitmaps, text or clipping paths: convert text to paths first, and shadow an
object before you use it as a clipping path.  For a live shadow on anything, including text,
use the Drop Shadow effect (xref:raster-effects.adoc[Live raster effects]).

// TODO screenshot: Hard Edge, Soft Edge and Zoom shadows under the same shape

To set Shadow tool options:

. Double-click the Shadow tool.
. Choose a *Type*: Hard Edge, Soft Edge or Zoom.
. For Hard Edge and Soft Edge, choose a *Fill*:
* *Tint*: a tint of the object's own color, 0 (white) to 100%.
* *Shade*: the object's color with a percentage of black added.
* *Color*: any color from the pop-up palette.
. For Soft Edge, drag a color to *Fade to* (white by default) and set *Soft edge* from 0
  (hard) to 100 (soft throughout).
. For Zoom, set a *Stroke* color and *Fill* color.
. Set *Scale*: the shadow's size as a percentage of the object.
. Set *Offset X* and *Y*: how far the shadow sits from the object.
. Click btn:[Apply] to preview without closing, or btn:[OK].

To add a shadow to the selected objects:

. Select the Shadow tool from its pop-up.
. Click to place the shadow at the options' offset, or drag to place it by hand.  Each click
  starts again from directly under the object.

Soft Edge and Zoom shadows are built from a series of intermediate colors.  Objects colored
with spot or RGB colors produce process CMYK intermediates.

== Embossing

The Emboss operation builds a relief out of real paths: highlight and shadow facets around the
object's edges, grouped with the original.  It applies to closed paths with a basic, gradient
or pattern fill.  For a live relief use the Bevel and Emboss effects
(xref:raster-effects.adoc[Live raster effects]).

// TODO screenshot: Emboss, Deboss, Chisel, Ridge and Quilt on a rounded square

To emboss a selected object:

. Choose menu:Extensions[Create > Emboss], or click btn:[Emboss] on the Operations toolbar.
  kbd:[Cmd]-click the toolbar button to apply the previous settings without the dialog.
. Click a style at the top of the dialog: *Emboss* (raised), *Deboss* (sunken), *Chisel*,
  *Ridge* or *Quilt*.
. Choose how facets are colored from *Vary*:
* *Contrast*: highlights are tints of the object's color, shadows are its color with black
  added.
* *Colors*: highlights and shadows use the two color boxes.
. Set *Depth*: 1--72 points by field, 1--20 by slider.
. Set *Angle*: the light direction, by field or dial.
. For Emboss and Deboss, select *Soft edge* to blend the relief in steps.
. Click btn:[Apply] to preview, or btn:[OK].

Intermediate facet colors are process CMYK when the object uses spot or RGB colors.

== Fractalize

Fractalize replaces every segment with a spiked version of itself and nearly doubles the point
count each time.  Applied several times to a circle it produces a snowflake-like pattern.

To fractalize a selected object:

* Click btn:[Fractalize] on the Operations toolbar (menu:Extensions[Distort > Fractalize]).
  Apply again for more detail; watch the point count in the Object panel.

== Mirror

The Mirror tool reflects a path, text block or image across one or more axes to build
symmetrical patterns.  The whole object is mirrored even if only some of its points are
selected.

To set Mirror options:

. Double-click the Mirror tool.
. Choose an *Axis*: *Horizontal* (top to bottom), *Vertical* (left to right), *Horizontal &
  Vertical* (both), or *Multiple* (around several axes).
. For Multiple, set the number of *Axes* (1--50 by slider, 1--100 by field) and choose
  *Reflect* (each copy is mirrored) or *Rotate* (copies are rotated only).
. Select *Close paths* to close open paths whose ends fall within the snap distance
  (xref:preferences.adoc[Preferences]).
. Click btn:[OK].

To mirror the selection:

. Select the Mirror tool from its pop-up.
. Drag to set the halfway point between the object and its reflection, or the centre of a
  multiple reflection.  Keylines preview every copy.  While dragging:
* kbd:[Option] rotates the axes around the pointer.
* kbd:[Option+Shift] constrains the rotation to 45° steps.
* kbd:[Up Arrow] / kbd:[Down Arrow] switch between Reflect and Rotate for Multiple.
* kbd:[Right Arrow] / kbd:[Left Arrow] add or remove an axis for Multiple.
. Release to apply.  The copies are ordinary objects.

== 3D rotation

The 3D Rotation tool tilts a flat object in space and projects it back onto the page, with
perspective.  For a solid with sides use xref:extrude.adoc[Extruding objects].

To set 3D Rotation options:

. Double-click the 3D Rotation tool.
. Choose *Easy* or *Expert* control.
. Choose *Rotate from*: *Mouse click*, *Center of selection*, *Center of gravity* (the visual
  centre) or *Origin* (the lower-left corner).
. Set *Distance*, by field or slider: smaller distances exaggerate the perspective, larger
  ones flatten it.
. With Expert, choose the *Project from* point -- the vanishing point: *Mouse click*, *Center
  of selection*, *Center of gravity*, *Origin*, or *X/Y* coordinates you type (defaulting to
  the last pointer position).
. Click btn:[OK].

To rotate the selection in 3D:

. Select the 3D Rotation tool from its pop-up.
. Press on the object and drag.  The direction of the drag tilts the object; the outline
  previews.  Hold kbd:[Shift] to constrain the rotation to 45° steps.
. Release to apply.

== Envelopes

An envelope is an invisible wrapper drawn around one or more objects: reshape the wrapper and
the contents warp to fill it.  Envelopes are the exception on this page -- they are *live* while
the envelope exists, and become permanent only when you release them.  Text inside an envelope
stays editable text.

// TODO screenshot: a text block in a rectangular envelope, then with the envelope's top edge curved

To apply an envelope:

. Select the objects.
. Choose menu:Modify[Envelope > Create], or click btn:[Create] on the Envelope toolbar
  (menu:Window[Toolbars > Envelope]).  A rectangular envelope appears around the selection,
  with the preset chosen in the toolbar's pop-up.
. Edit the envelope as you would any path -- drag its points and handles, add points, use the
  Freeform tool -- and watch the contents follow (xref:editing-paths.adoc[Editing paths]).

To use another path as an envelope:

. Select the path and choose menu:Edit[Copy].
. Select the object to warp and choose menu:Modify[Envelope > Paste as Envelope] (or the
  toolbar button).  The path's shape becomes the envelope.

To see how the envelope maps its contents:

* Choose menu:Modify[Envelope > Show Map].  A mesh shows the distortion grid.  Choose it again
  to hide it.

To get the envelope's shape as a path:

. Select the enveloped object and choose menu:Modify[Envelope > Copy as Path].
. Choose menu:Edit[Paste].

To remove an envelope:

* menu:Modify[Envelope > Release] keeps the warped shape and turns the contents into ordinary
  paths (text becomes paths).
* menu:Modify[Envelope > Remove] discards the warp and restores the contents as they were.

To save an envelope as a preset:

. Select the enveloped object and choose menu:Modify[Envelope > Save as Preset].
. Type a name and click btn:[OK].  The preset appears in the Envelope toolbar's pop-up.  Presets
  are stored with your account preferences, so they follow you between Macs.

To delete a preset, choose it in the toolbar's pop-up and choose menu:Modify[Envelope > Delete
Preset].

== Working with others

* Every tool on this page writes ordinary path edits, so collaborating on them works as it
  does for any path: if you roughen a path while someone else moves it, both changes apply.
  If you roughen it while someone else drags one of its points, their point lands where they
  put it and your roughening applies to the rest -- a merge review after a long offline
  stretch shows both.
* Operations that create objects (Shadow, Smudge, Emboss, Mirror, Release of an envelope)
  create them on your side; a collaborator sees them appear next to the original.  If they
  deleted the original at the same moment, the copies remain and the original is offered
  back in the merge review.
* Envelopes are live and shared: while you reshape an envelope, a collaborator can edit the text
  inside it, and everyone sees both.  Two people dragging the same envelope point end with the
  later position, listed in the merge review.
* Tool options (a Roughen amount, a Shadow type) are yours, not the document's.  Envelope
  presets are yours too.

== Specification

=== Data model

The destructive tools and operations add no schema: they emit the DRAW epic's path operations
(`SetFields` on contours, `ElementInsert`/`ElementDelete` of points, `CreateNode` for copies,
`MoveNode` into a new `group`).

Envelopes are a node kind `envelope`: a group-like wrapper whose live children are the contents
and whose props hold the envelope shape as an editable path.

[source,protobuf]
----
// proto/wiretuner/doc/v1/envelope.proto (sketch); EnvelopeProps is a NodeProps.kind case
message EnvelopeProps {
  CommonProps common = 1;
  // The envelope outline: one closed contour, edited with the same path tools and merging
  // point by point exactly as PathProps.contours does (DRAW epic's Contour, SEQUENCE).
  repeated Contour contours = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_SEQUENCE];
  // The contents' bounds at creation, in the envelope's coordinates: the source rectangle
  // the warp maps from.  ATOMIC: written once by Create/Paste as Envelope.
  Rect source_bounds = 3 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  // Which four points of the contour are the corners of the warp patch (element ids, in
  // order TL, TR, BR, BL).  Points between corners shape that edge.  ATOMIC as a set of four.
  EnvelopeCorners corners = 4 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  bool show_map = 5 [(wt.crdt.field).local_only = true];   // view state
}
message EnvelopeCorners { ElementId tl = 1; ElementId tr = 2; ElementId br = 3; ElementId bl = 4; }

// Envelope presets are not document data: a `repeated EnvelopePreset` in the account
// Preferences message (PROTO-009), each {string name; Contour contour; EnvelopeCorners corners}.
----

Envelope shape edits reuse the path command set, so the whole of xref:editing-paths.adoc[Editing
paths] applies to the envelope contour.

=== Merge semantics

Destructive tools:: Each application is one change of ordinary path ops.  Concurrent edits to
the same path merge per point: a Roughen (which inserts points and moves existing ones) and a
concurrent point drag keep the dragged point at its dragged position and the roughened
positions elsewhere; a Roughen and a concurrent Add Points both insert, so the path ends up
with both sets of new points -- odd-looking but well-defined, and reviewed as *both edited* on
that node.  Object-creating tools (Shadow, Smudge, Emboss, Mirror) create nodes and a group;
if the original is concurrently deleted, the group contains a deleted member and the copies
remain, reviewed as edit-vs-delete with *Restore*.

Smudge/Shadow groups:: Grouping is `CreateNode(group)` + `MoveNode(original)`; a concurrent
move of the original elsewhere follows Kleppmann ordering, and the group may end up holding
only the copies.  No normalization needed.

Envelope:: Contour points merge as path points; `source_bounds` and `corners` are ATOMIC
registers written on create/paste.  Concurrent envelope-point drag and content edit: both
apply, contents re-warp on read.  Release vs. content edit: as blend release -- the baked
group is computed from the releasing replica's state, the edit lands on the deleted child,
reviewed as edit-vs-delete with *Restore* (re-deletes the baked group, un-deletes the
envelope).  Remove vs. edit: contents are moved out; the edit lands on the freed child.

Read-time normalizations::
* An envelope whose `corners` name deleted points: the corner falls back to the nearest live
  point by contour order; with fewer than four live points the envelope renders its contents
  unwarped and the Object panel says "Envelope needs four points".
* More than one contour in `contours`: the first live one is the envelope; others are drawn
  as its outline only when *Show Map* is on.
* An envelope with no live children renders as an empty outline.

=== Offline behavior

Nothing beyond the general rules.  Envelope presets need the account preferences sync to
follow the user to another Mac; on the same Mac they are available offline.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes, and storing envelope presets with account
preferences (PROTO-009's `Preferences` message).

=== Client

Package:: `WTGeometry/Distort/` (`RoughenKernel`, `FisheyeKernel`, `BendKernel` shared with
the Bend effect, `FractalizeKernel`, `Rotate3DKernel`, `EnvelopeWarp`), `WTApp/Tools/`
(one `Tool` per tool), `WTApp/Extensions/` (Emboss dialog, Add Points, Fractalize),
`WTApp/Toolbars/EnvelopeToolbar`.

Kernels::
* *Add Points*: split every segment at `t = 0.5` (GEO-001).
* *Roughen*: sample the outline every `1/amount` inch, displace by
  `random(−1, 1) × dragDistance × 0.1`, rebuild corner or curve points.  Unseeded: this is a
  one-time operation and the result is written, so replicas never recompute it.
* *Fisheye*: for each point inside the lens circle of radius `r` at distance `d`, map
  `d' = r × f(d/r, perspective)` with `f` the barrel/pincushion curve; handles mapped with
  their anchors.
* *Bend tool*: the Bend effect kernel with `size = ±dragDistance × amount/10`, sign from the
  drag direction (up negative for spike: points outward, segments inward -- implemented as
  anchor displacement outward with handle displacement inward).
* *Smudge*: `n = dragDistance / 2 pt` copies (min 1, max 1,000 objects including nested),
  each translated by `i/n` of the drag and colored by linear interpolation to the smudge
  colors; kbd:[Option]: scaled about the centre instead.
* *Shadow*: one copy (Hard), or `soft_edge/4 + 1` concentric insets (Soft, GEO-003 offsets)
  fading to *Fade to*, or a scaled-then-blended series (Zoom, reusing the blend interpolator
  from xref:blends.adoc[Working with blends]); grouped behind the original.
* *Emboss*: GEO-003 offset of the outline by `depth` along `angle` split into lit and shaded
  facets (`GEO-002` boolean difference), five style recipes, soft edge as `depth/2` stepped
  facets.
* *Fractalize*: replace each segment by four segments forming a Koch-style spike on the
  outward side.
* *Mirror*: affine reflections/rotations (GEO-005), keyline preview in the overlay; close paths
  by joining ends within the snap distance.
* *3D Rotation*: rotate the outline about the chosen origin in 3D by the drag vector
  (trackball), project with a perspective of focal length `distance` from the projection
  point; Easy mode fixes projection at the rotation origin.
* *Envelope*: a bicubic Coons patch from the four edge curves of the envelope contour (each
  edge is the contour section between two corners, refit as one cubic if it has intermediate
  points), mapping `source_bounds` to the patch; contents are warped by mapping every anchor
  and handle (curves are subdivided until the warped error is under 0.1 pt).  Text is laid
  out flat then warped glyph by glyph as outlines while remaining editable.

Tools:: Each is a `Tool` (APP-003) with a preview in the overlay layer during the drag and a
single change on release.  Options dialogs are sheets opened by double-click on the tool
(APP-003's tool-options hook); values persist in `UserDefaults`.

Undo:: One step per application ("Roughen", "Fisheye lens", "Bend", "Smudge", "Add shadow",
"Emboss", "Fractalize", "Mirror", "3D rotate", "Create envelope", "Release envelope"…).

NOTE: As built by FX-038 (`WTRender/Effects/Warp/EnvelopeWarp.swift`, `CurveWarp.swift`), in
WTRender rather than `WTGeometry/Distort/`.  The envelope is a `GroupItem` whose `live` is an
`EnvelopeSpec` in pasteboard space; `corners` are anchor indices of the contour, and one that is
missing or invalid falls back to the anchor nearest that corner of the envelope's bounds (WTModel
resolves the element ids, so the contour-order fallback is its).  An edge with points between its
corners is refit as one cubic by least squares with the chain's end tangents.  Contents map as
plain paths: effects resolved to their vector geometry (raster stages drop out), text as glyph
outlines filled with the text colour, transforms baked in.  `EnvelopeWarp.inverse` is the caret
map; Show Map draws 8 × 8 iso-curves as hairlines.
