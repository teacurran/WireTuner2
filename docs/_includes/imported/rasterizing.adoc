Rasterizing turns vector objects into a bitmap image inside the document.  You might do it to
flatten a complex effect that prints slowly, to make a pixel version of a logo for an image
editor, to freeze the look of something so nobody can nudge it, or simply because a screen
graphic only needs pixels.  The result is an ordinary image object that behaves like an
imported one (xref:bitmaps.adoc[Working with bitmap images]).

== Rasterizing selected objects

To rasterize:

. Select one or more objects.  Anything can be rasterized: paths, text, groups, blends,
  images, objects with live effects.
. Choose menu:Modify[Rasterize…].
. Set the options and click btn:[Rasterize].

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Effect

|*Resolution*
|*72 ppi* for screen and web work, *144 ppi* for proofs, *300 ppi* for print, or *Custom* with
any value from 36 to 2400.  This is the resolution at the objects' current size on the page:
a 2-inch-wide selection at 300 ppi becomes a 600-pixel-wide image.

|*Anti-aliasing*
|*None*, *Low*, *Medium* (default) or *High*.  Smooths edges and thin lines by rendering at
1, 2, 3 or 4 times the resolution and averaging.  *None* gives hard pixel edges, which is what
a 1-bit or pixel-art result wants.

|*Background*
|*Transparent* (default): the image has an alpha channel and shows the page through where
nothing was drawn.  *White*: the image is opaque.

|*Color mode*
|*RGB* (default), *CMYK* or *Grayscale*.  Grayscale produces an image that can be tinted and
made transparent with the Object panel.

|*Keep originals*
|Leaves the selected objects in place and puts the image directly above them.  Off by default,
in which case the objects are replaced by the image at exactly their position.
|===

The image is placed at the selection's bounding box, including the extra space live effects
and strokes take, with the resolution you chose as its stored resolution, so its natural size
is the selection's size.  Its name in the Object panel is *Rasterized* followed by the name of
the first selected object.

What the result keeps::
Everything you see: fills, strokes, gradients, transparency, live effects, text rendered with
its fonts, images with their transforms and crops.  What it loses is everything you cannot see:
paths, text as text, effects as adjustable settings.  There is no way back except Undo, so
rasterize a copy if you might want the objects again (turn on *Keep originals*, or
xref:copying.adoc[copy] first).

Very large results::
The result's pixel count is shown in the sheet as you change options.  A result over the
*Downsample images larger than* preference is not downsampled -- you asked for that resolution
-- but a warning shows the size, and a result over 200 MiB cannot be created; lower the
resolution or rasterize in pieces.

== Working with others

Rasterizing replaces objects with an image.  If a collaborator is editing one of those objects
at the same moment, their edit lands on the now-removed object; the merge review lists it as an
edit of something deleted and offers to restore the object, which then sits beside the
rasterized image.  Collaborators see the new image as a placeholder with its name until its
pixels have uploaded from your Mac, exactly as for an imported image, and can move and crop it
meanwhile.

Rasterizing works offline.  The image is created immediately; its pixels upload when you
reconnect.

ifdef::spec[]
== Specification

=== Data model

Nothing new: the result is an `image` node (`ImageProps` on
xref:importing.adoc[Importing artwork]) with `source_name = "Rasterized <name>"`,
`dpi_x = dpi_y = resolution`, `pixels.has_alpha` per *Background*, and
`CommonProps.transform` a translation to the selection's bounds.  One change: `CreateNode` for
the image, then `SetDeleted` for each original unless *Keep originals*.

=== Merge semantics

* Rasterize vs. concurrent edit of an original: the edit applies to the deleted node; review
  lists *edit vs delete*, *Restore* brings the original back beside the image.
* Rasterize vs. concurrent delete of an original: both delete; nothing to show.
* Two people rasterizing the same selection at once: two images, both originals deleted; the
  review lists nothing (no register collided) but the large-merge rule may show the summary.
  Acceptable; the second image is one Undo away.
* Read-time: none beyond the image rules.

=== Offline behavior

Fully offline; the result blob queues for upload like an import.  If the change is later
coalesced away (image created and deleted offline), the pending upload is dropped.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes and blobs.

=== Client

* `WTRender` draws the selection into a `CGBitmapContext` sized from the union of the
  selection's rendered bounds (including effect expansion from REND-004's bounds) at
  `resolution × supersample`, with only the selected nodes visible, then box-downsamples by
  the supersample factor.  Colors are rendered in the document's working RGB space; *CMYK*
  converts through the document's CMYK profile (CMS epic) and encodes as TIFF; *Grayscale*
  converts with Core Image and encodes as 8-bit gray PNG.
* Rendering runs off the main actor with a progress sheet above 16 MP; the resulting blob
  goes through IMG-002's placement path so dimensions, mode and alpha are computed once.
* Undo: one change, labelled "Rasterize _n_ objects"; undo restores the originals and deletes
  the image; the blob stays cached.
