The Trace tool turns pixels into paths.  Point it at a scanned logo, a hand-drawn sketch or a
photograph and it produces filled or stroked paths that follow the shapes it finds, ready to
edit with every path tool.  It can trace everything inside a marquee, or only an area of
similar color you pick with its wand, and it traces anything on the page -- an imported image,
but also {product} objects and text, which it treats as a picture of themselves.

== Preparing an image to trace

Tracing works best on a clean original.  Scan line art at 300 to 600 pixels per inch; more
resolution produces more points than the drawing needs and slower traces.  Photographs trace
into many overlapping shapes, so decide first whether you want a posterized rendering (a few
colors, the *Loose* overlap) or a precise one (many colors, *Tight*).  If the image has stray
specks, raise *Noise tolerance* rather than cleaning it by hand.

== Setting the Trace tool options

To set the options, double-click the Trace tool in the Tools panel.  {product} remembers the
settings across documents and launches.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Option |Effect

|*Tracer*
|*Classic* (default) traces regions of similar color, as {fh} did; it is exact and
predictable and right for logos, lettering, scans and line art.  *Photo* first divides the
picture into the things in it -- a face, a shirt, the sky, a car -- on your Mac, and traces
each of those separately, so the shapes follow objects rather than bands of color.  See
<<photo-tracer,Tracing photographs>>.

|*Colors*
|How many colors the result may use, 2 to 256.  Every pixel is assigned to the nearest of the
chosen colors before tracing, so 2 gives a black-and-white result and 256 a poster-like one.
With the *Photo* tracer this is the number of colors *per thing found*.

|*Colors / Grays*
|Whether the result's colors are the image's colors or gray levels.

|*RGB / CMYK*
|The color model of the result's fill and stroke colors.  Use CMYK for print work.

|*Resolution*
|*Low*, *Normal* or *High*.  The resolution the image is sampled at before tracing: *High* keeps
every pixel and finds the most detail; *Low* samples at a quarter and is fastest.  *Normal*
samples at half.

|*Trace layers*
|*All*, *Foreground* or *Background*: which layers' objects the tool looks at inside its
selection.  Objects on other layers are ignored, so you can trace a scan on the *Background*
layer without picking up the guides and paths you have already drawn on top.

|*Path conversion*
|How pixels become paths; see below.

|*Trace conformity*
|0 to 10.  How closely the paths follow the pixel edges.  Low values give smoother paths with
fewer points; high values follow every bump.

|*Noise tolerance*
|0 to 20.  Specks smaller than this many pixels across are ignored.  0 keeps everything.

|*Wand color tolerance*
|0 to 255.  How different a neighboring pixel's color may be for the wand to include it in a
selection.  0 selects only identical colors; 255 selects everything connected.
|===

=== Path conversion

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Mode |Result

|*Outline*
|Closed, filled paths around each region of color -- the choice for logos, lettering and
solid artwork.  *Path overlap* sets how regions meet: *None* makes each region a separate
shape that touches its neighbors edge to edge (best for line art and text); *Loose* lets
lighter regions extend under darker ones so nothing shows through at the seams (best for
continuous-tone images); *Tight* overlaps regions by the smallest amount that hides seams
(more precise color, more points).

|*Centerline*
|Open, stroked paths along the middle of each line -- for drawings made of strokes with few
filled areas, such as maps, diagrams and pen sketches.  With *Uniform* on, every path gets a
1-point stroke; off, each path's stroke width follows the width of the line it traced.

|*Centerline / Outline*
|Both: thin features become centerline strokes, thick ones become filled outlines.  *Open
paths below* (2 to 10 pixels) is the width at which a feature switches from a stroke to a
filled shape.

|*Outer edge*
|One closed path around the outside of everything traced, ignoring holes and interior detail.
This is the path to use as a clipping path: trace, then paste the original image inside it
(xref:clipping-paths.adoc[Clipping paths]).
|===

=== Tracing photographs [[photo-tracer]]

The classic tracer sees only color, so a photograph traces into bands that cut across the
things in it: a face becomes a stack of skin-colored puddles, a red car and a red sign become
one shape.  The *Photo* tracer looks at the picture the way you do first.  It divides it into
regions that belong together -- the subject, each distinct thing around it, the background --
and then traces each region on its own with the classic tracer and your other options.  The
result is the same kind of group of ordinary paths, with about as many objects, but the edges
of the paths run along the edges of things.

Use it for photographs and paintings.  For a logo, a scan or lettering it finds one region and
does no better than *Classic*, only slower.  With *Colors* at 2 to 4 and *Path overlap* at
*Loose* it produces a clean poster; with 16 or more and *Tight* a detailed illustration.

The regions are found on your Mac, with nothing sent anywhere, and they are only a step on the
way: nothing about them is kept once the paths exist.  See the note on
xref:bitmaps.adoc[Working with bitmap images] for what {product} deliberately does not do
with pictures.

== Tracing an area

To trace everything inside an area:

. Select the Trace tool in the Tools panel.  The pointer becomes a wand.
. Drag a marquee around what you want to trace.  Hold kbd:[Shift] to constrain the marquee to
  a square; hold kbd:[Option] to drag it from its center.
. Release.  {product} traces and places the result directly above the traced objects, on the
  same layer, as a selected group named *Trace of _name_*.

The original is not changed or removed; delete or hide it once you are happy with the trace.

== Tracing an area of color

The wand selects connected pixels of similar color, like a magic wand in an image editor, and
traces only those.

To trace by color:

. Select the Trace tool.
. Click a color in the image.  Connected pixels within the *Wand color tolerance* are selected
  and shown with a marching outline.
. Adjust the selection:
* kbd:[Shift]-click another area to add it -- it can be a different color, so you can select a
  rose and its stem.
* kbd:[Shift]-click a selected area to remove it.
* Press kbd:[Tab] to deselect everything.
. Click inside a selected area.  The wand options popover appears:
* *Trace selection* traces the selected pixels with the current Trace tool options.
* *Convert selection edge* makes one closed path along the boundary of the selection, with no
  fill and the default stroke -- a quick way to get a clipping path or a mask.
. Click btn:[Trace].

The result is placed directly above the image, selected, as a group named after the image.

TIP: To add the colors of a traced result to the Swatches panel, select it and choose
menu:Extensions[Colors > Name All Colors], or pick them with the Eyedropper tool
(xref:swatches.adoc[Swatches]).

== Working with others

A trace adds new objects and changes nothing else, so it never collides with anyone.  If a
collaborator moves, scales or even deletes the image while you are tracing it, your trace
still appears where the image *was* when you started, since it is drawn from the pixels you
saw.  Tracing an image someone else placed works as soon as its pixels have downloaded to your
Mac; a placeholder cannot be traced, and the tool says so.

Trace tool options are yours alone; collaborators keep their own.  Tracing works entirely on
your Mac, so it works offline, and the result syncs like any drawing.

=== Data model

Nothing new.  A trace writes one change: `CreateNode` for a group and its paths on the traced
object's layer, positioned directly above the topmost traced node, with fills and strokes in
the ATTR epic's messages and colors as literal `ColorRef`s (not swatches, until *Name All
Colors*).  Trace options are a `Preferences` entry (`trace_tool`), not document state; the
*Tracer* choice is one more key in that entry (`tracer: classic | photo`, default `classic`).
The *Photo* tracer's regions are transient: nothing about them is written to the document.

=== Merge semantics

The trace is new nodes only, so there is nothing to merge.  If the source image is concurrently
deleted the trace remains; if the source layer is concurrently deleted the trace's group lands
on a deleted layer and the review sheet lists it as *edit vs delete* on the layer with
*Restore*.  Placement uses a fractional position above the source node as it was at trace
time; a concurrent restack does not matter.

=== Offline behavior

Fully offline.  Tracing requires the source blob to be in the local cache; a placeholder
refuses with "Waiting for this image to download".

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

* Tracing lives in `WTGeometry.Trace` and runs off the main actor with a progress sheet and
  cancel for traces over 1 MP of sampled pixels.
* Input: the selection rect (marquee or wand mask bounds) is rendered through `WTRender` to a
  bitmap at the chosen sampling of the underlying image's pixel density (for a bitmap source:
  its pixels; for vector sources: 300 ppi × the resolution factor) with only the *Trace layers*
  visible.  Coordinates are kept as an affine map from bitmap space back to pasteboard space.
* Quantization: median-cut to *Colors* palette entries (gray levels when *Grays*), then a
  3 × 3 majority filter for *Noise tolerance* 1–5 and a connected-component area threshold for
  larger values.
* Outline: per palette entry, a binary mask → contour extraction with hole polarity (Potrace's
  path decomposition: boundary following with turn policy "minority") → polygon
  approximation → Schneider curve fitting (GEO-004) with the tolerance from *Trace conformity*
  (`tolerance = 2.5 - 0.2 * conformity` pixels).  *Path overlap* *None* emits each mask as
  traced; *Loose* dilates each lighter region by 2 px under darker ones (regions ordered by
  luminance, painter's order); *Tight* dilates by 0.5 px.
* Centerline: Zhang–Suen thinning of the foreground mask → skeleton graph extraction (junction
  and end nodes, edge pixel chains) → per-chain curve fitting → stroke width from the distance
  transform sampled along the chain (median for *Uniform* off; 1 pt for *Uniform* on).
  *Centerline / Outline* classifies each connected component by its maximum distance-transform
  value against *Open paths below* and routes it to one algorithm or the other.
* *Outer edge*: outline of the union mask with holes removed.
* Wand: flood fill in the quantized-free source pixels with Euclidean RGB distance ≤ tolerance,
  8-connected; the mask is displayed as a marching-ants overlay by the canvas, and additive
  selections are unions of masks.  *Convert selection edge* runs the outline stage on the mask
  with no fill.
* Performance budget: a 4 MP source at *Normal* with 16 colors and *Outline* traces in under
  2 s on M1; centerline of a 4 MP line drawing in under 3 s.
* *Photo* tracer (`WTGeometry.Trace.Segmenter`, IMG-029): the sampled bitmap is segmented
  into regions before quantization.  Two sources are combined: Vision's
  `VNGenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest` (IMG-027's kernel) for the subject instances,
  and a small bundled Core ML semantic-segmentation model (a MobileNet-class DeepLab
  variant, under 10 MB, compiled into the app, run through `VNCoreMLRequest`) whose class map
  splits the remainder into regions; regions under *Noise tolerance*² pixels merge into their
  largest neighbor, and region edges are snapped to the nearest strong color edge within 2
  px so they do not float off the picture's own contours.  Each region is then quantized to
  *Colors* entries and traced by the classic pipeline with the same options, in painter's
  order from the largest region to the smallest, with *Path overlap* applied inside a region
  only.  Output is one group per trace, as before, with a sub-group per region named after
  the model's class label ("Person", "Sky", "Vehicle", …; "Region 3" when unlabelled).
  A source with one region (a logo) degrades to the classic path with no visible difference.
  The model is an optional resource: if it is absent from the bundle (a build without it) the
  *Photo* tracer uses foreground instances only, splitting the picture into subject and
  background, and the options sheet says so.  Nothing leaves the Mac.
* Undo: one change per trace, labelled "Trace _name_".
