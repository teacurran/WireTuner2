this page except named views is private to your Mac; named views are part of the document and

== Zooming

To zoom with the Zoom tool::
. Select the Zoom tool (kbd:[Z]), or hold kbd:[Cmd+Space] to use it without leaving your
  current tool.
. Do one of the following:
* Click to zoom in one step, centered on the click.
* Drag a rectangle to zoom so that area fills the window.
* kbd:[Option]-click to zoom out one step, or kbd:[Option]-drag to fit the window's current
  view into the dragged rectangle.
* kbd:[Control]-click to jump to the maximum magnification; kbd:[Control+Option]-click to jump
  to the minimum.
* kbd:[Shift]-drag to define a named view of the dragged area (below).

To zoom with the magnification field::
Click the magnification field in the status bar, type a percentage or a multiplier (`6`,
`400%`, `12x`; up to `256x`), and press kbd:[Return]; or open its pop-up and choose a preset,
a Fit command or a named view.

To zoom with the menu::
Choose menu:View[Magnification] and a level: 25% kbd:[Cmd+Shift+5], 50% kbd:[Cmd+5], 100%
kbd:[Cmd+1], 200% kbd:[Cmd+2], 400% kbd:[Cmd+4], 800% kbd:[Cmd+8].

To zoom with a trackpad or mouse::
Pinch on a trackpad, or hold kbd:[Option] and scroll.  Zooming centers on the pointer.  Pinch
zoom is continuous rather than stepped.  Double-tap with two fingers on a trackpad, or with
one finger on a Magic Mouse, to *smart zoom*: the view zooms to fit the object under the
pointer, or to the page if there is none; double-tap again to go back.

How far you can zoom out::
Zooming out stops once the whole pasteboard -- your pages with the room around them
(xref:workspace.adoc[The workspace]) -- fits the window, so the pages never shrink to a speck
or drift out of sight.  The lowest magnification therefore depends on the size of your pages and
of the window: a Letter page in a laptop-sized window stops at roughly 30%, while
very large pages can still go down to 6%.

To zoom with a context menu::
kbd:[Control]-click an empty area of the pasteboard and choose a level from the *View*
submenu (xref:context-menus.adoc[Context menus]).

== Scrolling

* Scroll with the trackpad or mouse wheel in any direction; kbd:[Shift]-scroll moves
  horizontally on a mouse wheel.
* Hold kbd:[Space] and drag to pan with the Hand tool (kbd:[H]), whatever tool is active.
  While typing in a text block, use the Hand tool from the Tools panel instead.
* The scroll bars appear on hover in the macOS style; the pasteboard's edge is the limit.  The
  pasteboard reaches half your pages' size beyond them (at least an A4 sheet), and further when
  an object lies outside that, so you can scroll to every object but never lose the pages.
* Dragging an object or a guide to the window's edge scrolls the view automatically; turn off
  the guide behavior with *Dragging a guide scrolls the window* in *General* preferences.

== Rotating the canvas

Illustrators turn the paper.  {product} lets you turn the canvas -- pages, artwork, rulers,
grid and guides together -- to any angle, so that a stroke you find awkward becomes a
comfortable one.  Rotation changes only how the document is shown on your screen: nothing in
the document turns, nothing prints differently, and collaborators see no change.

To rotate the canvas::
Do one of the following:

* On a trackpad, place two fingers on it and turn them, as you would rotate a photo.  The
  canvas turns about the point between your fingers.  Hold kbd:[Shift] to snap to 15° steps.
* Choose menu:View[Rotate Canvas > Rotate Clockwise] or menu:View[Rotate Canvas > Rotate
  Counter-clockwise].  Each turns the canvas 15° about the middle of the window.
* On a Wacom tablet, set the Touch Ring to *Rotate* in the tablet's settings; turning the
  ring turns the canvas.  ExpressKeys can be assigned the menu commands.

To straighten the canvas::
Choose menu:View[Rotate Canvas > Reset], or click the *compass* in the status bar.  The
compass is a small needle beside the magnification field that shows which way the top of the
page is pointing and the angle in degrees; it is hidden while the canvas is straight.

While the canvas is turned:

* Rulers, the grid, ruler guides and smart guides turn with the page, so a horizontal guide is
  still parallel to the top of the page.
* kbd:[Shift] constraints, the *Constrain angle* and the arrow keys are measured on the page,
  not the screen: a kbd:[Shift]-constrained drag stays parallel to the page edges, and
  kbd:[Right] nudges toward the page's right edge.
* The Info toolbar and the Object panel report page coordinates, as always.
* Text is drawn turned, and you can still edit it in place.
* Zooming, scrolling, drawing modes and named views work as usual.  A named view does not
  record the rotation; recalling one leaves the canvas at its current angle.

To turn the trackpad rotate gesture off, so that an accidental twist never turns the canvas,
untick *Rotate canvas with trackpad* in menu:WireTuner[Settings… > General].  The menu
commands and the compass still work.

== Drawing modes

The drawing mode changes only what you see, never the document or its printed output.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Mode |Shows

|Preview
|The artwork as it prints: strokes, fills, effects, images, text.  Custom, PostScript and

|Fast Preview
|Preview with blends reduced to ten steps and text at 50 points and below drawn as gray bars

|Only the paths, as hairlines in each layer's highlight color, with no fills; images and imported EPS as crossed boxes.

To choose a drawing mode::
Choose it from the *View mode* pop-up in the status bar, or:

* menu:View[Keyline] (kbd:[Cmd+K]) toggles between the Keyline pair and the Preview pair.  A
  check mark means you are in a Keyline mode.
* menu:View[Fast Mode] (kbd:[Cmd+Shift+K]) toggles between the fast pair and the full pair.  A
  check mark means you are in a fast mode.

=== Redraw preferences

The *Redraw* category (xref:preferences.adoc[Preferences]) holds the settings that affect the
canvas:

* *Preview drag*: the number of objects drawn live while you drag.  Above it, only the
  outlines move, as in Keyline mode.  Default 200.
* *Display text effects*: turn off to draw text without effects on screen for speed.
* *Greek type below*: text smaller than this on screen is drawn as gray bars (default 8 px).
  Selected text always draws as characters.
* *On-screen image resolution*: Full, Half or Quarter resolution for imported bitmaps on
  screen.

Anti-aliasing is always on: every Mac {product} runs on draws vector art smoothly at any zoom,
and the screen matches the PDF and print output.

To preview objects while dragging::
Drag them.  Up to the *Preview drag* count, objects draw fully while they move.  Hold
kbd:[Option] while dragging to force a full preview of the dragged objects regardless of the
count -- but only when *Option-drag copies paths* is off in *Object* preferences, because
kbd:[Option]-drag otherwise makes a copy.

== Multiple views of one document

To open another view::
Choose menu:Window[New Window].  The document opens in a new tab with its own magnification,
scroll position and drawing mode; up to eight views of one document can be open.  Drag the tab
out to put the view in its own window, on another display if you like.

To close views::
Close the tab (kbd:[Cmd+W]).  kbd:[Option]-click a tab's close button to close every view of
that document.

Edits made in any view appear in all of them immediately; they are the same document.

== Named views

A named view records a magnification, a drawing mode and a scroll position under a name.
Named views are part of the document, so a view you name for "Logo detail" is there for
everyone who opens the document.

To create a named view::
. Set the magnification, drawing mode and scroll position you want.
. Choose menu:View[Custom > New…].
. Type a name and click btn:[OK].

To create a named view with the Zoom tool::
. Select the Zoom tool.
. kbd:[Shift]-drag around the area.  The view zooms to it and the New View sheet appears.
. Name the view and click btn:[OK].

To recall a named view::
Choose it from menu:View[Custom], or from the magnification pop-up in the status bar.

To redefine, rename or delete named views::
. Choose menu:View[Custom > Edit…].
. In the Edit Views sheet, select a view.  Click btn:[Redefine] to give it the current
  magnification, mode and scroll position; double-click its name to rename it; click
  btn:[Delete] to remove it.
. Click btn:[OK].

To return to the previous view::
Choose menu:View[Custom > Previous].  It swaps between the two most recently recalled named
views, so it needs at least two.

To preview in the browser::
Choose menu:View[Preview in Browser] or press kbd:[Cmd+Return].  Interactions assigned with

== Working with others

Your zoom, your scroll position, your canvas rotation and your drawing mode are yours; nobody
sees the document move or turn when you scroll or rotate, and you never see it move when they
do.  If you want to look where a
collaborator is looking, click their avatar in the status bar
(xref:presence.adoc[Presence]) -- that is a one-time jump, after which the view is yours
again.

Named views are shared.  When someone creates one, it appears in your menu:View[Custom]
submenu and magnification pop-up within moments.  If two people redefine the same named view
at the same time, the later change is the one that sticks; if one person deletes a view while
another redefines it, the redefinition restores it.  Recalling a view is never shared: it only
moves your window.

After being offline, any named views you created or edited merge with the others'.  Two
people creating a view with the same name while apart produces two views with the same name;
rename one in the Edit Views sheet.

== Specification

=== Data model

Two things: the per-document *view state*, which never leaves this Mac, and *named views*,
which are document nodes.

[source,protobuf]
----
// proto/wiretuner/doc/v1/view.proto

// Local view state.  Held in the local store's `view` table and attached to the document's
// well-known `settings` node as one local_only field so WTModel exposes it through the same
// observation API as everything else.  Never sent; the outbox filter drops any op whose
// field path enters a local_only field.
//
//   message SettingsProps {
//     ...
//     ViewState view = 40 [(wt.crdt.field).local_only = true];  // BASIC block
//   }
message ViewState {
  double magnification = 1;        // 0.06 .. 256.0 (1.0 = 100%); 0 reads 1.0
  // Point and OpId fields are annotated [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC].
  Point scroll_origin = 2;         // pasteboard point at the window's top-left
  DrawingMode mode = 3;            // PREVIEW, FAST_PREVIEW, KEYLINE, FAST_KEYLINE
  OpId current_page = 4;           // page node; unset = first page
  bool snap_to_point = 5;          // Tools panel Snap section
  bool snap_to_object = 6;
  bool snap_to_grid = 7;
  bool snap_to_guides = 8;
  bool page_rulers = 9;            // View > Page Rulers > Show
  bool text_rulers = 10;
  bool grid_visible = 11;
  bool guides_visible = 12;
  OpId previous_view = 13;         // last named view recalled, for Custom > Previous
  OpId previous_view_2 = 14;       // the one before it
  // Canvas rotation in degrees, counter-clockwise, -180 < r <= 180; 0 = straight.  View
  // state like magnification: never document data, never shared, not part of a named view.
  double rotation_degrees = 15;
}

enum DrawingMode {
  DRAWING_MODE_UNSPECIFIED = 0;
  DRAWING_MODE_PREVIEW = 1;
  DRAWING_MODE_FAST_PREVIEW = 2;
  DRAWING_MODE_KEYLINE = 3;
  DRAWING_MODE_FAST_KEYLINE = 4;
}

// A named view.  Node kind `custom_view` = 260 (BASIC block 260-279), child of the
// well-known `settings` node (0:1); sibling order is the order in View > Custom.  Shared: it
// is the one piece of "view" that is a document fact.  The name is CommonProps.name (empty
// reads as "View n"), as for every other named kind.
message CustomViewProps {
  CommonProps common = 1;
  // Magnification, mode and scroll position are one register: a view redefined by two
  // people concurrently must not end up with one person's zoom and the other's scroll.
  ViewTarget target = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
}

message ViewTarget {
  double magnification = 1 [(buf.validate.field).double = {gte: 0.06, lte: 256.0}];
  Point scroll_origin = 2 [(wt.crdt.field).merge = MERGE_ATOMIC];
  DrawingMode mode = 3;
}
----

`Preview in Browser` writes nothing.  `Restore view when opening document`, `Remember
window size and location` and `Rotate canvas with trackpad` are preferences
(xref:preferences.adoc[Preferences]).  `ViewTarget` deliberately has no rotation field: a
named view is a place in the document, and the angle someone else holds their trackpad at is
not.

=== Merge semantics

`ViewState`:: No merge; the field is `local_only`, so no op carrying it ever reaches the
outbox, and two Macs of the same user each hold their own.  `current_page` and
`previous_view*` are `OpId` references read with `REF_FALLBACK_UNSET`: a deleted page falls
back to the nearest page by pasteboard distance at read time (a normalization in `WTModel`),
a deleted named view simply drops out of the Previous pair.

`custom_view` nodes:: `common.name` is an ATOMIC string register (LWW); `target` is one ATOMIC
register; position among siblings is the tree position.  Cases:

* *Redefine vs. redefine*: LWW on `target`; the loser's window does not move (recall is
  local), and the loser's value is retained in the log.  The review sheet lists it only if
  the local write lost and the user was offline (the "same register, local lost" rule),
  under "Views", with *Use mine* re-asserting the target.
* *Rename vs. redefine*: independent registers; both survive.
* *Delete vs. redefine*: the redefinition applies to the deleted node; the review sheet shows
  "edited something that was deleted" with *Restore*.  Online, the deletion wins visibly
  (the view disappears from the menu) -- no restore is offered, matching object deletion.
* *Create vs. create with the same name*: two nodes; the menu lists both; no normalization.
  Uniqueness is not enforced because a name is a label, not a key.
* *Reorder*: `View > Custom > Edit…` allows drag-reordering, which is a `MoveNode` with a
  fractional position; concurrent reorders interleave harmlessly.

Read-time normalizations:: A `custom_view` whose `target.magnification` is outside the range
(possible only from a buggy client) is clamped on read.  A view under any parent other than
`settings` (impossible by construction, kept for totality) is ignored.

=== Offline behavior

Everything works offline.  Named-view ops are ordinary `CreateNode`/`SetFields`/`SetDeleted`
and coalesce like any other (redefining a view ten times offline sends one `SetFields`).
`Preview in Browser` needs no network: the export is local and the browser opens a file URL.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

* `WTApp/View`.  `CanvasViewController` owns the `NSScrollView` with a custom

  as the scroll view's `magnification` for display and to the renderer as the rasterization
  scale; `allowsMagnification = true` provides pinch, with `minMagnification = 0.06`,
  `maxMagnification = 256`.
