{product} is a vector drawing application: artwork is made of paths, text and images described
mathematically, so it scales to any size and prints at any resolution without losing detail.
Everything you do happens in one place -- the document window -- surrounded by panels, toolbars
and the menu bar.  This page walks through that window: the pasteboard, the tab bar that holds
several open documents, the status bar, and the command palette.

== The document window

Each document opens in a document window.  The window has, from top to bottom:

* The *title bar*, showing the document's name and its sync state (xref:saving.adoc[Saving]).
  There is no "unsaved" marker: every change is kept as soon as you make it.  When you are
  offline, the title bar says so and shows how many of your changes are waiting to sync.
* The *Main toolbar* (xref:toolbars.adoc[Toolbars]).
* The *tab bar*, one tab per open document (below).  It is hidden while only one document is
  open, unless you choose menu:View[Show Tab Bar].
* The *canvas*: the pasteboard with your pages on it, with rulers along its edges when they are
  shown (xref:rulers.adoc[Rulers]).
* The *status bar* along the bottom edge (below).

Panels dock at the right edge of the window, and the Tools panel docks at the left edge
(xref:panels.adoc[Using panels]).  Any of them can float instead.  Because panels and toolbars
dock inside the window, a single window holds the whole workspace, and a second display can
hold floating panels if you prefer.

[[pasteboard]]
=== The pasteboard

The pasteboard is the working area around your pages.  It reaches beyond them by half their
width on the left and right and by half their height above and below, and never by less than
an A4 sheet's width across or its height down, so there is always room beside, above and below
the pages to keep artwork, a pasted object or a spare A4 sheet's worth of sketches.  With
several pages the pasteboard surrounds all of them together.  You can never scroll so far that
the pages are lost, and zooming out stops once the whole pasteboard fits the window.

The pasteboard follows the document.  Add, move or enlarge a page and it grows or shrinks
with it.  If an object lies beyond the edge -- one you dragged or pasted past it, or one an
imported or older document put far away -- the pasteboard stretches to include it, with an
A4 sheet's worth of room around it, so no object is ever out of reach and you can bring it to the
middle of the window.  menu:View[Fit All] always brings
every page back into view.

Pages themselves can be placed anywhere in a 222 by 222 inch area, room for up to 520
letter-sized pages.  Objects can sit anywhere; objects on a page print with menu:File[Print],
and objects outside every page print only through an output area
(xref:output-area.adoc[Output area]).  Pages are added, moved and resized with the Document
panel and the Page tool (xref:pages.adoc[Pages]).

=== Tabs: several documents in one window

Every document you open becomes a tab in the current window, so one window can hold a whole
project.

To open a document in a tab::
Open it from the library window, from menu:File[Open Recent], or by dropping it on the Dock
icon.  It appears as a new tab in the frontmost window.

To switch tabs::
Click a tab, or press kbd:[Control+Tab] and kbd:[Control+Shift+Tab] to cycle.  kbd:[Cmd+1]
through kbd:[Cmd+9] are *not* tab switches -- {product} uses them for magnification
(xref:document-view.adoc[Setting the document view]).

To move a tab into its own window::
Drag the tab out of the tab bar, or choose menu:Window[Move Tab to New Window].

To merge windows::
Choose menu:Window[Merge All Windows].

To reorder tabs::
Drag them.

To close a tab::
Click its close button, or press kbd:[Cmd+W].  Closing never asks whether to save; there is
nothing to save.  If you are offline with changes that have not synced, {product} keeps them
and sends them the next time the document opens or the network returns.

Each tab shows a small colored dot per collaborator who has that document open, so you can see
which of your documents are busy without switching to them.

=== The status bar

The status bar runs along the bottom of the canvas.  It cannot be undocked.  From left to
right:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Control |What it does

|btn:[Add Page]
|Adds a page after the current one, using the current page's size (xref:pages.adoc[Pages]).

|Page selector
|btn:[<] and btn:[>] step to the previous and next page; the field between them shows the
current page number.  Click the field to type a page number, or open its pop-up to choose a
page by name.

|Magnification
|The current zoom.  Type a percentage (6% to 25,600%), or a multiplier such as `4x`, and press
kbd:[Return]; or open the pop-up for preset levels, *Fit Selection*, *Fit to Page*, *Fit All*,
and your named views (xref:document-view.adoc[Setting the document view]).

|View mode
|Preview, Fast Preview, Keyline or Fast Keyline (xref:document-view.adoc[Setting the document
view]).

|Units
|The document's unit of measure: points, picas, inches, decimal inches, millimeters, centimeters
or pixels.  Changing it here changes the document for everyone who has it open
(xref:document-panel.adoc[Document panel]).

|Sync indicator
|A cloud glyph: synced, syncing, offline (with the count of waiting changes), or a warning when
a merge needs your review (xref:saving.adoc[Saving]).

|Collaborators
|Avatars of everyone who has the document open right now.  Click one to jump to where that
person is working (xref:presence.adoc[Presence]).
|===

=== The command palette

The command palette is a search field that runs anything in the application.  Press
kbd:[Cmd+/] (or choose menu:Help[Command Palette…]), start typing, and a list narrows to
match: menu commands, tools, panels, the pages and layers of the open document, its named
views, and the documents you opened recently.  Each command shows its keyboard shortcut, so the
palette doubles as a way to learn them.

To run something from the palette::
. Press kbd:[Cmd+/].
. Type part of the name.  Matching is forgiving: `rot cw` finds *Rotate Canvas > Rotate
  Clockwise*, `lay` lists the document's layers, `swat` finds the Swatches panel.
. Press kbd:[Up] and kbd:[Down] to choose, then kbd:[Return] to run it -- or click it.  A
  command runs on the current selection exactly as its menu item would; a panel opens and
  comes to the front; a page or named view scrolls the window there; a layer becomes the
  current layer; a recent document opens in a new tab.
. Press kbd:[Esc] to close without doing anything.

The things you use most rise to the top: the palette remembers what you ran on this Mac and
lists it first for the same search.  Commands that cannot run right now (nothing selected,
wrong kind of object) are shown greyed with the reason, so you know what to select first.

=== Magnification and the canvas

Scrolling, zooming and the Hand tool are covered on xref:document-view.adoc[Setting the
document view].  Two things belong here because they are properties of the window:

* The magnification and scroll position are remembered per document, on this Mac, and restored
  when you reopen the document if *Restore view when opening document* is on in Preferences
  (xref:preferences.adoc[Preferences]).
* A document can have several views open at once, each in its own tab or window
  (menu:Window[New Window]).

== Working with others

The workspace itself -- which panels are open, where the window sits, which tab is in front,
how far you are zoomed in -- is yours alone.  Nobody else's window changes when you rearrange
yours, and yours does not change when they rearrange theirs.

What everyone shares is the document: its pages, its units, its artwork.  If a collaborator
changes the document's units in the status bar, your status bar changes too, and the rulers
follow.  If someone adds a page, it appears on your pasteboard and in your page selector as
soon as the change arrives.

When you have been offline, reopening the document (or the network coming back) brings in the
changes you missed.  Your zoom and scroll position stay where they were; if the page you were
looking at has been deleted, {product} scrolls to the nearest remaining page.

== Specification

=== Data model

Nothing on this page is shared document data except the units field, which belongs to the
`settings` node and is specified on xref:document-panel.adoc[Document panel].  Window and tab
state is application state kept outside the document entirely; per-document view state is
held in the local store's `view` table (xref:offline.adoc[Offline]) and specified on
xref:document-view.adoc[Setting the document view].

[source,protobuf]
----
// Local store only (GRDB `view` table); never a wire message.  Sketched here so the
// fields have one home.  Every field is local_only by construction.
message WindowState {
  // Restored when the app relaunches with this document open.
  string document_id = 1;        // UUIDv7 of the document
  Rect frame = 2;                // window frame in screen points
  string tab_group = 3;          // NSWindow tabbingIdentifier the tab belonged to
  uint32 tab_index = 4;          // order within the tab group
  bool key = 5;                  // was the key window at quit
}
----

=== Merge semantics

None.  No field on this page is a document register.  The units pop-up writes the
`settings.units` register (ATOMIC, LWW) exactly as the Document panel does; concurrent unit
changes by two users end with the later one, and the loser sees the status bar flip -- no
review entry, because units are a display preference of the document, not artwork.

=== Offline behavior

Window and tab state is always available.  Opening a document that has no local store while
offline is refused in the library window with an explanation (xref:offline.adoc[Offline]).

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.  Collaborator avatars come from presence
(xref:sync-protocol.adoc[Sync protocol]).

=== Client

* `WTApp`.  `DocumentWindowController` owns one `NSWindow` with `tabbingMode = .preferred`
  and a `tabbingIdentifier` per app instance so any document can join any window.  Window
  restoration uses `NSWindowRestoration` with the `WindowState` above as the restorable state,
  keyed by document id, so an unavailable document (trashed, no local store) simply does not
  reopen.
* The status bar is an `NSView` strip below the canvas scroll view (not an `NSToolbar`, which
  cannot sit at the bottom), height 24 pt, hosting AppKit controls: `NSButton` (add page),
  `NSStepper`-style page buttons with an `NSComboBox` for the page number, an `NSComboBox`
  for magnification (editable, accepts `%` and `x` suffixes), `NSPopUpButton` for view mode
  and units, and SwiftUI-hosted sync indicator and avatar strip.
* Tab decorations (collaborator dots) come from `NSWindowTab.accessoryView`, driven by the
  presence model of each document.
* Magnification field parsing: `"400"`, `"400%"`, `"4x"` all mean 400%; values are clamped to
  6%--25,600% and rounded to two decimals; invalid input restores the previous value with an
  `NSBeep`.
* The command palette is a window-level `NSPanel` owned by `DocumentWindowController` (one
  per window, shown over the key window); its search, ranking and sources are specified
  on xref:customizing.adoc[Customizing your environment] (BASIC-033).  Running a command
  through it is identical to choosing the menu item: same registry entry, same validation,
  same undo label.
* Undo grouping: nothing here is undoable except the units change, which is one change
  labelled "Change Units".

NOTE: Deviations recorded by BASIC-001, BASIC-002 and BASIC-003.  *Tabs*: native window
tabbing; menu:View[Show Tab Bar], menu:Window[Show Next Tab] (kbd:[Control+Tab]), Show Previous
Tab (kbd:[Control+Shift+Tab]), Move Tab to New Window and Merge All Windows are responder-chain
commands AppKit performs (`WindowTabCommands`).  `WindowState` restoration is not
`NSWindowRestoration`: `DocumentController.sessionState()` is written to
`WireTuner/Session.json` at quit and reopened at launch (skipped for test launches unless they
pass `-WTRestoreSession`); a document the library cache knows as trashed is skipped.  A window
opened on its own is ordered front with tabbing disallowed for that moment, because a
`.preferred` window otherwise joins the front window.  Tab dots are a `TabPresenceDotsView`
accessory driven by `PresenceProviding` (the stub until SYNC-009).  *Status bar*: the page
selector is an `NSComboBox` listing "Page 1", "Page 2"... until pages have names; stepping to a
page centres it at the current zoom; the current page is kept on the `DocumentHandle` (one window
per document until BASIC-016).  Until `WTModel`, the placeholder document draws every page (a
shadow, the white sheet and its border) as one group at display-list index 0, so adding a page
never renumbers objects; btn:[Add Page] records one change labelled "Add Page" and places the page
after the current page's row (a new row below when the pasteboard is full); `settings.units` is a
last-writer-wins `UnitsRegister` (Lamport counter, then replica id) on the handle, and remote
page deletions and unit changes arrive through `removePage(at:)` and `mergeUnits(_:)`, stand-ins
for `WTSync`, so the merge test is a unit test of that register.  Named views are empty until
BASIC-012.  The sync glyph and avatars are SwiftUI hosts over `StatusBarModel`.  *Title*: the sync
state comes from `SyncStatusProviding` (`StubSyncStatus`, always synced, until SYNC-001); the
separator is an em dash.  The canvas's scroll limits are APP-002's scroller model;
`Pasteboard.clamp` keeps a page on the pasteboard for the DOC epic's page drag.

*The canvas's extent* (D-093).  How far a window scrolls and zooms out is a view property derived
from the document, never stored or synced, so collaborators, offline edits and merges need nothing
for it: each window computes it from the merged state it draws.  `CanvasExtent.extent(pages:artwork:)`
(`WTModel`, platform-neutral) takes the union of every page (`PageList.bounds`; pages lie side by side,
so the extent surrounds them together, not each page) and grows it by `max(width / 2, 210 mm)` on the
left and right and `max(height / 2, 297 mm)` on the top and bottom -- the A4 minimum is per margin, so a
whole portrait A4 sheet fits in every margin band, which the stricter reading "the whole extent is at
least A4" would not give.  The drawn objects' bounds (`CanvasExtent.artworkBounds(of:)`: one pass over the
display list's precomputed item bounds, skipping the page furniture, which carries no node id) grown by
A4's width across and height down are added (enough to centre an object at the edge in a window), so an object already outside -- an old document, an import, a paste -- can always be
scrolled to and pulled back.  The 222 × 222 in square remains the *page area*: where pages may lie
(`Pasteboard.clamp`, `PageEditing.clamped`, `pageRects` wrapping and the miniature in the Document
panel), unchanged, so the model, proto and conformance vectors are as they were.  Objects are never
clamped: a drag or paste past the extent's edge lands where it is dropped and the extent grows to
include it on that change; auto-scroll during the drag stops at the extent as it stood when the drag
began, so the view never runs away into empty space.
