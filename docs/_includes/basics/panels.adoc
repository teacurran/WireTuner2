Panels are the small windows that show and change what is selected: the Object panel for an
object's properties, Swatches for colors, Layers for stacking, and so on.  They live in
*panel groups* -- tabbed sets that dock at an edge of the document window or float over it.
This page explains the framework: opening, closing, grouping, docking, collapsing, and the
Options menu every panel has.  What each panel *contains* is documented on that panel's own
page; the table at the end links them.

Panel groups have the look of a current Mac app: they are translucent glass over the document,
which runs beneath them to the window's edge, so your artwork shows through the panels.  Each
group is a frosted card; the card is frosted more heavily where a panel is dense with controls, so
its text stays readable over any artwork.  Groups are *magnetic*: they click to the window's edges
and to each other, and groups that have clicked together move as one.  Groups docked at an edge
sit flush against it; floating groups are rounded glass panels of the same kind.  The Tools panel
is a panel group like the others.  If you prefer opaque panels, set *Panel transparency* to Solid
(see <<Panel appearance>>); turning on Reduce Transparency in System Settings does the same.

Layers, Help and the Properties, Assets, and Mixer and Tints groups are open at first launch;

== Opening, closing and activating panels

To open a panel::
Choose its name from the menu:Window[] menu.  The panel's group expands and that panel comes
to the front.  If the panel is already open and in front, choosing it again closes a floating
group or collapses a docked one.

To open the Find & Replace Graphics or Select panel::
Find & Replace Graphics is also menu:Edit[Find and Replace > Graphics…]; both are in the Window menu.

To activate a panel in a group::
Click its tab.  The front tab is joined to the card below it like a folder tab, with a colored
bar along its top and its name in bold; the other tabs are dimmer and light up under the pointer.
Tabs show an icon, a name, or both, depending on the *Label panel tabs with* preference
(xref:preferences.adoc[Preferences]).  When a group is too narrow for every tab, the tabs you are
not using show only their icons; if even that does not fit, the tabs that do not fit move into
the menu at the end of the strip -- choose one there to bring it to the front.  A group with a
single panel has no tabs.  With keyboard navigation on, press kbd:[Tab] to reach a tab strip --
the focused tab shows a focus ring -- then the arrow keys to move between tabs; kbd:[Home] and
kbd:[End] go to the first and last tab.

To expand or collapse a group::
Click the group's title, or the disclosure triangle beside it.  A collapsed group keeps its
place in the dock and takes only its title bar's height; the other groups share the room it
gave up.  Choosing a collapsed panel from the
menu:Window[] menu expands it.

To close a floating group::
Click the close button at the top left of its title bar.  Docked groups are not closed
individually; collapse them, or hide every panel with menu:View[Panels].

To show or hide all panels::
Choose menu:View[Panels].  The Tools panel and toolbars are separate: menu:Window[Tools] and
menu:View[Toolbars] (xref:toolbars.adoc[Toolbars]).

To show or hide the whole dock::
Click the thin handle at the dock's inner edge, or drag it to resize the dock.  The document
runs beneath the dock, but Fit to Page, Fit Selection, the rulers and the scroll bars use only the
part the dock leaves uncovered, so nothing you fit or center lands under it.  Floating groups do
not count: they float over the document.  Hiding the dock gives that part the full window; the
panels come back with one click.

To cycle through floating groups::
Press kbd:[Cmd+`] (the standard macOS window cycle) while a floating group is in front.
kbd:[Control+Tab] cycles document tabs instead (xref:workspace.adoc[The workspace]).

== Docking and floating

Panel groups are magnetic.  Groups that have clicked together form a *cluster* and move as one;
a single group on its own is a cluster too.  Every group has a *gripper* -- the three lines at the
left of its title bar -- and you can drag a cluster by any of its groups' grippers or title bars.
While you drag, the cluster moves with the pointer; when one of its edges comes within about 10
points of something it can click to, it jumps into line, a colored line (or, at a window edge, the
area it will take) shows where it will join, and on a trackpad you feel a click.  Release to
settle it there.  Release anywhere else and it floats where you left it.

To dock panels at the window's edge::
Drag a cluster until its side reaches the left or right edge of the document window, and
release.  Docked panels sit flush against the edge -- square corners on that side, rounded ones
toward the document, with a thin line and a soft shadow between them and your artwork -- and
run the height of the window.  They move and resize with the window, and Fit to Page, the rulers
and the scroll bars leave room for them.  Only one cluster docks at each edge; drag another up to
its inner side to put it beside the docked one.

To click panels together::
Drag a cluster beside another -- floating or docked -- so their sides touch: it becomes a new
column of that cluster.  Drag it above or below a floating cluster's column so their edges touch:
its groups join that column.  Drag it over a docked column and a blue insertion line shows where
its groups will land between the groups already there.  Groups in a column share its height;
drag the divider between two of them to change how, or the divider between two columns to change
their widths.

To move panels that have clicked together::
Drag any of their title bars or grippers: the whole cluster moves.  Dragging a docked cluster away
from its edge floats all of it.

To pull one group out of a cluster::
Hold kbd:[Option] as you start to drag its title bar or gripper: only that group moves, and the
rest stay where they are.  You can also choose *Float Group* from its Options menu.

To dock a group onto another group::
Drag a single group by its gripper onto another group's title bar or tab strip.  The strip (or,
for a group without tabs, the title bar) highlights and the dragged panels join that group as tabs.

To float a group::
Drag it away from everything, or choose *Float Group* from its Options menu.  Floating clusters
stay above the document window, keep rounded corners and a shadow, and can be moved to a second
display.  Press kbd:[Esc] while dragging to put everything back where it was.

To resize a floating cluster::
Drag any edge of it.  A docked cluster runs the height of the window; drag the handle at its
inner edge to change the width of its column beside the document.  When a panel's contents are
taller than its group, the panel scrolls; it never covers the group below.

The Tools panel docks at the left edge by default and works like any other group: drag its title
to float it, or click other groups beside or below it.  In a narrow column its title bar shows
just its name and the Options button; click the name to collapse it, drag it to move it.

== Grouping panels

To add a panel to another group::
Choose *Group <panel> With* from the panel's Options menu and pick the target group, or drag
the panel's tab onto the other group's tab strip.  A thin line in the strip shows where the tab
will land.

To split a panel into its own group::
Choose *Group <panel> With > New Panel Group*, or drag its tab out of the strip: onto the dock
between two groups to dock it there, or onto the pasteboard to float it.

To rename a group::
Choose *Rename Panel Group* from the Options menu of any panel in the group, type the name and
press kbd:[Return].  Click anywhere else to cancel.

To reorder tabs within a group::
Drag them.

== The Options menu

The button at the top right of every group opens the *Options menu* of the active panel.  The
top of the menu is specific to the panel (for example *New Swatch* in Swatches, *Move Objects
to Current Layer* in Layers) and is documented on that panel's page.  The bottom is the same
for every panel:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Item |What it does

|Group <panel> With ▸
|Moves this panel into another group, or into a new group.

|Rename Panel Group…
|Renames the group.

|Float Group / Dock Group
|*Float Group* floats the group on its own, out of its cluster; *Dock Group* docks a floating
group at its usual edge.

|Close Group
|Closes a floating group (docked groups offer *Collapse Group*).

|Help for <panel>
|Opens this guide at the panel's page.
|===

== Panel layouts

Your arrangement -- which groups exist, which panels they hold and in what order, which groups
have clicked together and where each cluster docks or floats, their columns and sizes and how
each column's height is divided, what is collapsed -- is saved automatically and restored when
you relaunch.  A layout saved by an earlier version opens with its docked groups as the cluster at
their edge and each floating group floating on its own.  You can also name layouts and switch
between them, and reset to the factory layout.

To reset the panels to their default positions::
Choose menu:Window[Panel Layout > Reset to Default].  It does not touch your other
preferences.

To save and recall layouts::
See xref:customizing.adoc[Customizing your environment].

== Panel appearance

Three preferences in the *Panels* category control how panels look
(xref:preferences.adoc[Preferences]):

* *Label panel tabs with*: Text only, Icon only, or Text and icon.
* *Show tooltips*: whether pausing over a tool or button shows its name and shortcut.
* *Panel transparency*: Translucent (the document shows through the dock and floating groups)
  or Solid.  With Reduce Transparency on in System Settings > Accessibility > Display, panels
  are solid whatever this says.

== Working with others

Panels show the shared document, so what they list changes as collaborators work: a swatch
someone adds appears in your Swatches panel, a layer they rename is renamed in yours, and the
Object panel updates while you look at an object somebody else is editing.  Panel *layout* is
never shared -- where your panels sit, which are open and how they are grouped is yours, on
this Mac.

If a value in a panel changes under you while you are typing into it, your typing is not
interrupted: the field keeps your text until you press kbd:[Return] or leave it, and then your
value is applied.  Fields that show a value someone else changed a moment ago briefly show
that person's color at the edge (xref:presence.adoc[Presence]).

After being offline, panels simply reflect the merged document when it arrives; nothing about
your layout changes.

== Specification

=== Data model

None.  Panel layout is application state, stored as a JSON file per named layout under
`~/Library/Application Support/WireTuner/Layouts/` and, for the current layout, in
`~/Library/Application Support/WireTuner/PanelLayout.json` (APP-004: the same format and the
same file writer as a named layout, so BASIC-030's Save Layout is a copy of that file; an
earlier draft of this page named `NSUserDefaults`).  Inside the sandbox these paths are under
the app container.  It is never in a document and never synced.  (A `Preferences` field to sync
named layouts was considered and rejected: layouts depend on display geometry.)

[source,json]
----
{
  "version": 2,
  "docks": {
    "top": [ { "name": "Text", "panels": ["toolbar.text"], "collapsed": false } ]
  },
  "clusters": [
    { "id": "right", "edge": "right", "columns": [
        { "width": 280, "groups": [ { "name": "Properties", "panels": ["object", "document"], "collapsed": false, "height": 420 } ] } ] },
    { "id": "left", "edge": "left", "columns": [
        { "width": 84, "groups": [ { "name": "Tools", "panels": ["tools"], "collapsed": false } ] } ] },
    { "id": "layers", "columns": [
        { "width": 260, "groups": [ { "name": "Layers", "panels": ["layers"] } ] } ],
      "frame": [1200, 300, 260, 480], "display": "<uuid>" }
  ],
  "dockWidth": { "right": 280, "left": 84, "top": 44, "bottom": 44 }
}
----

`docks` holds only the top and bottom strips.  A cluster with an `edge` is docked there (at most
one per side); one without has a `frame` in screen points.  Version 1 files -- `docks` with
`left` and `right` groups and a `floating` list of groups with frames -- are read and migrated:
the side edges' groups become the cluster docked there, at the edge's `dockWidth`, and each
floating group becomes a floating cluster of its own, named after the group.

=== Merge semantics

None; no document registers are written by the framework.  Each panel's own writes are
specified on its page.  The framework does guarantee one behavior every panel inherits: a
remote change to a register bound to a field that has keyboard focus does not replace the
field's text; the new value is shown as a placeholder hint and applied only if the user
abandons the edit with kbd:[Esc] (APP-007).

=== Offline behavior

Nothing here needs the network.  The Help panel's content is bundled.

=== Server

Nothing beyond storing and relaying changes.

=== Client

* `WTApp/Panels`.  `PanelRegistry` holds `PanelDescriptor { id, title, icon, menuOrder,
  defaultGroup, body: () -> NSView, optionsMenu: () -> NSMenu, helpSlug }`.  Registering a
  descriptor is the only step a feature epic performs; the framework adds the Window menu item,
  the tab, the Options menu tail and the Help link.
* `PanelGroupController` is an `NSViewController` with an `NSTabView`-style tab strip
  (custom, to support drag-reorder and drag-out), a title bar with gripper and collapse
  triangle, and an `NSStackView` host.  Panel bodies are SwiftUI via `NSHostingView` for
  form-like panels, AppKit for list-heavy ones (Layers, Swatches, Library).
* `DockController` owns each edge: an `NSSplitView` (vertical) of group controllers, a
  resizable width, and the hide handle.  Floating groups are `NSPanel`s with
  `.utilityWindow` style, `hidesOnDeactivate = true`, that become child windows of the
  document window so they follow it between Spaces.
* Drag and drop uses `NSDraggingSession` with a private pasteboard type carrying the panel
  id; drop targets are the tab strips and the dock edge; the insertion indicator is a
  2 pt accent-colored line.
* Layout persistence: debounce 500 ms after any layout change; a layout that references a
  panel id no longer registered drops that panel silently; a floating group whose display is
  absent is moved onto the main display.
* Help panel: `WKWebView` over the bundled Help Book (this guide's HTML render), with a
  search index built at packaging time; hover help uses `NSView.accessibilityIdentifier`
  conventions from TEST-002 to map the view under the pointer to a `helpSlug`.

joined on its left keeps its own place (its frame grows leftwards), joined on top grows upwards and below
downwards; losing its leftmost column keeps the others in place.  `normalize()` drops empty columns, clusters and
strips after every operation.  A version 1 file migrates on decode: its `left`/`right` groups become the cluster
docked there (id `left`/`right`, width `dockWidth[edge]`), each floating group a floating cluster (id = the group's);
`version` becomes 2, and a later version is left for `PanelLayoutStore` to refuse.
*Snapping* (`Panels/PanelSnapping.swift`, pure): `PanelSnapping.snap(moving:pointer:groupCount:scene:)` over a
`PanelSnapScene` (the window's canvas area, the other clusters with their column and group frames, the group
headers, the shown strips; screen points).  In order: a lone group whose pointer is over another group's header merges
(`.header` guide); a pointer over a docked column inserts between its groups by their middles (`.line` insertion
guide, the cluster not pulled); a pointer over a shown strip joins its end; otherwise the nearest edge within
`threshold` (10 pt) wins -- a free window side (the cluster is drawn flush, the full height; `.area` guide), another
cluster's side with at least 24 pt of shared height (never outside a docked cluster's window edge; tops within the
threshold line up), or a floating column's top or bottom with at least half the narrower width shared (lined up
with the column).  *Dragging* (`Panels/PanelClusterDrag.swift`): the title bar and the gripper call
`PanelInteraction.beginClusterDrag` (no longer an `NSDraggingSession`; a floating group's title bar no longer uses
`performDrag`).  `PanelClusterDrag` begins by undocking the cluster, or -- Option held on the mouse-down or as the drag
begins, or a strip's group -- by detaching the group (a collapsed one gets at least 160 pt, its top kept), so the
moving thing is always a floating cluster window; each mouse position is snapped, the window placed at the snapped
frame (`FloatingPanelWindow.isTracking` keeps those moves out of the layout), a `PanelSnapPreview` (a borderless
click-through child window: accent line, or a tinted area or header with an accent border) shows the join and
`NSHapticFeedbackManager`'s alignment tick marks entering a new target.  On release the window animates to the
settled frame (0.14 s ease-out; at once under Reduce Motion) and the layout takes `attach` or `setFrame`; kbd:[Esc]
restores the layout from before the drag.  The loop cancels at once if the button is already up (a synthetic
mouse-down), and finishes if it came up unseen after dragging.  The scene is built from the document window
(`PanelHost`: `DocumentWindowController.panelArea` is the ruler host in screen points, `panelDocks` its docks) and
every visible `FloatingPanelWindow`.  *Look* (`Panels/PanelClusterView.swift`): a cluster is one `PanelClusterView`,
docked in the side dock's view (flush: `PanelDockController.inset` is 0; the dock's width is the cluster's) or as a
floating window's content: `PanelClusterChrome` (the glass, clipped; the chrome frost; docked, a layer shadow of radius
8 at 22% along the shape) under columns (`DockColumnView`, one per column, 4 pt top and bottom inset) and
`ClusterColumnDivider`s (5 pt, a hairline; drag or accessibility increment resizes the column on the left).  Docked,
the corner radius is 12 and the glass extends past the window edge by it, so the attached corners are square (the
system glass rounds all corners alike); `PanelFrostView` gained `squareEdge` and `strokesOutline` (a separator-colour
hairline along the rounded sides only, `PanelShape`).  Floating, radius 14 all round, no view shadow (the window's).
Group views no longer carry glass of their own.  A floating cluster whose columns are all collapsed shrinks to its
title bars (`PanelClusterView.collapsedHeight`), its top kept; its window's minimum width keeps the last column 44 pt.
An edge with nothing docked hides its dock and its handle, and the canvas's safe area then stops at the window edge;
a floating cluster never counts.  *Tools panel*: unchanged as a panel (it always was a framework group, docked left at
84 pt); it now sits on the same flush docked cluster as the right-hand panels, and `PanelGroupView` drops the gripper
and disclosure triangle below 140 pt (`isCompact`) so its title shows -- which is what made it look unlike the others.
The Options menu adds *Float Group* for a floating group clicked to others.  Accessibility: a cluster is a group
("Panels, docked at the right edge", "Floating panels", identifier `panel-cluster.<id>`); column dividers are
splitters; the edge column's group dividers keep their `panel-divider.<edge>.<n>` identifiers.  Tests:
`PanelSnappingTests`, `PanelClusterLayoutTests`, `PanelClusterDragTests`, `MagneticPanelWindowTests`,
`PanelClusterRenderingTests`.  Deviations: the window's top and bottom stay toolbar strips (a cluster dropped over a
shown strip joins it as a row of groups; there is no cluster docked at the top or bottom); a cluster docks at an edge
only when that edge is free (a taken edge offers the docked cluster's inner side, and `.dock` onto a taken edge adds
columns on the outer side); groups in a floating cluster are one window, so *Cmd+`* cycles clusters, not groups; the
haptic tick needs a Force Touch trackpad; column widths of a docked cluster other than the one beside the canvas change
only with the column dividers.
