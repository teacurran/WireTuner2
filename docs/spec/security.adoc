== Accounts

Every person has one *personal account*, identified by a Keycloak subject.  Sign-in is OIDC
with PKCE from the Mac (`ASWebAuthenticationSession`); Keycloak owns every credential and
the server never sees one.  The realm declares, in this order of preference:

Passkeys (WebAuthn)::
The preferred credential.  Keycloak's passwordless WebAuthn flow is the first option on the
sign-in page and is offered to every account that does not yet have one after its first
sign-in by any other means.  `ASWebAuthenticationSession` runs the platform authenticator, so
a passkey created on one Mac is in iCloud Keychain on the next.
Sign in with Apple::
Required by App Store guideline 4.8 as soon as any third-party sign-in is offered, and the
natural choice on a Mac.  Keycloak has no built-in Apple provider: it is configured as a
generic OIDC identity provider with `response_mode = form_post`, the `name` and `email`
scopes, and a client secret that is a JWT signed with the developer team's `.p8` key and
valid for at most six months -- the *Apple secret* job (xref:server.adoc[Server]) rotates
it every 30 days through the admin API.  Apple sends the user's name only on the first
authorization; the first-broker-login flow stores it then.  The generic OIDC provider is the
decision (xref:decisions.adoc#D-064[D-064]): no community extension to track, and the secret
rotation is a job we already run.
Email and password::
The fallback, with Keycloak's email verification and brute-force protection.
Workspace SSO::
A SAML or OIDC identity-provider connection per company workspace (below); a workspace may
require it for its members.

Every method ends in the same Keycloak subject, so the server sees one account.  Which method
signed in on a device is a claim in the access token (`wt_auth_method`, a protocol mapper:
`passkey`, `apple`, `password` or `sso:<idp alias>`) that SRV-002 records on the device row
the first time it sees the device; the account page's device list shows it beside each Mac.

Account linking:: A person who signs in by a second method must end up in the same account.
The rules, implemented as Keycloak's first-broker-login flow plus a server-side check:

* An identity whose email the provider asserts as *verified* links automatically to the
  existing account with that email (Apple sets `email_verified`; workspace SSO asserts it;
  email+password verifies by mail before the account is usable).  An unverified email never
  links: the person is asked to sign in the existing way and add the method from the account
  page.
* Apple *private relay* addresses (`@privaterelay.appleid.com`) are treated as verified for
  linking -- they are Apple's, and unique per app, so they only ever match an account created
  through Apple -- but are *never* used for workspace domain matching: a relay address neither
  auto-admits anyone to a workspace nor satisfies a require-SSO rule.  A person who hid their
  email and later joins a workspace links their work identity from the account page, which
  adds the verified work address to the account.
* Passkeys never link anything: a passkey is registered on an account that is already signed
  in.
* Linked identities are rows in `account_identity`; unlinking the last remaining identity is
  refused.  The device list shows the method; the account page lists the identities and lets
  the person add or remove them.

An account owns: a personal space of documents, a list of devices, its linked identities,
preferences, and its team memberships.  Personal documents can be shared with anyone by
invitation or link (xref:sharing.adoc[Sharing]).

== Teams

A *team* is a named group of accounts with a shared document space, shared libraries
(xref:library.adoc[Library]) and shared settings.  Requirements:

* A person can belong to *any number* of teams at once, and switches between Personal and each
  team in the library window without signing out.  A document belongs to exactly one space.
* Team roles: `owner` (billing, delete team, transfer), `admin` (members, workspace settings,
  team libraries), `member` (create and edit team documents per the team's default document
  role), `guest` (only documents explicitly shared with them; cannot see the team list).
* A team may be bound to a *company workspace*: one or more verified email domains and,
  optionally, a Keycloak identity-provider connection (SAML/OIDC SSO).  A workspace-bound team
  can auto-admit anyone who signs in with a verified address on its domain, can require SSO for
  its members, and can restrict sharing of its documents to team members only.  Domain
  verification is by DNS TXT record.  Only addresses asserted verified by their provider
  count, and Apple private relay addresses never count (Accounts, above).
* Leaving or being removed from a team removes access to the team's documents at the next
  change or session open (xref:sync-protocol.adoc[Sync protocol]); local stores remain on the
  device until the user deletes them, but the app will not open them past the role check
  without a fresh token (they can be exported as a package if the team allows it; a
  workspace-bound team may disallow this).
* Deleting a team trashes its documents for 30 days; the owner can transfer documents to
  another space before that.

== Document roles

Per document, independent of team role: `owner`, `editor`, `commenter`, `viewer`.  A document
has exactly one owner, who can do everything on it (share, change roles, transfer, move between
spaces, manage its credentials, delete); an editor has every editing privilege, including
fetching with the document's credentials and every command a script can run, but none of the
owner's administrative ones.  In a team document the team's admins hold the owner's
administrative powers.  `commenter` (may write only under the
`comments (0:12)` collection: start threads, reply, mention, react, edit and delete own comments,
resolve own threads -- xref:comments.adoc[Comments]), `viewer`.  A change from a `commenter`
session is accepted only if every op targets a node under `0:12` and obeys the per-comment
ownership rules on the Comments page; a `viewer` session may not write under `0:12` at all.  Effective role = max(explicit membership, team default for members, share
link role if the link was used).  The server evaluates it on every RPC and at every session
open; a change from a session whose role dropped below `editor` is rejected with
`ROLE_INSUFFICIENT` (xref:api-conventions.adoc[API conventions]).

== Replica binding

A replica id is bound to `(account, device)` on first use.  Changes carrying that replica id
from any other principal are rejected, so a leaked local store cannot impersonate its owner's
history.  Devices can be revoked from the account page; a revoked device's token refresh
fails and its outbox is offered for export as a package.

== Tokens and transport

* Access tokens: 15 minutes; refresh tokens: 30 days rotating, stored in the Keychain.  The
  access token carries `wt_auth_method`; a workspace's require-SSO rule is checked against it
  on every call, not only at sign-in.
* TLS everywhere; the compose stack terminates TLS at the API for parity with production.
* Server-side rate limits per account and per document (SRV-014), `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` with
  `RetryInfo`.

== Data protection

* Documents are private by default; share links are unguessable (128-bit) and can expire.
* Blobs are content-addressed but only served to principals with a role on a document that
  references them.
* Exports and packages are produced on the client; the server never renders artwork.
* Data-source credentials are stored envelope-encrypted in `data_credential`, decrypted only
  inside one outbound request, never returned by any RPC (xref:data-merge.adoc[Data merge]).
* Account deletion: personal documents trashed, team memberships removed, personal data purged
  after 30 days; change-log attribution keeps an opaque author id.
